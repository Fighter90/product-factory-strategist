Quick answer
Resume.io is an online resume and cover letter builder with AI writing help, an ATS scorer and many templates. Its headline price is a trial: €2.95 for seven days of full access on its international site, which then renews automatically at €24.95 billed every four weeks, about €324 a year if you never cancel. The quarterly plan, €49.95 every three months, works out much cheaper at about €200 a year. The free plan allows only one resume and one cover letter and downloads them only as plain TXT files, so a PDF needs a paid plan. Every purchase has a seven-day money-back guarantee, and plans can be cancelled at any time in the app.

✓Best for: Job seekers who want a polished, ATS-friendly resume and matching cover letter quickly and will manage the subscription carefully
✗Skip if: You only need one PDF and don't want to track a cancellation date, or you're comfortable using a free template in Google Docs or Canva

The €2.95 on Resume.io's pricing page isn't the price of a resume. It's the price of seven days. After that, unless you cancel, the trial becomes a subscription billed every four weeks. That detail decides whether Resume.io is good value or an expensive habit.

The builder itself is polished: clean templates, an AI writer, an ATS scorer and matching cover letters. This review focuses on what most comparisons skip, which is what the subscription actually costs and how to keep it under control.

## What the subscription costs over time

Option | What you pay | Over a full year | 
Free plan | €0 | €0, but TXT downloads only | 
Trial, then four-weekly | €2.95 for 7 days, then €24.95 every 4 weeks | About €325, from 13 renewals a year |

The four-weekly cycle is the detail to notice. Billing every four weeks rather than monthly means 13 charges a year, and the trial converts automatically. Paid plans include unlimited resumes and cover letters, all premium templates and colors, and unlimited PDF downloads, and both carry a seven-day money-back guarantee.

## The free plan's catch: plain text only

Resume.io's homepage calls it a free resume builder, and you can build a full resume without paying. What you can't do on the free plan is download it in a format employers expect. Free downloads are TXT only, and the plan allows a single resume and cover letter with limited sharing and analytics. In practice the free plan is a workspace for drafting; the trial is how you get the PDF.

## Cancelling, and the seven-day guarantee

Resume.io lets you cancel, upgrade or downgrade from inside the app at any time. It also promises a no-questions-asked refund within seven days of a purchase. Two habits keep the bill predictable:

- Build and polish your resume on the free plan first, so the trial days are spent downloading and tailoring rather than writing.

- When you start the trial, set a reminder for day six. If you still need the tools, choose the quarterly plan instead of letting the four-weekly one renew.

## Matching the plan to your job search

The right choice depends on how long you expect to be applying:

- One application this week. Draft on the free plan, start the trial when the resume is finished, download the PDF and cover letter, and cancel before day seven if you don't need more.

- Six to eight weeks of applications. The trial plus two four-week renewals costs about €53, while the quarterly plan costs €49.95 and covers twelve weeks, so quarterly is the better buy if you're sure you'll keep going.

- A long or open-ended search. Quarterly, every time; the four-weekly plan costs about 60% more over a year.

The free plan's plain-text export has one real use. Many online application forms ask you to paste your resume into a box, and a TXT file is exactly what they want.

## What the paid plan gives you

The AI resume builder helps write and improve resume content. The ATS scorer checks how a resume is likely to read in applicant tracking software, and the templates come with matching cover letters. Paid plans also unlock full resume sharing and analytics, and job search tools sit alongside. Resume.io's own claims, such as job seekers being 39% more likely to land the job, are marketing figures without published methodology.

## Cheaper routes to the same resume

For a single, simple resume, you may not need a subscription at all. Canva has free resume templates that export to PDF, and Google Docs offers basic ones. A general assistant such as ChatGPT or Claude can tighten bullet points and tailor a resume to a job description for free. Resume.io earns its price when you're applying widely for several weeks. Tailored versions, cover letters and the ATS check then sit in one place, ideally on the quarterly plan.

## Frequently Asked Questions about Resume.io

### Is Resume.io really free?
Only partly. The free plan lets you create one resume and one cover letter, but downloads are limited to plain TXT files, with limited sharing and analytics. A formatted PDF requires a paid plan, starting with the €2.95 seven-day trial.

### What happens after the Resume.io trial?
If you don't cancel within seven days, the trial renews automatically into a subscription billed at €24.95 every four weeks on Resume.io's international site. Billing every four weeks means 13 charges a year rather than 12, so a forgotten subscription costs about €324 a year.

### How do I cancel Resume.io and get a refund?
You can cancel, upgrade or downgrade at any time from your account in the app. Resume.io offers a no-questions-asked seven-day money-back guarantee: ask within seven days of buying a plan and it promises a refund. After that, cancelling stops future charges but doesn't refund past ones.

### Is the quarterly plan cheaper?
Yes, by a wide margin for anyone who needs Resume.io for more than a month. The quarterly plan costs €49.95 every three months, about €200 a year, against roughly €324 a year on the four-weekly plan. For a job search of six to eight weeks, two four-week periods cost about the same as one quarter.

### Does Resume.io use AI?
Yes. It includes an AI resume builder that drafts and improves resume content and an ATS scorer that checks how well a resume is likely to read in applicant tracking systems, along with resume and cover letter examples by job title.
