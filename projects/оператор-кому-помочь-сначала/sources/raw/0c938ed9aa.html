Smartwatch eSIM: sharing your number costs £7.50 a month on Vodafone and £9 on EE, and what to ask O2 and Three

By Selectra UK · 23 October 2026 · 4 min read

A cellular smartwatch needs a monthly add-on to work without your phone. What it does, what it costs over two years and what to check before buying the watch.

A cellular smartwatch can take calls, stream music and call for help with the phone left at home. To do that it needs a small add-on on your mobile plan, and that add-on is not free. Over the life of a watch, it can cost more than many people expect when they pick the cellular model at the till.

What a watch eSIM actually does

It is not a second contract. The watch gets an eSIM, a SIM built into the device, linked to your existing line. It shares your number, and its calls, texts and data come out of the same allowance as your phone. There is no separate bundle and no double charge for what you use.

The watch switches on its own between three connections: Bluetooth to your phone when it is nearby, a known Wi-Fi network, and the mobile network when neither is available. That last one is what you pay for.

For some people it matters a great deal. Emergency calls and fall detection reach help over the mobile network even when the phone is miles away, which is why runners, walkers on remote routes and older relatives living alone are the people this feature was built for.

What the networks charge for it

There are three mobile networks in the UK, EE, Virgin Media O2 and VodafoneThree, and four big consumer brands, since Vodafone and Three still sell separately. Vodafone and EE publish their prices; we could not confirm O2's or Three's current price on their own sites.

Vodafone's page lists no set-up fee and covers the Apple Watch from Series 3 cellular onwards, Galaxy Watch 4G models and the Pixel Watch. EE's price guide of 25 February 2026 sets its Personal Paired Watch Plan at £9 a month, rising by £1.50 every 1 March, and the watch has to sit on the same account as an EE phone plan. O2 and Three sell an equivalent too, so ask for the monthly price before you sign. We found no UK network that includes watch sharing free as standard: it is only folded in when you buy the watch itself on a monthly plan, so budget for a charge whichever brand you are with.

What the add-on costs over the life of the watch

The charge is fixed. It runs every month while the add-on is active, whether the watch spends a single minute on the mobile network or not. A watch is rarely kept for less than two years, and at Vodafone's entry price two years comes to £180. At EE, £9 a month is £216 before the yearly rise.

Four checks before you buy the watch

The add-on is only half the decision. The watch itself, and the plan behind it, have to fit.

  1. Look for Cellular, LTE or 4G on the product page: a GPS-only watch has no modem, and no network can add an eSIM to it later.
  2. Check your network supports that exact model, brand and generation: compatibility lists are short and change slowly.
  3. Check your plan qualifies: at Vodafone you need an Airtime Plan, at EE a phone plan on the same account. On a smaller provider's SIM-only deal, check that watch sharing is offered at all.
  4. Ask for any set-up fee in writing, since one-off charges are rarely displayed next to the monthly price.

Do not confuse this with a travel eSIM. A travel eSIM is a temporary foreign line with a different number; the watch eSIM extends your own UK number. Same technology, different job. If you want to know more about how eSIMs work in general, see our guide to eSIM, or our piece on running two numbers on one phone.

If your network does not offer it

A watch can still use a known Wi-Fi network, at home, at the office or at the gym, to receive calls and notifications without the mobile network. The other route is changing network, which keeps your number:

  • Text PAC to 65075 from your current line to get your porting code.
  • Give the code to the new network, which moves your number across.
  • Add the watch plan at the same time, once you have confirmed it covers your model.

Then ask the honest question: how often do you actually leave the house without your phone? A runner, a swimmer or a parent who wants to stay reachable without a screen gets real value from the add-on. For everyone else, a watch on Bluetooth does nearly everything, and £180 over two years can do more elsewhere. Our guide to switching mobile provider goes through the process.
