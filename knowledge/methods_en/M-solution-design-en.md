# Solution Design: Generation and Validation (EN summary)

**When:** the problem is formulated and prioritized; propose solution options, pick the best, and plan the check so that the verdict "confirmed / refuted" is unambiguous and fixed in advance.

**Core:** A solution hypothesis — an assumption that a specific action (feature, service, change) improves user experience and leads to a business result; we don't know in advance whether it works, so we test. Formula: "If [we make ___], then [users will be able to ___], which leads to [business result]" — with a metric and effect magnitude; example: order-status chatbot → users resolve the issue without seller/support (y% of tickets) → faster answer → information gap closed → purchase conversion +x%. Seven-point checklist of a good hypothesis: explicit link to the user problem; concrete ("support response time from 2 min to 30 sec", not "improve the service"); contains an assumption about UX impact; expected business effect stated as a metric; testable ("confirmed/refuted" measurable); contains success criteria (guideline: +10% clicks, −5% churn); focused (one idea = one hypothesis). Generation: benchmarking/competitive analysis; brainstorm with cross-functional colleagues (sales, marketing, support, development); customer co-design — usually combined; if the problem is concrete and the solution lies on the surface (no Apple Pay for iOS users) — don't spend resource on "originality". Prioritization: ICE = I×C×E; RICE = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort; example for "sellers aren't trusted": money-back guarantee 4×4×5/4 = 20; seller reviews 3×4×3/3 = 12; "reliable seller" badge 2×3×3/2 = 9. Assumption sources for scoring: funnel/retention analytics and share of users with the problem; qualitative and quantitative research; sales/support (ticket counts); competitor and adjacent-market benchmarks; results of past experiments; team expert estimates (tech cost, UX complexity). Confidence tiers: analytics data — high, user research — medium, team guess — low. Solution risks (assess before choosing; plan B for every critical one): Value — users don't need it (pushes about new listings disabled as spam); Usability — can't use it (complex filters crash conversion); Feasibility — hard/expensive to build and maintain (photo AI recognition needs servers and experts); Business viability — won't pay off, harms the model (free delivery raised sales but losses grew); Legal/reputation — paid reviews: libel, trust loss. What we check: value, usability, business effect, impact on counter-metrics. Method per check: value — qualitative interviews with prototype ("would you pay?"), landing/fake door (button without implementation, count clicks), survey/NPS/conjoint; usability — Figma prototype, usability test (task: "find housing near the metro"), A/B with funnel and CES; business effect — A/B test (control vs experiment), cohort analysis (LTV, ARPU, retention of the feature group); counter-metrics — A/B watching adjacent products/metrics (revenue of other promo tools, NPS, SLA).

**How:** (1) take the prioritized problem with baseline and segment; generate 3–7 solution hypotheses by at least two methods (benchmark + brainstorm); (2) write each in the "If… then… leading to…" formula, pass the 7-point checklist, split glued ideas; (3) score RICE/ICE; every score with a source and confidence type; add a risk column (Value/Usability/Feasibility/Business/Legal) and a plan B for critical ones; (4) choose 1–2 hypotheses to test; determine what exactly is unknown (value, usability, effect, counter-metrics) and take the cheapest method that removes exactly that uncertainty — cost ladder: interviews/fake door → prototype/usability → A/B → pilot; (5) before launch fix the validation plan: audience, channel, materials, period, success/failure criteria, counter-metrics with thresholds, action per outcome; (6) run the check; compare fact with criteria; record insights (confirmed / not / unexpected); (7) post-mortem and decision (pivot / persevere / kill): collect facts (target and guardrail metrics vs criterion); check which assumptions were wrong; root cause ("5 whys"); classify risks; decide; update backlog, RICE scores, wiki. Formats: light retro 30 min right after the A/B; deep dive 2–3h; quarterly learnings. Example report: goal +10% conversion to promotion; fact +4%, p = 0.12 (not significant); the Reach assumption failed (8% instead of 20%); cause — the banner is visible only in "My listings", 60% of sellers go straight to chat; decision — pivot: move the banner into chat and rerun the A/B; plan B — stop rollout if adjacent promo LTV falls >5%.

**Pitfalls:** hypothesis without metric and effect magnitude ("improve the experience"); several ideas glued into one hypothesis; RICE scores without sources; Impact "by eye" (+5 pp NPS turned out +1.5); success criteria formulated after the test or "adjusted" mid-run; no counter-metrics — the target metric grew while NPS/SLA/adjacent revenue fell; choosing the most expensive method (development + A/B) for a question a fake door or interview answers; "+4%, not significant" treated as success or "wait one more month" without a pre-set plan; post-mortem as blame search or "numbers fine, moving on" without updating assumptions; launching without approvals (legal, security) and without the analytics event — the effect later unmeasurable.
