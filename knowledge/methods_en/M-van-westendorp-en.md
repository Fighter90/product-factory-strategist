# Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (EN summary)

**When:** you are setting the price of a new product or revising an existing one; the category is familiar and has price anchors; you need the frame of acceptable prices before running expensive price experiments. Works for understandable B2C/B2B products; weak for radical novelties without analogies.

**Core:** 4 questions per respondent — too cheap (quality doubts), cheap/good value (bargain), expensive but would still consider, too expensive (would not buy). Plot 4 cumulative curves and read the intersections: **OPP** (optimal price point = "too cheap" × "too expensive" — minimum purchase resistance), **IPP** (indifference price point = "cheap" × "expensive"), **PMC/PME** (points of marginal cheapness/expensiveness), and **RAI** — the range of acceptable prices between PMC and PME. The meter shows the perceived price frame, not the final price: the decision must still pass unit economics and be confirmed by real conversion tests.

**How:** target-category sample per segment/tier → ask the 4 questions → plot curves and find OPP/IPP/PMC/PME/RAI → overlay the minimum marginally profitable price against the lower RAI bound → validate with a price pilot or A/B → revisit on repositioning.

**Pitfalls:** hypothetical answers shift estimates toward the category's average price; novelties without price anchors produce noise; a non-target sample invalidates all points; OPP/IPP are reference points, not the decision.
