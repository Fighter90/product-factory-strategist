# North Star Playbook: one metric of value and its drivers

## When to apply
The team and its stakeholders are each pushing different metrics (revenue versus loyalty versus reach), and priorities are in conflict — you need one agreed-upon metric of customer value and a tree of its drivers.

## Core idea
- **North Star Metric (NSM)** — the metric in which customer value and the long-term business result converge; it measures delivered value, not activity (inputs). Example patterns: Airbnb — nights booked; Spotify — time with music; Amplitude — "learned weekly active users".
- **6 requirements for an NSM** (per the playbook): it reflects value, it measures a result, it is not a vanity metric, it influences the business, it is simple for the team, it leads to action.
- **The input-metric tree**: the NSM breaks down into 3–5 inputs (space: breadth, frequency, depth, quality); each input breaks down further into sub-metrics agreed between the teams. Teams move the inputs — and the NSM moves as a consequence.
- **Not an NSM**: revenue (a result, not value), registrations, time in the app taken without context.
- **Pairing with counter-metrics**: every input metric has a control one, so that growth does not break quality.

## How to do it
1. State the value in one sentence ("who receives what, and what it changes in their lives").
2. Iterate NSM candidates against the 6 criteria; pick one and fix its definition and its window.
3. Build the input tree: 3–5 inputs, each with an owner and a control metric.
4. Wire the tree into the roadmap: initiatives → inputs → NSM; review the tree quarterly.

## Pitfalls
- NSM = revenue — customer value drops out of the picture.
- 5+ "north stars" — priorities are in conflict again.
- An input tree without owners — a decoration.
