# Scaling and PMF (EN summary)

**When:** "is it time to scale / invest in marketing / enter a new segment or region?" and "does the product have PMF, how strong is it, how to strengthen it?" — the output is a verifiable go / wait / fix decision with thresholds, an observation period and stop conditions.

**Core:** Scaling = growing business volume (clients, operations) without proportional cost growth; strategies: geographic expansion, new segments, platform models/ecosystems. Start conditions, by descending importance: (1) PMF achieved — needs understood, confirmed by metrics and continuous feedback; (2) confirmed demand and positive dynamics for ≥2 quarters, qualitative and quantitative ("two-quarter rule"); (3) unit economics — LTV and CAC computed, the path to profit clear; (4) team and infrastructure readiness — scalable architecture, hiring plan, talent availability. Guideline thresholds from lecture notes (validate on your data; "guideline, not fact"): LTV/CAC ≥ 3; payback ≤ 9–18 months; high stable NPS/CSAT (NPS ≥ 70 for two quarters as a "bell" that PMF is near); ~80% of key vacancies closed; no worsening trend for 2 quarters. Scaling plan across four directions: channels (integrations, partnerships, social), marketing (budget, diversification, personalization), technical (scalable architecture, cloud, automation), people (hiring, culture) — the whole system changes, not "more of the same". Metrics: CAC, LTV, retention rate, ROMI, NPS/CSAT, Contact Rate, paying share — choose 2–3 as stop-signals, monitor the rest. Signs of premature/unsuccessful scaling: no sales/user growth; CAC rises above LTV or unstable; metrics worsen (retention falls, churn grows); organizational breakdown. One yellow signal is treatable; two in a row is red. Exceptions — scaling without PMF: innovation/early market and a "window of opportunity", allowed only with a financial cushion and reinforced control of CAC, NPS, unit economics. Fake PMF: (a) subsidized growth (sales grow only on bonuses/cashback); (b) falling retention/NPS masked by total GMV ("revenue ↑, deals per user ↓"); counter with paying share, Contact Rate, LTV/CAC, cohorts. Traffic light: every metric gets 🟢/🟡/🔴 thresholds, an owner and a pre-agreed action; examples: CAC/LTV worsening for 3 months → rework offer and targeting; D30 retention below the segment threshold → stop paid traffic, strengthen onboarding; NPS −5 pp in a quarter → strengthen support. On red: freeze expansion, root cause within 48h, a recovery plan (metric, owner, deadline), re-check before continuing. PMF signals in six groups: feedback (reviews, NPS); market pull ("the market drags the product", fast sales); satisfaction surveys; usage metrics (retention, DAU/MAU); financial (steady revenue growth, positive unit-economics dynamics); growth indicators (viral effect). Quantitative data must combine with qualitative research for final confirmation; PMF is not forever — markets and expectations shift; monitor constantly. Gate logic: Gate 1 problem fit → Gate 2 solution fit (MLP) → Gate 3 traction fit (PMF achieved, scaling plan ready) → Gate 4 goals achieved (part of the business / pivot / closure); during scaling — quarterly deep dives against traction. Scaling artifacts ("10×10"): scale-hypothesis design doc; PMF evidence table (NPS, stable retention); Gate "go to scale" protocol; 12-month roadmap; channel/marketing/tech/people plans; quarterly OKRs; metric traffic light (CAC, LTV, NPS) with alerts; risk register (probability × impact, owner, SLA); financial balcony (LTV/CAC, BEP, CAPEX, base/pess/opt scenarios); post-mortems and knowledge base. Pre-wave checks: two green quarters; green traffic light; every High×High risk has a mitigator and a "red button"; BEP ≤ threshold and LTV/CAC ≥ 3; roadmap dependencies intact (a tech feature not later than its channel); ~80% of offers signed; load test and Contact Rate prediction passed; legal/compliance checklist green; documents accessible without access requests. Start with the first four artifacts (hypothesis, PMF evidence, Gate protocol, roadmap), each with an owner. Measuring PMF — the Superhuman engine: PMF can be measured and optimized, not just recognized by lagging signs (client queues, growth, press). Leading indicator (Sean Ellis): the share of users who would be "very disappointed" without the product; empirical threshold 40% — below it growth usually doesn't happen (Slack case: 51% of 731 users "very disappointed"). Four-question survey: (1) how would you feel if you could no longer use the product (very / somewhat / not disappointed); (2) which people benefit most, in your view; (3) the main benefit for you; (4) how to improve the product for you. Sample: users who recently experienced the core value (in the source: at least twice in two weeks); ~40 respondents suffice (case: 100–200); each surveyed once; repeat on new cohorts. Engine steps: (1) segment the "very disappointed" by persona/type of work and narrow the market to the segments where PMF is stronger — this often reveals non-obvious segments; build the "ideal target client" (HXC — the most demanding and most helpful client who spreads the product) portrait from question 2, in respondents' language; re-scoring the subset raised the score ~10 pp (22% → 33%). (2) Analyze feedback: study what the "very disappointed" love (question 3: speed, focus, keyboard shortcuts); ignore feedback of the "not disappointed" (distracting features, mismatched cases); split the "somewhat disappointed" by the main source of value — ignore those for whom it doesn't matter, study closely those for whom it does (they're held by a specific barrier: in the case — no mobile app, then calendar, integrations, attachments, unified inbox, search, read receipts). (3) Split the roadmap ~50/50: ~50% strengthening what's loved (speed, shortcuts, automation, design details), ~50% removing barriers for the "somewhat disappointed"; only strengthening → PMF doesn't grow; only barrier removal → competitors outrun. Prioritize cost (cheap/moderate/expensive) × impact (weak/medium/strong; for barriers, impact by request count); start with low-hanging fruit. (4) Repeat on new cohorts and make the "very disappointed" share the key metric (weekly/monthly/quarterly; OKR with a single KR). Case dynamics: 22% (start) → 33% (after segmentation) → 58% in three quarters; NPS grew at the PMF metric's pace. Caveats: past early adopters the score may fall (users more demanding) — rework the roadmap quarterly; with strong network effects value grows with the base; narrowing to a segment that badly needs the product beats "slightly needed by many"; the survey measures reaction to the product and doesn't replace behavioral metrics (retention, economics).

**How:** (1) confirm the stage (MVP/Growth) and the decision's goal: grow now or strengthen the core; (2) assemble the PMF signal complex: "very disappointed" ≥ 40% on a suitable sample (≥ ~40 responses; state period and cohort); stable D30 retention or a proxy for rare purchases (e.g. share of active listings); NPS/CSAT; market pull (organic, inbound feature requests); finances (revenue growing faster than costs, positive unit-economics dynamics) — never decide on one signal; (3) rule out fake PMF: organic share, subsidies/promos, cohort retention, GMV per user; (4) check stability: metrics held ≥ 2 quarters, seasonality accounted; (5) if below threshold — run the Superhuman engine (segments → loved/barriers → 50/50 roadmap → re-measure) instead of scaling; fix the target value and the next measurement date; (6) if PMF confirmed — build the four-direction plan and a 12-month roadmap; test the Gate questions: how will LTV/CAC change at a doubled budget? will infrastructure hold the peak? who will serve the flow? will NPS/retention hold at base-segment levels? — two confident "no"s → postpone; (7) set the traffic light (metrics, thresholds, owner, action on red, "red button" — feature flag / marketing freeze) and schedule re-checks of the conditions.

**Pitfalls:** scaling on GMV/traffic growth without looking at retention and cohorts; "flooding the market with money" without PMF; taking 22–39% "very disappointed" as sufficient PMF and accelerating; not segmenting although a narrow segment already exceeds the threshold; listening to "not disappointed" feedback and spending the roadmap on distracting features; a 100/0 roadmap bias (only strengthening or only barrier-fixing) instead of ~50/50; underestimating CAC, support and infrastructure costs; computing CAC once a month and without segments; applying guidelines (LTV/CAC ≥ 3, NPS ≥ 70) without checking applicability to the industry and model; surveying users who never felt the core value, or the same people repeatedly.
