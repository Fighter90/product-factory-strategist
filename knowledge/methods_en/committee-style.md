# Committee document style (RU and EN)

## When to apply
When writing and editing any document that a decision maker reads: a six-pager, a strategy, a validation plan, a decision record. The goal of the style is for the reader to reach the decision in a single pass, without tripping over language, jargon, and unverifiable claims. Separate rules apply for Russian output (PRO-52) and for the English mirror (PRO-53): EN is not a calque of the Russian but living English written from EN templates.

## Core idea
Three levels of style: **language** (grammar and vocabulary), **presentation** (structure and density), **evidence** (every substantive claim is verifiable).

Language (RU, PRO-52):
- short sentences; one thesis — one sentence; average length 12–18 words;
- active voice and a concrete subject: "the channel brings 12% of paying users" instead of "it was established that…";
- no anglicisms where a native Russian term exists: «удержание» (retention), not «ретеншн»; «тарифная лестница» (pricing ladder), not «прайсинг-леддер»; an English-language term goes in parentheses at first mention if the market is more used to it;
- no jargon or officialese: "we are testing the hypothesis", not "hypothesis validation is being carried out";
- numbers are not duplicated in words: "CAC 2,400 RUB", not "two thousand four hundred rubles (2,400 ₽)".

Language (EN, PRO-53):
- the EN document is written from an EN template, not translated word for word: calques like "make a decision about", "in the frames of", "actual situation" are defects;
- terminology follows accepted EN product writing: retention, payback, cohort, ladder; not "holding" instead of retention;
- verb-led headings and bullets: "Choose the unit", not "The choice of the unit";
- after writing — a separate pass "for calques": a Russian construction under English words = rewrite the sentence.

Presentation:
- answer first, justification after: the first paragraph of a section answers the section's question;
- a table instead of a paragraph wherever there is a comparison (options, competitors, metrics);
- one level of nesting in the text; lists of up to 5–7 items;
- every initiative/risk/metric has an owner and a deadline, where applicable.

Evidence:
- a substantive claim carries a [C###]/[S###] tag or is explicitly marked "assumption";
- forecast and fact are distinguished in wording ("we expect", "we observe");
- numbers come with units, a period, and a comparison base.

## Procedure
1. Write the draft from the section template (the structure already sets the questions — do not invent your own).
2. Pass 1 "structure": each section answers its own question starting from the first paragraph; remove redundant sections.
3. Pass 2 "language" (per the document's language — the RU or EN rules above): sentence length, voice, jargon, calques.
4. Pass 3 "evidence": every number with [C###]/[S###], units, and a period; forecasts are marked.
5. Read the first paragraph of each section aloud: if the idea is not clear by ear — rewrite it.

## Template / checklist
- [ ] the first paragraph of each section answers the section's question;
- [ ] sentences are short, active voice, with a concrete subject;
- [ ] RU: no duplicate anglicisms; EN: no calques from Russian, EN terminology;
- [ ] tables wherever there is a comparison; lists ≤ 7 items;
- [ ] all substantive claims carry a tag or are explicitly "assumption";
- [ ] numbers: units, period, comparison base;
- [ ] forecasts and facts are distinguished lexically.

## Typical mistakes
- "Beautiful" language instead of precise: metaphors and superlatives where a fact is needed.
- Calques in EN: "in order to understand the actual situation" — three calque markers in five words.
- Duplicate anglicisms in RU: «монетизировать монетизацию» ("monetize the monetization"), «чекпоинт по ретеншну» ("a checkpoint on retention").
- Long chains of noun cases and participles: «план по улучшению показателей удержания пользователей продукта» ("a plan for improving the retention indicators of the product's users").
- Numbers without a period and a base: "conversion 4%" — of what, when, relative to what.
- Hidden assumptions: "every second user will come back" — without an "assumption" mark it reads as a fact.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which sentence of the document will the reader understand ambiguously?
- Where will the reader stop and want to check a number — and is there a reference nearby?
- What should be removed so the document becomes a page shorter without losing any decisions?
