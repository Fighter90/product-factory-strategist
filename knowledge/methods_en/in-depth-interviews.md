# In-Depth Interviews: Preparation, Conduct, Ethics (EN summary)

**When:** understanding motives, behavior, barriers and client language; forming or refining hypotheses (problem, segments, solution) before expensive development. Not suitable for shares ("how many are like this") or reliable demand forecasting — those belong to the quantitative stage.

**Core:** An IDI is a structured 1-on-1 conversation guided by a semi-structured guide: 40–60 minutes; 5–15 quality conversations with relevant respondents (informal team rule: ≥10 per audience; fewer acceptable when comparing several services, with an explicit under-recruitment risk). Four interview types: Discovery (behavior picture, decision causes, goals, desired outcome — listen for context, situations, values), Problem (real pain, true motives, how the problem is solved today — listen for concrete cases, workarounds, cost of the problem), Solution/concept test (does the offer solve an already-understood problem — reaction, barriers, comparison with the current solution), JTBD (the "job" the client hires the product for — trigger, context, alternatives, result). Hypotheses are needed even for pure discovery: without goals and hypotheses it's "a heart-to-heart"; hypotheses set what to listen for but must not lead the respondent. Interviews can reveal: causes, decision mechanics, client language, barriers and triggers, contested spots of hypotheses, and generate hypotheses for testing; they cannot prove: prevalence ("most want X"), segment size, willingness to pay as a number, future behavior — people predict their behavior poorly and give socially desirable answers; therefore ask about past behavior and concrete cases, not intentions. The output is a hypothesis with living examples, not a conclusion about the market. Recruiting is the main reliability factor (course estimate: ~70% of research success): profile with mandatory and exclusion criteria; screener ≤5 min — (1) kill-filter, (2) behavioral block (frequency, scenario, spend), (3) motivation/barrier (how the task is solved now), (4) control trap ("choose option 3"), (5) contact + recording consent; ≥2 independent channels (client base, site intercepts, socials/communities, agencies/panels, snowball for rare and B2B); exclusions — marketing professionals, recent participants, team-connected people; incentives fixed upfront. Ethics: informed consent before start (who, purpose, duration, recording, audience, usage, voluntariness, right to skip), explicit recording/storage consent with retention and access, minimized and pseudonymized personal data, confidentiality (no identifying details in quotes, NDA), no deception or sales, incentives paid on time, extra care with vulnerable groups, independence — report "as is". Synthetic respondents: LLMs "playing" respondents generate plausible but fabricated answers from training texts, not real client experience; allowed for guide rehearsal, hypothesis/counterexample generation, barrier-list expansion — always labeled "synthetic — not client data", never as evidence of demand, willingness to pay, segment size or behavior causes; all hypotheses checked on real people; AI transcription/rough clustering of real transcripts allowed with human verification and privacy care.

**How:** Prepare — goal and hypotheses (the decision the results will influence), desk research, internal expert interviews (support, sales — vital when respondents are rare/expensive), profile + recruiting, guide written and polished on the first sessions, consent/recording/notes/data-retention ready. Conduct — show-up discipline (≥80%): reminder a day before + ~2h before (messenger/SMS with link and moderator contact), +20% overbooking, timebox and format announced, incentive paid on session day; pre-flight 24h before the first session: brief final, respondent list n+20% with slots, guide in print + digital with timecodes, dual recording (local + cloud, space checked), stakeholders briefed on observation rules ("no prompting"); session structure: warm-up (introduction, icebreaker, format and purpose, recording terms, "programming" — answer in detail, no right answers; online: sound/video check) → main part (guide blocks, one question at a time, no interruptions, pauses and probes; 40-minute arc: 0–3 icebreakers, 3–10 context and habitual processes, 10–25 deep "whys" — motivation, barriers, 25–35 concept/prototype if any, 35–40 close) → close (warn ~5 min ahead; the "magic question" — what important did we not ask; specific thanks; what happens to the results; incentive terms; permission for re-contact). Question types: open ("how do you solve X?"), neutral, probing ("why did you decide so?"), projective ("imagine the ideal process…"), evaluative (rating + "why that"); stop-list: hypothetical, closed, leading, double-barreled, several questions in one, jargon; never answer for the respondent, never teach, don't fear looking unprofessional. Techniques: pause (5–7s of silence — the respondent adds details), echo (repeat the last 2–3 words with rising intonation), paraphrase ("did I get right that…"), step-by-step reconstruction ("back to that moment: you opened the app… what did you see, do, think?" — bypasses memory distortion), 5 whys (to the root cause), "five-year-old child" (obvious questions — carefully in B2B), verification questions ("you said it matters but didn't do it — why?" — separates claimed from actual). Sensitive topics sideways: price not "how much would you pay" but "tell about the last service you paid for: what, why, what do you think of paid subscriptions"; failures via normalization, projection ("why is it hard for many to finish a course?"), self-disclosure. Difficult respondents: doesn't understand → name what you see ("you paused — what's confusing?"), rephrase in their slang; negativity → name the emotion, show you heard and logged it, switch to positive and return, offer a break or end; talks nonstop → directive style, join and redirect, summarize, remind of time; silent/terse → example of others' opinions, remind any opinion counts, no judging; expensive B2B → respect time, formulate clearly, explain you need exactly their experience. Fixation: recording (with consent) is the primary source; notes capture quotes and observations, not interpretations (interpretations tagged separately); marks "?" (clarify), "!" (bright insight), "//" (own comment); three-column template (fact/quote, observation, comment) with auto-timestamps; process notes within 15–30 min after the interview. Right after: back up the recording immediately (cloud + local; lost recording = lost insight); metadata table (pseudo-anonymous ID, segment, slot, key insight in one line); hot summary in 15 min (3 main observations, the surprise, guide changes); transcription within 24h into analysis; "field stage closed" checklist — recordings and notes uploaded, interview count ≥ brief minimum, incentives paid, consents stored, first-impression digest sent to the team. Guide skeleton (45–60 min): 0 intro 3–5 min (purpose, format, recording, consent, "no right answers") → 1 warm-up 5 min → 2 context and the last case 10–15 min ("remember the last time when…" — step-by-step reconstruction, trigger, alternatives) → 3 choice and barriers 10–15 min (why this solution, what got in the way, what was disappointing, what would be ideal) → 4 hypotheses/concept 5–10 min (after the spontaneous part, so as not to lead) → 5 close 3–5 min ("what didn't I ask?", thanks, next step, incentive, re-contact). The guide is good when: it has logic, all questions open, every question serves the goal, key topics have backup probes, and it allows deviating for a valuable insight.

**Pitfalls:** no goals/hypotheses; a guide of "general topics" or closed questions; leading questions ("it's convenient, right?"), questions in a row, hinting answers; the interview as sales or demo; defending your idea from criticism; asking about the future ("would you buy?") instead of past behavior; taking claims for reality; recruiting "own people" (colleagues, loyal clients, professional respondents); fear of looking unprofessional — skipping "obvious" questions; recording only hypothesis-confirming material or "by ear" without quotes; missing consent and storage terms, personal-data leaks; on-the-fly interpretation and labels before analysis; conclusions about prevalence from a small series.
