# Research Brief and Hypothesis Formulation (EN summary)

**When:** the initial request is vague ("we need to research…") or research is already running without agreement on why and what happens to the result. The card turns a request into a brief (a contract of meaning between product and researcher) and into testable hypotheses with PASS/FAIL criteria.

**Core:** Business goal and research goal are different things: different questions ("how to earn more / cut losses" vs "how does this work, why does it happen"), metrics (revenue, conversion, acquisition cost, NPS vs satisfaction, task time, error share, causes of behavior), horizons (quarter–year vs two weeks to months), outputs (sales growth vs insights and recommendations), risks if forgotten (blind decisions — features nobody uses — vs research for research's sake). Chain example: business goal "raise purchase conversion by 10%" → research goal "find out why users don't reach payment" → action (simplified checkout) → metric check. Hypothesis = a testable assumption about user behavior/motives/problems (research hypothesis) or about a change's effect on value and metric (product hypothesis); it contains a segment, an observed problem/opportunity and an expected measurable effect — that's what separates it from an idea. Types: problem hypothesis (understanding, "why do they behave so"; Discovery) vs solution hypothesis (outcome, "will the change work"; Delivery and A/B). Bad: "people don't like our pickup points" (generic, no segment or cause, seeks confirmation). Good: "new users choosing a pickup point for the first time don't reorder because of a negative experience: hard to find the point and expectations didn't match reality" (specific, testable, splits into sub-hypotheses, shows next steps). Sources of hypotheses: analytics (dashboards, funnels, dips), any research, support-ticket spikes, reviews and socials, plus benchmark and dogfooding (dogfooding never replaces validation on a real segment). Evidence: a hypothesis stays a hypothesis until checked by a method fit for the question type; interviews give a plausible explanation but not scale; desk data gives direction; only measurement with a threshold set in advance allows PASS/FAIL.

**How:** (1) hold a brief session with the product (feel like the CEO: what does this research give — benefit or resource waste?); (2) fill the 10-field brief — context (what we know incl. past research, why now, priority), business goal in the task (metrics affected), research goals and questions, next step (which decisions depend on the result; what we do on confirm and on refute), hypotheses and questions, method and why (topics/flows), audience (who, which segments, how to find, whom to exclude), timing and resources (deadlines), image of the result (format the recipient needs), related research; no answer = don't launch fieldwork, gather data; (3) separate goals: business goal with metric and baseline, research goal, next step; (4) collect hypotheses in ~60 minutes: open 4–5 dashboards and note anomalies, read top tickets and fresh reviews, scan the last UX report, quick benchmark; (5) formulate each hypothesis as: "If [segment] faces [X / does Y], then because of [cause] [measurable effect: metric, baseline, magnitude] within [timeframe/scenario]"; (6) filter and prioritize with ICE — Impact (main metric), Confidence (enough data to believe), Ease (cost and time), 1–10 each; record what the scores are based on; (7) choose method and audience per hypothesis; (8) write a Discovery plan for the main hypothesis: target metric (baseline → target change), audience IN/OUT, method and volume, data sources, PASS/FAIL criterion as a number before start, owners and dates; (9) ladder: formulation → Discovery → elaboration (involve the right people) → solution (final elaboration for development) → Delivery → feedback; (10) after validation log to the decision journal: hypothesis → outcome (PASS/FAIL/PARTIAL) → metric change (translated to ΔGMV/ΔLTV, otherwise value can't be computed) → decision (roll out / rework / kill) → next step; the Δ is confirmed by a dashboard before the card closes; log FAILs always, otherwise ideas repeat; the log goes to a shared read-only place. Fast 15-minute "hypothesis sprint" to warm up an idea cheaply: 10 min case analysis from data (logs, support, benchmark), 5 min ICE on 1–2 hypotheses, top one into a Discovery plan. Portfolio health per quarter (from the journal): validation rate (PASS+PARTIAL)/closed ≥ 40%; speed to verdict ≤ 20 days; 90-day ROI Σ(ΔGMV or ΔLTV)/validation costs > 3×; kill-early rate (closed before development) ≥ 25%.

**Pitfalls:** mixing business and research goals (unmeasurable conclusions or a report into a drawer); no image of the result and no next steps (insights sink into storage); slogan hypotheses ("the site is disliked") or hypotheses containing the answer; method chosen before the question; the brief filled pro forma; missing audience definition → wrong recruits → irrelevant data; a wishlist without must/should split; "we'll measure after release" without baseline and PASS described in words; FAILs not recorded (the team repeats the same ideas); dogfooding and colleagues' opinions taken as hypothesis validation; causal wording ("because") in a hypothesis to be checked only by descriptive methods.
