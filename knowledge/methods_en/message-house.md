# Message house: promise, pillars, proof

## When to apply
When the product strategy has been chosen and you need to decide: to whom, what, and on what grounds we
communicate. The communication strategy is aligned with the product strategy: we promise only what the
product already does or will do by the time of the campaign (every promise is confirmed in the product —
a gate criterion of the strategy step).

## Core idea
The message house is a "house" construction: the roof is the key message, the pillars are 2–4 supporting
theses, the foundation is the proof. Everything we publish is assembled from these elements rather than
invented anew for each channel.

- **Roof (key message):** one phrase that answers the target audience's question "what does this change
  for me". Checks: no jargon, a concrete benefit, distinguishable from competitors (link to [[M-usp]]).
- **Pillars (supports):** 2–4 theses, each expanding part of the roof — product, price/terms, trust.
  The order of the pillars = the message priority for this audience.
- **Foundation (proof):** for every promise there is a confirmation: a fact with [C###]/[S###], a demo,
  a case study, a product number. A promise without proof in the foundation is not published.

The comprehension barrier: a message does not work if the audience (1) does not understand the words
(jargon, abstractions), (2) cannot distinguish it from competitors, (3) does not believe it. The barrier
check is the 5-second test: show the message for 5 seconds and ask "what is this product and how is it
better than the alternative".

The role of channels: a channel is not "yet another venue" but a role in the journey: where the audience
learns about the barrier, where it looks for confirmation, where it decides. Each channel gets its own
message from the house (the same roof, a different depth), not a different house.

The audience's situation: communication is built from the **barrier** (what currently prevents them from
using/paying) — the message removes the barrier instead of "talking about features". The formula:
audience + situation → barrier → key message → confirmation → channel with a role.

## Procedure
1. Fix the audience and its situation: what they do now, how they solve the task today ([[M-customers]]).
2. Formulate the barrier: why they are not our customer yet (does not understand, does not believe,
   cannot distinguish us, switching costs too much).
3. Write the key message (the roof): removes the barrier, no jargon, 1 phrase; check it with the
   5-second test.
4. Assemble 2–4 pillars; for each one, a foundation of proof; check the promises against the product
   (what already works).
5. Assign channels roles in the audience's journey and choose the message "depth" for each of them.
6. Reconcile with the product strategy: every promise is backed by a feature/number by the moment of the
   campaign; discrepancies are escalated.
7. Write the verification plan: awareness/comprehension/trust, a comparison baseline, a timeframe
   ([[M-source-criticism]] for data).

## Typical mistakes
- A "house" without a foundation: a flashy roof, promises backed by nothing — distrust and drop-off.
- A feature-catalog message instead of an answer to the barrier: "we do A, B, C" instead of
  "you will no longer have to X".
- Different "houses" for different channels: the message blurs, the audience cannot assemble the picture.
- Jargon and abstractions: "an ecosystem of solutions for maximum efficiency" fails the 5-second test.
- Promising what is not in the product: the committee approved it, support catches the anger
  ([[M-product-communication]]).
- "Reach" metrics instead of checking the barrier: reach grows, comprehension does not.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which barrier actually holds the audience back — comprehension, trust, or distinctiveness?
- Which promise can we not prove today, and what do we do about it (drop it or wait for the product)?
- How does the audience verify our words after the first contact — and are our proofs present there?
- What would have to happen for us to change the key message (a revision criterion)?
