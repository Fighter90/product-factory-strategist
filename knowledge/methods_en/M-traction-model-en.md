# Traction Model (EN summary)

**When:** "what growth and revenue will the initiative/product give?"; "which hypotheses to bet on?"; "how to defend the forecast to leadership or an investor?"; "can hypothesis effects be summed?"; "check someone else's model for errors". The model underpins the business case, prioritization and plan-vs-fact tracking.

**Core:** a traction model (business case, growth model) is a forecast of the incremental effect of a product/initiative launch: `acquisition channels + product value (and value growth) + monetization = business result`; it answers how and on what horizon the product grows. **Four hypothesis layers** (a forecast is a "big hypothesis" of nested assumptions): 1) market — TAM/SAM/SOM, average check, competitor shares, legislation (the top-down check); 2) baseline ("if we do nothing") — funnel dynamics, base, prices, influence of other products; 3) growth drivers ("if we act") — funnel/base/metric changes from specific initiatives; 4) monetization — how income changes when drivers land (check, payment conversion, pricing). Extended scheme adds: revenue forecast, costs (fixed and driver-based), efficiency (UE, payback, NPV, IRR, EBITDA). Two structures: growth (new users, active base, conversion, ARPU, revenue) and efficiency (cost-to-serve, margin, LTV/CAC, opex). Metrics by stage: MVP/pilot — retention, activation, NPS; growth — growth rate, CAC, LTV, funnel conversions; maturity — ARPU, market share, margin; decline — churn, cost-to-serve.

**Approach choice:** stable history and market → baseline from time series (e.g. Prophet) with drivers overlaid; no data (new product) → no baseline, top-down scenarios of varying optimism; after MVP with CAC/LTV/retention → bottom-up conservative, cross-checked with top-down; increment from a feature/channel/geo → bottom-up with drivers; strategic frame, investors, "top-down check" → top-down. **Top-down**: TAM→SAM → market share → dynamics (CAGR, macro factors) → SOM → internal factors; weak spots: averaged retention, false seasonality (promo peaks mistaken for seasonal), underestimated cost-to-serve. **Bottom-up**: modules (PU, ARPPU, retention, conversions), cohorts (don't aggregate), transparent formulas, built-in sensitivity; limited by data scarcity and long-range vision. **Green zone: bottom-up vs top-down divergence < 15%.** Course Excel template: `Assumptions` sheet — parameter | value | unit | source/justification | confidence (high/medium/low); codes M1–M6 market (TAM/SAM, SOM share, market AOV, growth), D1–D9 demand funnel (MAU, CR MAU→search→query→confirm, cancel share, AOV, take rate), S1–S6 supply (listings, active share, PRO share, seller ARPU), SS1–SS12 seasonal coefficients (control row: sum = 12.0); hardcoded values = editable blue cells, everything else formulas. `Traction model` sheet — rows = metrics, columns = 60 months; blocks: MARKET ceiling (`SAM_GMV_month = TAM × SAM% × AOV / 12`; `SOM_cap = SAM × SOM%` growing pp/month); baseline demand tree: MAU × seasonal coefficient → ×CR search → ×CR query → ×CR confirm → orders paid → ×(1 − cancel share) = orders fulfilled (NSM) → ×AOV = GMV → ×take rate = commission revenue; supply: active listings × active share × season → VAS revenue = listings × ARPU/month; TOTAL: commissions + VAS, plus the check `GMV < SOM cap` — bottom-up must not pierce the top-down ceiling; each month computed from the previous: `value_N = value_(N−1) × (1 + growth rate)` plus seasonal coefficient, first month referenced to Assumptions. Hypothesis block: hypothesis | metric changed | status | confidence | uplift | rollout % | source | validation method | cannibalization risk | scenario switch (on/off) — uplift applied as `baseline × (1 + uplift × rollout %)`; switches build base/optimistic/pessimistic scenarios. Costs sheet: cost of sales (per-order marketing/logistics — variable, into unit economics), staff (FTE × average salary with taxes), marketing, IT, other. Hygiene: not a single hardcoded number in calculation rows (everything references Assumptions); retrospective (dashboard facts) separated from forecast; helper coefficient rows instead of manual month fills.

**Adding effects:** hypothesis effects are mostly **not additive** — sum only hypotheses on disjoint segments/channels/periods (an assortment promo + a performance channel); overlapping (partner channel and mailing to the same base) means double counting. 4-question checklist: same driver/metric? same calendar period? total growth > 10% (double-digit)? same segment/channel? — "yes" on ≥2 → apply a discount: `Total = Σ effect_i × k`, k usually 0.5–0.8 (example: +6% and +5% on conversion in one segment → 11% summed → 5.5–8.8% net). **Elasticity and plateau:** effects are almost always nonlinear — they fade (novelty, habituation) or accumulate; set the plateau timing from benchmarks and interest decay; lay out early periods by weeks, not quarters; sanity-check the ceiling (a shoe buyer won't buy 12 pairs at once); a **hockey stick is a red flag**, allowed only with fast physical scaling (entering new geography). **Cannibalization in the model:** `growth = (was − cannibalization) × K_growth` (before hypotheses) or `growth = (was × K_growth) × (1 − K_cann)` (after the total effect); equivalent if `K_cann = cannibalization / was` — fix which base the coefficient refers to; price as driver: `Revenue = ARPPU × PU` — weigh ARPPU growth against PU decline. **External factors map:** consumers (behavior, demography) → buyers, deals, check; laws and economy (rate, taxes) → market size, check, cost; competitors → share, CAC; technology → market size, search, CAC. **Valuation link:** the model feeds DCF (discounted cash flows; sensitive to WACC and terminal value), CCA (multiples of comparable companies) and precedent transactions.

**How:** 1) define the model's purpose and audience (decision, funding, tracking), horizon, stage; 2) preconditions: market stability, seasonality sustainability, history depth, known macro factors; 3) decompose the target metric to atomic drivers (`MRR = clients × ARPU`, and onward) — sufficiency criterion: every component explained by drivers; 4) baseline: history and trends, seasonality, cohort behavior, inertia of past launches, inflation, price rises, neighboring products; no data → three scenarios instead of a baseline; 5) driver hypotheses: target metric (activation/acquisition/retention/virality), "changing X → result Y", effect size, source/benchmark, confidence grade; 6) effects: timing, addition, decay; cannibalization as an explicit block; 7) monetization and economics: revenue (`ARPPU × PU` or the model formula), costs, unit economics; 8) sensitivity: pessimistic/base/optimistic per key driver; identify the 2–3 drivers moving the result; 9) top-down cross-check: model ≤ SOM; divergence with top-down; channel sum ≤ TAM; 10) validation (below) and review by an independent person with OK/WARNING/ERROR status. **Validation — four technical error types**: rounding (round only the final; the sum of monthly roundings ≠ the annual figure; check `=IF(ABS(SUM(B10:M10)-N10)>0.1,1,0)`); time anchoring (the most common: momentary costs against multi-year income; point and cumulative metrics mixed; `MAU(N) = MAU(N−1) + New(N) − Churned(N)` for base balance); formula contradictions (one value computed differently in two places; SSOT rule — compute once, reference everywhere; named ranges for key cells); logical inconsistency (start + new − gone = end; non-negativity; CR1 85% × CR2 90% = 76.5%, but the model shows 80% — contradiction). CHECKS tab, binary 0/1, global status `=IF(SUM(checks)>0,"ERROR","OK")`: broken cells (`SUMPRODUCT(--ISERROR(...))`), negatives, percentage bounds, retention monotonicity, not above TAM. Benchmarks in validation: sanity check, calibration, deviation explanation; a benchmark is a range, compared at the same stage and segment. No data: proxy metrics (similar region/segment), expert interviews (ranges), a 1–2-month MVP with discounted extrapolation, three scenarios. Review (the author is blind: confirmation bias, anchoring, curse of knowledge): an analyst (technique), finance (economics), another PM (logic), a stakeholder (goals); validate before presentation, after major changes, before planning on the model, at handover. Red flags: unrealistic input dynamics; a missed-period shortfall carried over without justification; no history/trends in the baseline; cannibalization ignored; no input metrics ("commitment without inputs"); one strong assumption holding all outputs. Green flags: inputs justified by A/B, research, trends; trends discounted; "market share" not used as an input metric; a small premise change doesn't disproportionately shift outputs.

**Pitfalls:** ignoring cohort decay — overstates ARPU/LTV by 30–40%; aggregate retention without phasing — wrong budget allocation; linear growth missing seasonality and jumps; an error in the monetization model — wrong ARPU/LTV; weak data hygiene — garbage in, garbage out; no sensitivity — false confidence; effects summed without a discount — inflated total at overlaps; a driver that is a correlation, not a cause, or one the team doesn't control — an unrealizable plan.
