# Unit Economics: LTV, CAC, Payback (EN summary)

**When:** "does the economics converge?", "should we scale acquisition?", "which channel pays back?", "how to test price/monetization per unit" — the entry block of the traction model and the monetization checkpoint.

**Core:** a **unit** is the basic revenue-bearing unit: order, client, deal, ride, event. Main rule: `UE = LTV − CAC`; the economics converge when `LTV > CAC`; expanded: `UE = (unit revenue − unit cost) × lifetime periods − CAC`. LTV = the margin a client brings over their lifetime; CAC = acquisition spend / acquired count (always per channel). **Contribution margin** `CM = Revenue − Variable costs`; per unit `CM_unit = ARPU − unit variable costs`; marketing share of revenue (Marketing/Revenue) is the controlled "advertising" slice of variable costs. `GM% = GM1 / Revenue`, where GM1 is profit after variable, before fixed costs. Positive UE does not prove product profitability (fixed and indirect costs unaccounted); **negative UE guarantees a loss at any scale**. Compute **by cohorts**: a base average hides losing new cohorts behind profitable old ones. Five improvement levers: price (ARPU); frequency/retention; variable cost per order; CAC (efficient channels); operational efficiency. Place in the P&L: UE counts only variable costs; fixed, indirect and capex belong to the financial model — the CM structure is `Revenue − Variable = CM → − Fixed = EBITDA`, and `UE = CM_unit × Lifetime − CAC`; cost hierarchy: variable → direct → indirect → admin → net profit ("unit economics fails early" — it's the earliest indicator). Classification rule: a cost arising per unit is variable (order logistics, acquiring 1.5–3% of transaction, packaging, per-deal bonus, SMS); volume-independent is fixed (rent, salaries, depreciation, flat-license software); scale-dependent costs (support, hosting) are a gray zone — book the fixed share into UE as a percentage; mixed cost `Y = a + bX` (salary + % of sales) splits into fixed a and rate b; stepped costs (hosting, call center) — account for the step jump. Business-level metrics: breakeven `BEP = Fixed / CM_unit`; operating leverage `DOL = CM / EBITDA` (+10% sales → +DOL×10% profit); safety margin `(Sales − BEP)/Sales`; volume for target profit `(Fixed + target profit) / CM_unit`. Lifecycle: don't compute UE at creation (too early), model it when hypotheses confirm, it's critical at scaling, a prioritization tool at maturity; exception — deliberate "investment holidays" running at a loss with plans B and C. Unit by business model: subscription → user; transactions/marketplace → order/deal; retail → order or SKU; frequency business (scooters, rentals) → client; advertising → impression. Two "turns" of one business: per transaction (new business, unknown return rate) and per client (mature base with a known return pattern — acquisition amortized over 3 trips); compute both where useful. Absence of cohort data does NOT mean "count per product" (a causality trap): unit choice follows the business model, not data availability. **Seven LTV methods:** 1 classic `ARPU × Lifetime × GM%` (subscription, stable ARPU/margin, predictable churn); 2 retention/cohorts `Σ_t (ARPU_t × GM% × Retention_t)` (cohort data, changing ARPU, uneven retention); 3 via churn `ARPU × (1/Churn) × GM%` (stable churn, long retention plateau); 4 gross margin per order `GM1_order × orders per user × lifetime` (heavy logistics: e-grocery, food delivery); 5 ARPPU (`ARPU = ARPPU × payer conversion; LTV = ARPU × Lifetime × GM%`; freemium, small payer share); 6 GMV (`GMV per user × commission% − variable costs = GM1 per user; LTV = GM1 per user × lifetime`; commission marketplaces); 7 AOV × frequency (`ARPU = AOV × F; LTV = AOV × F × Lifetime × GM%`; repeat-purchase e-commerce). Selection matrix: subscription with stable ARPU/churn → 1 or 3; cohorts and shifting ARPU → 2; heavy logistics → 4; freemium → 5; GMV commission → 6; repeat purchases → 7. Start simple; complicate only on visible distortion; two methods giving very different LTV means one is wrong — find the cause. Course rules: **Lifetime** is the most unstable parameter — retro cohort data (high accuracy), churn (medium), industry benchmark, expert guess (low); at start never set Lifetime above 3 years; `Lifetime = 1/Churn` is valid only at constant churn and explodes as churn → 0. **ARPU across tariffs** is weighted by base shares `Σ(tariff price × tariff share)`, not an arithmetic mean; compute UE per geo separately or average explicitly. **Median over mean** for transactional businesses: order spread >3× → median; >10× → mandatory ("95 orders at 500 and 5 at 50 000: the 3 000 average is a lie; the typical order is 500"); cross-margin matters more than per-item margin (2%-margin traffic magnets carry the 8%-margin robot vacuum in the basket). **Freemium:** costs are borne by everyone, revenue comes from payers; `CAC_payer = CAC_acquisition / CR_free→paid` (marketing CAC 100 at 5% conversion → the real payer costs 2 000); minimum payer share ≈ cost per user / payer ARPU; shifting part of the cost to buyers (a service fee) improves margin if payer churn doesn't rise. **Cohort breakeven:** cumulative cohort revenue crosses CAC after N months (payback) — until then it's an "investment cycle": continue consciously (a breakeven plan plus funding for it) or stop; with positive UE acquisition can be paused and margin reinvested — marketing spend needn't be uniform. CPA model (booking platform): `CPA = AOV × take rate × booking→payment conversion`; `Revenue = 365 × DAU × OrderRate × CPA`; `clientLTV = LifetimeSessions × OrderRate × CPA` with `LifetimeSessions ≈ (1 + MRR + MRR² + …) × Stickiness`; two reconciliation points: Revenue vs Expenses (is the business profitable overall) and clientLTV vs CAC (does acquisition pay back); compute on the marketing-acquired cohort; check dimensionality (stickiness = DAU/MAU, 0–1, plus days and sessions multipliers).

**How:** (1) define the unit and period; fix the units of every quantity; (2) choose the LTV method from the table; record the rationale and missing data; (3) gather inputs (ARPU/AOV/F/churn/retention/GM1) separately by cohort and channel, each with a source and evidence grade; (4) compute LTV with substituted numbers — Lifetime = 1/Churn or the retention-curve sum, capped by the model horizon; (5) compute CAC per channel (`channel spend / channel new clients`); show blended too, but decide per channel; (6) compute UE = LTV − CAC and LTV/CAC; payback = `CAC / (period ARPU × GM%)`, or the minimal T where cumulative cohort margin ≥ CAC; (7) cross-check with a second LTV method and a benchmark (thresholds only from A–B-grade sources; this method's threshold is simply LTV > CAC); (8) levers: UE sensitivity to ±10% in check size, margin, retention, CAC — which lever gives the most; (9) pass the result to the traction model and monetization. Worked example: ARPU 500/mo, churn 5%/mo, GM 70%, CAC 3 000 → Lifetime 20 mo, LTV = 500×20×0.7 = 7 000, UE = 4 000, LTV/CAC ≈ 2.3, payback ≈ 8.6 months; capped at a 12-month horizon LTV = 4 200, UE = 1 200 — the conclusion is sensitive to the horizon assumption. Auto-checks: retention and churn ∈ [0;1], retention never rises without reactivation; identical period units across ARPU, Lifetime, churn, payback; GM% computed after variable costs; cohort sum ≈ total with the difference explained; acquisition costs and revenue attributed to the same cohort and comparable periods.

**Pitfalls:** an average LTV over the whole base instead of cohorts (old and new mixed); revenue instead of margin (GM% forgotten); fixed costs inside UE — or variable costs (logistics, acquiring) ignored entirely; period mismatches (one-moment costs against years of revenue); Lifetime = 1/churn at tiny churn inflating LTV; blended CAC hiding losing channels; "UE > 0, so we're profitable" without fixed costs; LTV/CAC benchmarks without a comparability check; marketing CAC instead of payer CAC in freemium; a simple instead of tariff-weighted ARPU; a hardcoded Lifetime > 3 years; mixing Gross and Net margin — salaries, rent, development and marketing itself are not in CM/Gross Margin (marketing sits in CAC).
