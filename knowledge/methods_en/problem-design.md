# Problem Design (EN summary)

**When:** a leader brings a vague request ("conversion dropped", "clients complain", "we want feature X"). The method turns it into testable problem hypotheses with a root cause, segment and metric, selects the research approach, and ranks problems so resources go to the most significant ones.

**Core:** a **problem** is an unmet user need blocking the desired outcome — it exists in the user, not in the product or the team. Problem signs: (1) belongs to the user, not the team; (2) blocks an outcome; (3) systemic — recurs across many users; (4) has context (where, when, which scenario); (5) triggers a reaction — anger, distrust, workarounds ("kludges"). A **symptom** is the external manifestation of a hidden problem (a metric, behavior, complaint): it answers "what is happening" but not why — e.g. falling order conversion ← the user can't find the product; "the filter doesn't work" ← can't filter by the criterion that matters; rising pickup-point refusals ← can't judge real size/color/quality — too few photos and description. Formulas used literally — Problem: `[Typical user] experiences [specific difficulty] when [action/goal], because [something blocks]`. Problem hypothesis: `We believe [segment] has the problem [essence], because [cause/context], which leads to [negative consequence/metric]` (lecture example: 60% of professional sellers don't know their listing's search position because the stats lack that data, and can't manage promotion bids). Signal sources: internal ("what's happening") — metrics/analytics, support tickets, sales/marketing/PR feedback (especially B2B); external ("why") — interviews and surveys, market and competitors, open reviews (the users' vocabulary); real problems "leak" from analytics, support and user words simultaneously. Research: qualitative (interviews, ethnography/observation, diary study) reveals the essence; quantitative (analytics, surveys) confirms scale; the hybrid chain analytics/support → interviews → survey builds an evidence-based solution (signal → cause → importance ranking). Method by stage: Discovery/Idea — is there pain (in-depth interviews, ethnography, diary); Problem/Solution Fit — why it arises (qualitative + first rough surveys); PMF — how many suffer (analytics, large surveys, A/B); Scale — growth bottlenecks (end-to-end analytics, logs, NPS). Prioritization: **ICE** = Impact × Confidence × Ease (1–5 or 1–10 scale; fast, minimal data; example: distrust of sellers 3×4×3=36, delivery benefits unclear 2×3×5=30, damage fear 4×3×2=24); **RICE** = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort (example: 4×3×4/3=16; 2×4×3/2=12; 2×2×3/5=2.4; Impact often on a 0.5–3 scale, Effort in person-weeks); **Kano** survey ("how would you feel if the problem were fixed / persisted") — must-be (fix first), performance (better = more satisfied), delighters (with spare resources), indifferent (spend nothing), reverse (don't "fix"; possibly a false hypothesis). Scoring is a ranking instrument, not truth: scores are ordinal; business goals override (a strategy sacrificing GMV for margin demotes the "high commission" problem). Verification rule: Impact anchored to the baseline metric and affected share; Reach from analytics/survey; Confidence high for analytics+support+interviews, medium for interviews or survey alone, low for team guesswork; Effort estimated by engineers, not the PM.

**How:** (1) fix the symptom with metric, period, segment and baseline — no metric means it's a "complaint", mark as hypothesis; (2) collect signals from ≥2 independent sources (internal + external); one source = hypothesis, not conclusion; (3) interpret: signal → interpretation → hypothesis ("users don't reach the CTA" → "the button is below the fold"); alternatively JTBD ("When I…, I want…, so that…" → what blocks? which kludge do they use?); (4) dig to the root cause with **5 whys**, each answer naming a product or behavioral barrier, until you reach a cause the product can influence — example: 30% returns ← item doesn't match expectations ← photo color/size differ ← too few photos and data ← sellers not required to fill them ← uploading content is hard and slow; stop at the level where an action and an owner exist; verify each link with data; (5) write the problem hypothesis by the formula plus: symptom metric (baseline), segment, verification signal (specific answer/behavior), confirmation criterion (what share of respondents or metric shift confirms it); (6) choose the verification method (table above), sample and timing; (7) prioritize problems (ICE/RICE/Kano), every score with a data source; (8) record in the problem backlog: segment → formulation → metrics → confirmed reach → next step → owner; hand off to the roadmap (problem roadmap) and solution design.

**Pitfalls:** a solution-hypothesis ("we need a fast review filter" instead of "the user can't choose a product because reviews overwhelm them"); too generic and unvalidated ("users don't like the product"); no metric link ("sometimes confusing at checkout" instead of "up to 15% of paid orders are lost at the last step"); unclear who has the problem ("people don't understand how to find housing" instead of "new mobile users don't see the map entry"); treating the symptom as the problem and jumping to solutions; stopping 5 whys at the first answer; a survey where interviews are needed (surveys confirm scale but don't reveal causes) or interviews where numbers are needed; RICE scores "by eye" without sources; the PM estimating Effort; fitting scores to the desired conclusion; trying to solve every problem — resources are always fewer than tasks, and not every problem needs solving.
