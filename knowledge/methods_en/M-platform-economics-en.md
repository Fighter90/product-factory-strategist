# Platform Economics (EN summary)

**When:** "does the platform economics close, and how to prove it to the committee?"; "what to compare against and how to count the effect?"; "when does it pay back and at how many connections?"; "which assumptions are critical and how to verify them?" Used after the platform concept; linked to unit economics.

**Core:** platform economics = **TCO** (build, support, integrations) + **business effect** (what becomes faster, bigger, better — converted to money) + **comparison with alternatives** (As Is without the platform vs To Be with it). A direct "revenue effect of the platform as a product" is hard to defend — the usual route is comparing platform TCO against alternatives. Cost structure: local solutions (development in each team, repeated rework, support and infrastructure) vs platform (core and launch, client integrations, migration and client-side launches, infrastructure). Costs split into one-off (core build, architectural changes, migration and transition tooling) and recurring (platform team, client support, documentation); an external market adds CAPEX (material assets, e.g. servers) with depreciation (non-cash, not in cash costs) and OPEX (operations). The unit of labor is an FTE-month at the organization's average employee cost; finance finalizes the full model. **Six effect sources**: incremental revenue or growth of other user metrics; infrastructure savings; faster launches (TTM); lower run costs; avoided development (teams and solutions not created); prevented losses.

Key formulas: base comparison `ΔFCF = FCF_ToBe − FCF_AsIs` by year; `Cumulative ΔFCF = Σ ΔFCF`; **break-even** = the first year where cumulative ΔFCF ≥ 0. Payback threshold by client count: `launches = (Build + Run over horizon) / saving per launch`, where saving per launch = (local build + local support − platform onboarding). ROI = (benefit − cost) / cost; **IRR** = the discount rate at which NPV of the ΔFCF stream = 0 (more persuasive than ROI for a committee). WACC — weighted average cost of capital, higher risk → higher rate; discounting `PV = ΔFCF_t / (1 + WACC)^t`; terminal value `TV = sustainable annual FCF / WACC` (only the sustainable post-horizon flow); `NPV = Σ PV ΔFCF + PV TV`. **Incremental effect (CRM-type)**: Audience → Coverage (share reached) → Conversion (share of the target action) → Increment (difference vs control, A/B) → Value (increment × action value → revenue, EBITDA, FCF). Count increment from **company revenue**, not GMV and not the take rate directly; revenue ≠ margin (increment in deal volume at tiny margin is worth almost nothing in money). TTM effect: `saving = (local launch time − platform launch time) × monthly margin effect × number of launches`. For ecosystem (market) platforms: `Revenue = GMV × take rate`, differentiable by side; subsidize the deficient side (free/negative price), monetize the side whose value grows with liquidity; unit economics per side and cohort; liquidity (share of completed search→deal journeys) is the main health indicator; network effects and multi-homing affect take-rate durability. Platform metrics: number of client teams; platform usage; onboarding TTM; incremental effect "with vs without"; adoption separately — by clients (base width), by places/components (replacement depth), by volume (share of traffic/impressions through the platform).

**How:** 1) fix the management decision the model must support: launch / narrow scope / postpone / reject / name the critical condition / schedule the next check; 2) assemble the 10-block business case: part 1 — problem, solution, clients, alternative, adoption (who connects when); part 2 — effect (via what), costs, payback, scenarios, recommendation; 3) build the As Is / To Be model: assumptions (horizon 3–4 years, onboarding schedule by year, FTE-month cost, local build + support per launch, platform build, annual run, onboarding cost); `FCF_AsIs = −(local costs × N launches)`; `FCF_ToBe = −Build − Run − (onboarding × N)`; ΔFCF, cumulative flow, break-even; then an effects row (payment/infrastructure savings, earlier launch) on top of the base ΔFCF; 4) compute ROI, IRR, payback, break-even by client count; WACC, TV, NPV when needed; 5) run scenarios (conservative / base / optimistic) on the sensitive parameters: number of connections, local costs, build and run cost, onboarding cost, adoption pace; honesty rule: numbers from approved plans and actual costs of your own and neighboring teams, not industry benchmarks — every line must be checkable; 6) register assumptions: parameter and value → source (expert estimate / past products / benchmark) → allowed range → assumption owner → how and when to verify; 7) link the model to the roadmap — economics change by stage and each stage gets its own decision: Discovery (estimate the range) → Build (invest in the core) → Pilot (verify with a client) → Scale (onboard the next) → Close (switch off legacy); 8) formulate the management conclusion: decision, constraints, next step; PR-level deliverable is three pages — decision and alternative, economics, result and recommendation. Illustrative example: payment platform; local = 15 FTE-months build (4.5M ₽) + 24 FTE-months/yr support (7.2M ₽/yr) per launch; platform = build 30M ₽, run 12M ₽/yr, onboarding 2 FTE-months (0.6M ₽); launches 0/2/3/4, horizon 3 years. TCO only: ΔFCF = −30 / +10.2 / +21.3 / +32.4; cumulative −30 / −19.8 / +1.5 / +33.9 → payback in year 2, IRR ≈ 40%. Break-even threshold: saving per launch = 4.5 + 7.2 − 0.6 = 11.1; costs = 30 + 12×3 = 66 → 66 / 11.1 ≈ 6 launches — fewer than six doesn't pay back. With effects (acquiring savings +2M ₽/mo; earlier launch 3 months × 2M ₽ × N): ΔFCF = −30 / +46.2 / +63.3 / +80.4 → payback ~8 months into year 1; the conservative 5-launch scenario is positive only with effects — adoption is critical.

Auto-checks: revenue ≠ margin, increment from revenue not GMV, margin stated separately; consistent units and periods (FTE-months, years, ₽); dual-run and migration included in platform costs; effects counted only on products in the approved plan ("products that might appear" are not in the model); cumulative ΔFCF ≥ 0 in some year → the payback year is named; if the conservative scenario doesn't close — that IS the conclusion; the model is recomputed at every review with changed numbers explained.

**Pitfalls:** booking all GMV through the platform as its incremental revenue (the effect is the ΔFCF of two scenarios, "with" and "without"); ignoring migration, dual-run and client support — platform TCO understated; booking FTE savings as a cash effect when people stay in the company; a single point estimate instead of ranges and scenarios; a benchmark instead of actual costs of your own teams; ROI instead of IRR as the only argument; forgotten TV with a sustainable flow (or TV with an unsustainable one); a static model not recalculated after the pilot although economics must be refined along with the roadmap.
