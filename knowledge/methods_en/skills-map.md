# Methods Map (EN summary)

**When:** the product leader asks a vague question ("the product isn't growing", "we need to defend the project", "should we enter the market") and the agent must choose which cards to read in which order. Start here when the route isn't obvious; for a crisp task ("compute TAM", "build the traction model") go straight to the card.

**Core — three routing principles:** 1) order: context and stage first, then market and environment, then problem/solution, then goals and metrics, then modeling and economics, then plan and launch, then scaling — any theme can be revisited at any phase; 2) market analysis and modeling apply at any lifecycle stage; only depth changes (IDEA — top-down estimates; SCALE — full validation); 3) every external number entering a calculation passes source criticism and receives an A–D grade; assumptions without a source are marked explicitly.

**Skills → cards:** product-context-strategy → product context, strategy growth; business-models → business models; risk-management → risks; growth-frameworks → growth frameworks; market-sizing → market sizing; competitor-analysis → competitors; pestle-analysis → external environment; benchmarking → benchmarking; trend-analysis → trends/scenarios; analytical-reports → source criticism; problem-design → problem design (+ research types, JTBD); solution-design → solution design; product-metrics → product metrics, success metrics; growth-drivers/product-bets → goals-drivers-bets; traction-modeling/growth-effects/model-validation → traction model; unit-economics → unit economics; cannibalization → cannibalization; monetization-models/auction-monetization → monetization; roadmap-okr → outcome roadmap, roadmap; mvp-pilot-launch → MVP/pilots; team-org-structure → team; scaling-communication → PMF scaling, product communication.

**Competency matrix (9 categories, 0–5 self-assessment)** — second routing input when the request comes as a competency ("learn to plan", "upgrade analytics"): structure & simplification → source criticism, product communication, problem/solution design; management & communication → product communication, decision DACI, workshops; planning & organization → roadmap, delivery, team, risks; discovery quality & speed → research types, JTBD, CJM, problem design; analytics & metrics → product metrics, traction model, quant methods; PnL → unit economics, monetization; CX improvement → CJM, usability testing, loyalty metrics, UX backlog, pilots; PM & strategy → competitors, product context, strategy growth, outcome roadmap, USP, market sizing; ML/AI in products — a knowledge-base gap: no dedicated card; partially solution design (feasibility, TDR questions) and AI-in-research; name the gap honestly and assemble hypotheses from adjacent cards.

**Typical question → route:** launch a new product: product context → market sizing → competitors → business models → growth frameworks → problem design → solution design → MVP/pilots. Enter a market/segment: market sizing → source criticism → competitors → external environment → business models (Viability) → unit economics → risks. Year strategy: product context → strategy growth → market sizing → competitors → goals-drivers-bets → outcome roadmap → risks. Product not growing: product metrics → goals-drivers-bets → problem design → benchmarking → growth frameworks. Defend the project: product context → business models → market sizing → traction model (with validation) → risks → product communication. Plan the quarter: goals-drivers-bets → outcome roadmap → team → risks. Understand competitors: competitors → landscapes → benchmarking → market sizing. Choose metrics/NSM: product metrics → success metrics → goals-drivers-bets → benchmarking. Choose a monetization model or price: monetization → unit economics → traction model → auction. Compute unit economics: unit economics → benchmarking → traction model. Launch on an existing base: cannibalization → unit economics → traction model → benchmarking. PLG or SLG, subscription or commission: business models → monetization → growth frameworks. Verify a number or report: source criticism → benchmarking. Where the market is heading: external environment → trends/scenarios → source criticism → risks. Understand users and the problem (Discover): research types → audience research / in-depth interviews → interview analysis → JTBD → problem design. Choose and validate a solution (Define): solution design → MVP/pilots → success metrics → risks. Improve UX: UX frameworks → CJM → usability testing / UX heuristics → UX backlog. Positioning and communication: USP → marketing research → product communication. Loyalty, NPS: loyalty metrics → success metrics. Stakeholder dispute: decision DACI → workshops → risks.

**By agent process phase:** intake — product context, brief/hypotheses, strategy growth; research — external environment, market sizing, competitors, customers, benchmarking, source criticism, trends, research types; options — problem design, solution design, business models, growth frameworks, monetization, unit economics; strategy — strategy growth, goals-drivers-bets, traction model, outcome roadmap, cannibalization; validation — traction validation, source criticism, benchmarking, risks, MVP/pilots; after-decision — roadmap, delivery, success metrics, product metrics, PMF scaling, product communication. **By product stage:** IDEA — market sizing, external environment, competitors, business models, growth frameworks; top-down traction, first unit-economics pass. DISCOVERY — competitors (dogfooding), benchmarking, trends; unit economics. MVP — benchmarking (targets), competitors, risks; bottom-up traction, unit economics, monetization. GROWTH — market sizing (next segment), trends, goals-drivers-bets; full traction model, effects, cannibalization. SCALE — market sizing (new markets), external environment, PMF scaling; model validation, monetization incl. auctions, unit economics.

**How:** 1) phrase the leader's question in one sentence and find the nearest route row (or assemble the route by process stages); 2) ask 3–5 context questions (product and domain; current task; what's already done; what data exists; B2B/B2C, market stage) — skip what's known; 3) walk the route in order — each card produces an artifact feeding the next; 4) at each step record: input (data and its grade), output (artifact), open questions; 5) before the conclusion, run a verification step (sources, model, risks) and state assumptions explicitly; 6) transition criterion: the current phase's artifact is ready and key assumptions have validation plans.

**Pitfalls:** starting with modeling without market and sources — or endlessly collecting market data without a decision; reading all cards sequentially instead of the minimal route for the question; not returning to an early card when the conclusion refutes its assumptions (the route is iterative); mixing the growth model with the metrics framework; computing unit economics and the model before choosing the business model and monetization.
