# Dual-Track Agile: discovery and delivery — two tracks of one team

## When to apply
The team works in sprints, but discovery happens "whenever there is time", the backlog is a "dump of ideas", and the sprints are built from unvalidated features.

## Core idea
- **Discovery Track**: the answers to "what to build" — interviews, prototypes, value/usability tests. **Delivery Track**: validated ideas are turned into a working product. The tracks run in parallel, ONE team (not a "research department").
- **Progress by a readiness criterion**: an idea enters delivery only after its value/usability/feasibility have been validated (a definition of ready — validated, not "invented").
- **Cadence**: in every sprint, part of the capacity goes to discovery activities (interviews, tests, prototypes) and part to delivering validated work.
- **Artifacts**: an opportunity tree, an assumption map, prototypes, experiments with criteria — all of them short-lived and continuously updated.
- **Anti-pattern**: a "research sprint" once a quarter; "analysts do discovery, developers do delivery" (this breaks the learning loop).

## How to do it
1. Reserve a share of discovery capacity in every sprint (usually 20–30% for product teams).
2. Maintain an idea pipeline: opportunity tree → assumptions → tests → "ready for delivery".
3. Definition of ready = the value, usability, feasibility, and business risk have been validated.
4. Retro on both tracks: not only "what we shipped" but also "what we learned".

## Pitfalls
- Discovery without delivery = endless research; delivery without discovery = a conveyor belt of unvalidated features.
- "Done" without validated assumptions — a backlog of fiction.
- Different teams on the tracks — "one team" is lost.
