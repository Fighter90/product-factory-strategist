# Dual-Track Agile: discovery and delivery — two tracks of one team

## When to use
The team works in sprints, but discovery happens "someday," the backlog is an "idea dump," and sprints are built out of unvalidated features.

## Core idea
- **Discovery Track**: answers "what to build" — interviews, prototypes, value/usability tests. **Delivery Track**: validated ideas turn into a working product. The tracks run in parallel, ONE team (not a "research department").
- **Progress by readiness criteria**: an idea enters delivery only after value/usability/feasibility have been tested (a definition of ready — validated, not "invented").
- **Cadence**: in every sprint, part of the capacity goes to discovery activities (interviews, tests, prototypes), part — to shipping validated work.
- **Artifacts**: the opportunity tree, the assumption map, prototypes, experiments with criteria — all short-lived and continuously updated.
- **Antipattern**: a "research sprint" once a quarter; "discovery is the analyst's job, delivery is engineering's" (it breaks the learning loop).

## How to do it
1. Reserve a share of discovery capacity in every sprint (usually 20–30% for product teams).
2. Run an idea pipeline: opportunity tree → assumptions → tests → "ready for delivery."
3. Definition of ready = value, usability, feasibility, and business risk have been tested.
4. Retrospectives on both tracks: not only "what we shipped" but also "what we learned."

## Pitfalls
- Discovery without delivery = endless research; delivery without discovery = a conveyor belt of unvalidated features.
- "Done" without validated assumptions — a backlog of fiction.
- Different teams on the tracks — the "one team" principle is lost.
