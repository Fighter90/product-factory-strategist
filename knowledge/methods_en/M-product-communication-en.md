# Product Communication and Defense (EN summary)

**When:** turning analysis and a roadmap into decisions of the people who give money, people and priority: preparing the document (one-pager / decision memo / traction doc), the presentation, the stakeholder map and communication channel; then preserving the memory of the decision and reviewing the outcome.

**Core:** documents serve three tasks: align context in a large organization; make the decision on facts, not charisma; preserve the product's memory (another team comes tomorrow). Principle: "1 page → one decision"; a good document is not one with nothing to add but one with nothing to remove. Portfolio context: 200+ initiatives running simultaneously, 3K+ people in P&T; **six initiative types**: New Big Bet (high risk, potential ~1B incremental revenue or savings by year 5), Business as Usual, Product, Stream (a series of linked small improvements), Enabler (a "foundation" for other products, often not a product itself), Other; streams and enablers use modified gates (stream — stream-review and one headline metric; enabler — demand from consuming products). Product stages: New idea (Gate 1) → Opportunity Discovery → Solution Discovery (hypotheses tested, end-state/MLP/PMF criteria, TDR passed; Gate 2) → Development (MLP launched, PMF reached; Gate 3) → Scaling (Gate 4; quarterly deep dive on traction) → Baseline (maintain / pivot / close). **Time-to-market** = idea entering the portfolio → passing Gate 3, including "useful" (work, experiments, development) and "useless" (waiting on other teams/partners/resources) time; reference corridors: kick-off → Gate 2 ≈ 120 days, Gate 2 → 3 ≈ 6 months, Gate 3 → 4 ≥ 1 year; breaking the corridor is treated as a communication failure. **MECE**: mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive; ≤5 items per level; tests: >5 items → a layer is missing; two items mergeable by one phrase → not exclusive; a leftover ("besides iOS/Android another 15% of smartphones") → not exhaustive.

Documents: **one-pager** (Gate 1/2): (1) problem in a paragraph; (2) insights; (3) solution — MLP sketch; (4) metrics and MDE; (5) risks and plan B. **Decision memo** (≤4 pages, Gate 2→3): goal and ask; problem recap with numbers; solution and end-state, what's cut; MLP/MVP and why; PMF criteria in numbers; TDR summary (feasible / limited / infeasible); resources and budget; top-5 risks (probability × impact, owner, SLA, plan B); timeline/TTM; the decision requested (money, people, quota). **Traction doc** (≤5 slides or 2 pages): MECE segmentation (2–5 blocks), output metrics with a 3-year target, input drivers with causal links, pilot hypotheses (with MDE), 0–36-month growth timeline, risks and plan B. **Traffic-light dashboard** (3 layers): business result (GMV, revenue), user/PMF (NPS, retention D30, LTV/CAC), tech/ops (SLA, latency, contact rate); thresholds 🟡/🔴 come from the memo, colors are computed by rules, not manually; 1–2 core metrics + 3–5 proxies + counter metrics; ≤ ~5 on screen (illustrative thresholds: GMV −5% → 🟡, −10% → 🔴; NPS < 70 → 🟡, < 65 → 🔴). **Risk heatmap 3×3**: High-Impact × High-Prob = red before start; without a "red button" (feature flag, marketing freeze) no rollout. **TDR** — the tech team's mini-gate before Gate 2: load, server cost, latency, bus-factor; result goes into the memo. **Post-mortem** (≤2 pages): context/goal; hypothesis and metric (MDE); actions and chronology; fact vs target and root cause; what worked / didn't (3–5 items); next steps with owners; links; without actions it's a diary, not a tool.

**10 slides × 10 minutes**: 0 title + ask (1 phrase, 1 number, 1 decision); 1 pain; 2 pain proof (≤3 facts); 3 solution / end-state; 4 MLP and PMF criteria; 5 traction; 6 unit economics (LTV/CAC, payback); 7 resources; 8 risks and plan B; 9 the decision requested. Storyline: problem → dream → road → price → fears → action. Q&A by the "yes / and" rule ("yes, the number is bold, and there is a review every 3 months"). Dry run: read aloud, colleagues' tough questions, a stopwatch, tech check; tables in the memo, graphs on slides. **Anti-fumble checklist**: the punchline contains market share and payback, not just an absolute ("1B GMV = 5% share, 7-month payback"); the core metric has a number and a unit ("contact rate −30% in 6 months"); BEP below the board threshold in base and pessimistic scenarios; bus-factor ≥ 2 per critical service/role; every High×High risk has a plan B (feature flag, backup channel, manual process); a dry run with a devil's advocate as CFO/CTO/CMO. Five typical failures that kill even a perfect pitch: (1) empty punchline — a loud absolute without share and payback; (2) ghost metric — a goal without a unit; (3) tech blind spot — TDR green but bus-factor = 1; (4) "red pessimist" — base scenario OK, pessimistic breaks the payback threshold (fix: phased rollout, lower CAPEX/CAC); (5) risk without plan B. Repackaging principle: don't redo the design — close the hole with a fact, a number, a plan or a resource; in a quarter the same deck passes without questions. **Stakeholders**: channels — chats, meetings, reports, internal mailings, a unified portfolio feed; types — approvers, anxious, dominant, demanding, indifferent, initiators, silent experts, politicians, optimists, skeptics; tactics: anxious — risks and plan B early; dominant/demanding — pre-scheduled 1-on-1s and clear ask boundaries; silent experts — personal questions outside the meeting; skeptics — data and a failure criterion; approvers — a sign-off checklist. Writing rules: short and simple; one idea per sentence; don't write what you're unsure about; share results, not process; give answers, not questions; back "grew/fell" with numbers and significance.

**How:** 1) define the decision requested and who decides it (Driver / Approver); deadline and format (Gate, deep dive, QBR); 2) pick the document by gate: Gate 1 → one-pager; Gate 2/3 → decision memo + TDR + risk log; quarter → deep dive with the traffic light; 3) assemble facts and split them in the document: facts (source, period, n), hypotheses (how tested, criterion), forecasts (assumptions, scenarios); numbers instead of adjectives — replace "we want to improve loyalty" with "NPS +7pp over 2 quarters"; 4) fix PMF/success criteria and traffic-light thresholds in the document BEFORE launch; top-5 risks with owners, SLA and plan B; 5) structure MECE (≤5 items per level); verify input → output links; 6) run 2–3 review passes (D-7 draft; D-4 cuts and number check; D-2 dry run); agree contentious points with critical stakeholders beforehand; send the memo ≥48h before the board; 7) at the defense: ask and key number first, then pain, solution, price, risks; on objections — "yes / and" with a plan; 8) after the decision: log it in the decision journal, update the roadmap, assign action owners, schedule the post-mortem (date, metric, MDE).

**Pitfalls:** "we'll do it because competitors did" — no numbers, no key-metric impact; requesting "3 more months of research" past the TTM corridor; Discovery without stop criteria; "users will like it" without MDE and an NPS target; a 30-slide document; strategy and feature levels on one slide; a backlog copy-paste instead of 3 drivers; a dashboard metric without a threshold; 10+ metrics; a red risk without owner and SLA; no plan B / red button; risks discussed for the first time at the board; last-minute approvals and unknown opponents; a post-mortem without causes and actions or turned into blame-hunting.
