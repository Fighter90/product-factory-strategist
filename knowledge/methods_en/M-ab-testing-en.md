# A/B Testing: Full Cycle and Alternatives (EN summary)

**When:** proving a causal link "change X → metric Y moves" before full rollout; estimating upfront how much traffic and how many weeks it takes; interpreting the result honestly; finding alternatives when user-level randomization is impossible (network effects, offline campaigns, "already shipped to everyone").

**Core:** An A/B test = random split, each group sees its version, metrics compared — the gold standard because external factors (season, competitors, marketing) hit both groups equally. Evidence hierarchy: survey (opinion, self-selection) → matching (residual bias) → before/after (trends, external events) → A/B (causality). H₀ = no effect; decisions only by pre-registered rules; in a mature team only ~20% of tests are "green" — normal, not failure. A/B is unnecessary when: (1) the question is "why" not "how much" (interviews, UX research); (2) no alternatives exist (legal requirement, critical bug — just ship); (3) the expected effect is huge and obvious; (4) splitting is physically/ethically impossible. Metrics config: 1–2 target, 1–3 proxy (more sensitive, earlier signal), ≥2 guardrail (conversion, NPS/complaints, churn, fraud, latency) with thresholds; more metrics = power loss. Hypothesis template: "Variant B (change X) significantly changes metric M for segment S by ≥Z% vs A, at α and β, provided guardrails are no worse than threshold", plus a written H₀. Criterion by metric nature: averages (check, revenue/user) → classic t-test; shares and ratios (conversion, CTR, ARPU) → linearized t-test; percentiles/heavy tails (p95 latency) → quantile t-test on bootstrap; several groups → ANOVA + post-hoc (Tukey) with correction.

Sample size and duration: n = (z_α + z_β)²·2σ²/MDE² per group; α usually 0.01–0.05 (type I error), β 0.1–0.2 (power = 1−β ≥ 0.8), MDE in absolute fractions; at α=5%, β=20% the rule is n ≈ 16·σ²/MDE²; duration = n / (daily traffic × share per group) — but hold the test 7–14 days regardless, to cover a full weekly cycle (example: p=1.8%, σ²≈0.0177, MDE=1 pp → n ≈ 2,770 per group). Reverse calculation for fixed traffic: MDE = (z_α + z_β)·√(2σ²/n); if the reachable MDE exceeds the economically meaningful effect — don't run or redesign. Variance levers: more traffic or longer run; lower variance (linearization of ratio metrics, stratification, CUPED/ML-CUPED, sensitive proxy); a conscious α/β trade fixed before start.

Design: sticky randomization by a constant key (user_id / seller_id / region_id — "first contact → forever"; flickering between groups distorts metrics); layers (bucketing — independent experiments in different layers with different hash keys, 100 buckets per layer — hundreds of parallel tests without stealing traffic); exposure flag set as close to the UX change as possible and before the measured action; A/A test before A/B (≥24h, ideally a full weekly cycle; alarm if p<0.1 on any metric or group-size imbalance — catches broken randomization, caches, late exposure) plus "dark launch" (code at 100%, feature off by flag — latency warmup and instant rollback). Multiple comparisons: with m checks the chance of at least one false "win" (FWER) grows; at 10+ metrics raw p<0.05 means little. Bonferroni α′ = α/m (simple, conservative; fine for 1 target + 2–3 guardrails); Holm step-down (sort p ascending, thresholds α/m, α/(m−1)…, stop at first violation; more powerful; for 4–8 expensive checks); Benjamini–Hochberg controls FDR — find max k with p₍ₖ₎ ≤ (k/m)·Q (Q=5%; for 10–30 secondary checks). The correction is fixed before start; choosing it after seeing p is p-hacking. Peeking inflates α (10 daily looks raise the real α from 5% to ~19%): either decide exactly at the end, or sequential testing with α-spending (e.g. Pocock bounds), or a Bayesian stopping rule fixed upfront ("stop if P(Δ>0) ≥ 0.95 or P(Δ<0) ≥ 0.95").

Monitoring and interpretation: SRM/health — actual vs planned group sizes (50%±0.5 pp), expected vs actual exposures (±3%), covariate balance by device/age (p>0.3); any violation → stop and debug (an abnormally large effect is almost always a bug, not a success). Guardrails with pre-set thresholds; critical worsening = early stop (the only legal mid-flight look besides a sequential scheme); no evening launches without on-call; a unified experiment registry. Compute only after the end: effect, p-value, 95% CI (CI = x̄ ± z·σ/√n; CI excluding 0 ⇔ p<α two-sided); p-value is not effect size; significance ≠ business significance (+0.2% may be significant and useless). Segment forest plots (effect + 95% CI per segment, zero line) only for segments declared in advance. Verdicts: green (target ↑ significant, guardrails fine → check cuts/dynamics, roll out), green with nuances (guardrail slightly down, net increment > 0 → roll with monitoring/rework), red (guardrail worse than the gain, or target ↓ → don't roll, find cause), gray (nothing significant → check bugs and power — CUPED, proxy — and rebuild the hypothesis; don't extend "for luck"); every outcome recorded; decision Scale / Iterate / Drop. Bayesian alternative for low traffic: posterior = Beta(α₀+successes, β₀+failures), P(Δ>0) by Monte-Carlo; stopping rule (P threshold, expected loss) fixed in advance.

**How (7 "golden" steps):** (1) formulate problem and hypothesis per template (X → M → Z%, segment, α/β) + written H₀; (2) choose metrics: 1–2 target, 1–3 proxy, ≥2 guardrail with thresholds; criterion per metric nature; (3) justify MDE by economics (minimum effect that pays off the change), compute n and duration, confirm it fits the traffic, justify why classic A/B; (4) define the split: key, layer, sticky, traffic share; plan dark launch and A/A; (5) launch A/A → A/B; watch only health metrics and guardrails, never peek at targets; (6) analyze per plan: effect + p-value + 95% CI (with correction), cuts and weekly dynamics, Simpson and novelty checks; (7) decide Scale / Iterate / Drop and record the result with conclusions in the knowledge base.

Alternatives when classic A/B is impossible: regional test (randomization unit = city/store; match "twin pairs" by historical metrics like GMV/orders, then randomize pairs; <30 units → Welch/bootstrap, higher MDE); switch-back (the same region alternates test/control in time blocks ≥ a full user cycle; ABBA/BAAB order against trend; cluster t-test, cluster = region×period; for strong network effects — pricing, matching; not for long, noticeable changes); Diff-in-Diff: effect = Δ(test: after−before) − Δ(control: after−before), key assumption of parallel pre-trends checked on history; synthetic control ("synthetic" control as a weighted combination of similar objects matching the test before intervention; one unique region/channel, long history); PSM (retrospective matching of similar users to feature adopters; residual bias risk, sensitivity to feature set); interleaving (both ranking algorithms in one mixed feed to the same user; far more sensitive at low traffic — for relative algorithm order, not absolute business effect); multi-armed bandits (epsilon-greedy, Thompson sampling — traffic flows to the leader; loses strict p-value interpretation); fake door (demand measured by a "coming soon" button before build). Selection rule: user split possible → classic; geo only → regional; network effects → switch-back/geo-split; shipped to everyone with history → DiD/synthetic control/PSM; ranking algorithms in one feed → interleaving; little data → Bayesian or honestly "no answer"; assumptions and limits documented in the report.

**Pitfalls:** peeking (stop at the first "win" → α inflation); survivorship (conclusions from accidentally lit metrics outside the hypothesis — at m metrics ~α·m light up by chance); Simpson's paradox (every segment up, total down due to traffic-mix change: channels with CTR 10%/90% split 50/50 give 50%; after re-mixing to 90/10 at CTR 11%/91% the total ≈ 19% — check structure and total); novelty/habituation effect (check weekly dynamics, keep an "old users" subsample); access bias (test = updaters, control = everyone — compare comparable subgroups); experiment interference (someone else's test in your control — layers + registry); ignored seasonality (run at least 1–2 full weeks); MDE "from the ceiling" or fitted post hoc; α/β changed mid-flight; extending a gray test "one more week"; no A/A or health dashboard ("victory" turns out to be a cache bug or late exposure); comparing non-equivalent subgroups; no rollback plan; conflict with a parallel test of another team.
