# Go-to-Market: launch tiers — a resource decision, not a news genre

## When to apply
You are taking a feature or a product to market; you are planning a quarter of launches; you are arguing with the product team about how many resources a release deserves; you want launches to stop being "a blog post after the fact".

## Essence
- **A tier is the size of the bet, not the type of news**: it defines the budget, the timelines (T1 ≈ 8–12 weeks, T2 ≈ 4–6, T3 — a changelog entry and one sentence for sales) and the owner (DRI). It is set before planning, not after.
- **Tier criteria**: revenue/goal impact (net new revenue vs expansion vs retention), the size of the affected audience, differentiation (do competitors have it), the depth of change to user habits. A scoring rubric (in the spirit of Intercom's P1–P4) removes the "my release matters more" argument — the scoring table decides, not the loudest voice.
- **Tier norms**: a few T1s per year (1–4), dozens of T2s, a stream of T3s/changelog entries. If every release is a T2, the audience tires and stops listening, and genuine T1s lose their weight.
- **Launch audience segmentation**: existing users (in-app, email by segment), the new market (press, content, paid channels), sales and CS (enablement, talk tracks, FAQ), the internal audience (the company, support — so that nobody learns about the feature from a customer).
- **Channels follow the tier**: T1 — PR/press, a landing page, a campaign, enablement, an event; T2 — the blog, email to a segment, in-app, social media; T3 — the changelog, help docs, a note to sales.
- **Launch metrics are fixed before the launch**: adoption (the share of the target audience that tried the feature, at 30/90 days), conversion to the target action, contribution to pipeline/net new, retention of the launched cohorts — plus counter-metrics (complaints, feature opt-outs, churn).
- **The checklist has three phases**: before (tier, goal, metrics, audiences, messages, assets, availability), during (a go/no-go gate, launch day by the minute), after (a 30/90-day report, a retro).
- A go/no-go gate with a named owner of the postponement decision — 48 hours before the launch.

## How to do it
1. Score the release against the criteria; assign the tier (PM + PMM; for T1 — C-level sign-off).
2. Fix the launch goal and the 30/90-day metrics with a baseline for comparison — before any work begins.
3. Build an "audience × channel × activity" map per tier; for T1, include sales and CS enablement in the plan as a mandatory stage.
4. Run the checklist: messages (what it is / what it does / why now), the top-3 objections, assets per tier, the availability model (all plans / beta / waitlist), support readiness.
5. Launch with monitoring of adoption and the counter-metrics; keep the go/no-go gate and a plan for rolling back communications.
6. At 30/90 days — an adoption vs plan report, a retro (which activities worked); feed the conclusions into the roadmap and the traction model.

## Pitfalls
- Tiers "by feel": every PM pulls their release toward T1; without a rubric, loudness wins over impact.
- A T1 without prepared sales and support fails at the very first customer call — enablement is not "after the launch".
- Metrics assigned after the launch — success is indistinguishable from noise, there is nothing to compare against.
- Everything is a T2: the audience disables notifications, nobody reads the changelog, the signal of big launches gets diluted.
