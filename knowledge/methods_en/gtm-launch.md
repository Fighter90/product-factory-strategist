# Go-to-Market: launch tiers — a resource decision, not a news genre

## When to apply
You are taking a feature or a product to market; you are planning a quarter of launches; you are arguing with a
product manager about how many resources a release deserves; you want launches to stop being "a blog post made
after the fact".

## Core idea
- **A tier is the size of the bet, not the type of news**: it determines the budget, the timeline (T1 ≈ 8–12
  weeks, T2 ≈ 4–6, T3 — a changelog entry and one sentence to sales) and the person responsible (DRI). It is
  defined before planning, not after.
- **Tier criteria**: impact on revenue/the goal (net new revenue vs expansion vs retention), the size of the
  affected audience, differentiation (do competitors have it), the depth of the change to user habits. A
  scoring rubric (in the spirit of Intercom's P1–P4) removes the "my release is more important" argument — the
  table decides, not the volume of the argument.
- **Tier norms**: a few T1s per year (1–4), dozens of T2s, a steady flow of T3/changelog entries. If every
  release is a T2, the audience grows tired and stops listening, and the real T1s lose their weight.
- **Launch audience segmentation**: existing users (in-app, email by segments), the new market (press,
  content, paid channels), sales and CS (enablement, talk tracks, FAQ), the internal audience (the company,
  support — so that nobody learns about the feature from a customer).
- **Channels follow the tier**: T1 — PR/press, a landing page, a campaign, enablement, an event; T2 — blog,
  email to a segment, in-app, social media; T3 — changelog, help docs, a note to sales.
- **Launch metrics are fixed before the launch**: adoption (the share of the target audience that has tried the
  feature, at 30/90 days), conversion to the target action, contribution to pipeline/net new, retention of the
  launched cohorts — plus counter-metrics (complaints, feature opt-outs, churn).
- **The checklist has three phases**: before (tier, goal, metrics, audiences, messages, assets, availability),
  during (a go/no-go gate, the launch day minute by minute), after (a 30/90-day report, a retro).
- A go/no-go gate with a named owner of the postponement decision — 48 hours before the launch.

## How to do it
1. Score the release against the criteria; assign the tier (PM + PMM; for a T1 — C-level agreement).
2. Fix the launch goal and the 30/90-day metrics with a comparison baseline — before any work begins.
3. Build a "audience × channel × activity" map for the tier; for a T1, include sales and CS enablement in the
   plan as a mandatory stage.
4. Run the checklist: messaging (what it is / what it does / why now), the top-3 objections, assets for the
   tier, the availability model (all plans / beta / waitlist), support readiness.
5. Launch with monitoring of adoption and counter-metrics; keep the go/no-go gate and a rollback plan for the
   communications.
6. After 30/90 days — a report of adoption vs plan, a retro (which activities worked); the conclusions go into
   the roadmap and the traction model.

## Pitfalls
- Tiering "by feel": every PM pulls their release toward T1; without a rubric, volume wins, not impact.
- A T1 without prepared sales and support fails on the very first customer call — enablement is not "after the
  launch".
- Metrics assigned after the launch — success is indistinguishable from noise, and there is nothing to compare
  against.
- Everything at T2: the audience turns off notifications, nobody reads the changelog, the signal of big
  launches gets diluted.
