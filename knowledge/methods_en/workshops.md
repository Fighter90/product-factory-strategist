# Workshops and facilitation

## When to apply
When a decision cannot be made by one person or a shared picture is needed: aligning the team around a goal, idea generation, a search for root
causes, prioritization, a retrospective, a CJM walkthrough, a strategy session. Facilitation is needed when the typical meeting pains are present: "we agreed
on nothing", one person does the talking, 100 stickies and zero ideas, the team does not believe in the decision, "handed down from above". If the decision has already been made and
is not up for discussion — a workshop only imitates participation, do not run it.

## Essence
**Facilitation** — the simplification and improvement of group discussion. The facilitator is responsible **for the process**, the team — **for the content**.
He is not a "boss" and not an expert; his main asset is neutrality. Four functions: the guardian of the rules (the goal, the meeting rules, agreed upon
by the group), the keeper of time (timeboxes, a reserve for transitions, stopping the unconstructive), the neutral chair (does not take a side,
redirects questions to the group), the manager of group dynamics (every 10–15 minutes asks the participants to do something; Me → We → All).

The facilitator does not voice his own opinion on the substance, does not push the decision of the opinion leaders, does not break the agreed rules unilaterally
(if a rule gets in the way — brings it out to the group), does not suppress a conflict by force, but helps the parties voice their positions and find common ground.

**Divergence → convergence** (the "diamond" principle): first you diverge into ideas without criticism, then you converge on a decision with criteria.
The time split "1-1-1": a third for expansion, a third for narrowing, a third for the decision and the next steps.

## Procedure

### 1. The brief (the compass) — before the invitations
Context | The goal (measurable) | The picture of the outcome ("what will be on the board/in the minutes") | Participants | Time and place | What is NOT the goal (constraints).
If the goal is "to discuss", the brief is not ready. For a decision workshop add: **which decision is being made, who makes it (the decision owner), by which
criteria, which data comes in as input**. The decision roles can be formalized per [[M-decision-daci]].

### 2. Participants
By roles (the knowledge of the user, the implementation, the business/strategy) and by knowledge (deep expertise, a fresh look, adjacent specialists). Up to ~8 active participants.
Better not to invite: the eternal critic, the direct manager of the majority (the "boss's opinion" effect), the dominator, the silent "passenger", those with
a personal negativity toward the topic. Hand over the data for the discussion in advance (read-ahead: the brief, the digest of the research).

### 3. The scenario (a minute-by-minute plan)
A standard frame for 60 minutes: warm-up 5 → the goal and the picture of the outcome 3 → individual divergence (silent) 10 → clustering 7 →
voting 10 → the discussion of the top 2 (for example, by ICE) 10 → the decision and To-Do 5 → a buffer 5 → the closing "what I take with me" 5.
Budget a reserve for the transitions between the exercises (about 20%). Formats: 60 min — one problem → one solution; 90 min — a classic
brainstorm + the selection; 120 min — add Ishikawa or the six hats. For online — the board and the templates are ready in advance, the access rights are checked, the timer is visible to everyone.

### 4. Tools
| Tool | For what | How |
|---|---|---|
| A warm-up / a "trailer" | Switch the brain; a provocative question on the topic | After the exercise let the people speak out, otherwise there will be a feeling of manipulation |
| HMW ("How might we…") | Turn a problem into a challenge | `How might we <the desired state> for <segment>, avoiding <constraint>?`; the wording stays on the board |
| Silent brainstorm / brainwriting / 1-2-4-All | Divergence without domination | The ideas are written silently → pairs → fours → everyone |
| The Ishikawa diagram | Root causes | The problem in the "head", 3 min for the causes individually, clusters along the "bones" (processes, people, tools…), for each branch "one more why" up to 5 times, an owner for each bone |
| The six hats (de Bono) | Change the angle of view | White — facts, red — feelings, black — risks, yellow — benefits, green — ideas, blue — process; a hard timer per hat |
| Dot voting | Convergence | `V = N/3` dots per person (N — the number of ideas); with 3 ideas do not use it — a "thumbs up/down" |
| "The royal decision" | A quick selection | The owner of the goal decides/votes with the golden right |
| A Venn diagram | A multi-criteria choice | Define the criteria (the circles), an idea closer to the intersection wins |
| ICE | A comparison of the top ideas | Impact × Confidence × Ease (the scores are set in advance) |

### 5. Difficult participants
The dominator: "Thank you, I will note it down. Let us first hear it through…"; a round in turn; limited time; a parking lot. The silent one: an addressed but
gentle appeal; a letter before the discussion; a 5-second pause after an open question. The skeptic: turn the criticism into a task for the group ("how
do we minimize this risk?"). Off-topic: the parking lot ("I will write it down and we will come back to it if we have time"). A conflict of experts: "I hear two positions; let us write out the facts
on which each stands". The phrasings — through "I hear/see" and a reference to the rules, not an evaluation of the person.

### 6. The exit and the control (the minutes — the publication — the confirmation)
1. **The minutes** ≤1 page: the date, the goal, the participants, a link to the board, the top ideas, the actions (who / what / when / the metric), the decisions made,
   the parking lot. The minimum exit: the top ideas, the To-Do, the owners and the deadlines; otherwise the session is chatter.
2. **The publication**: a page in a shared place, a link + a short summary to the team, the tasks in the tracker with a link to the minutes.
3. **The confirmation**: the owner confirms the task; a reminder if there is no reaction; a check of the execution in 2–4 weeks.
4. The results go into the UX backlog if there is one ([[M-ux-backlog]]); evaluate the ideas by impact and effort while the team is "hot".

### 7. Ready-made formats
- **Design Sprint** (5 days): the map and the focus → the sketches → the decision and the storyboard → the prototype → a test on ~5 users. It needs a concept and a validation; the team drops out of its normal work.
- **Lightning Decision Jam** (60–90 min): the pains → the voting → HMW → the silent ideas → the voting → Impact×Effort → the Action canvas. Many pains, little time, a quick "quick win" is needed.
- **A Value Proposition Canvas session** (about 3 h): the customer jobs → the pains/gains → the fit → the prioritization. The team argues about the value ([[M-jtbd]]).
- The choice by the rule: the time is <1 day → LDJ; a broad scope, a concept and a validation are needed → the Design Sprint; a doubt about the value → the VP canvas.

## Template / checklist

**The workshop brief:** Context | The decision/goal (measurable) | The picture of the outcome | The input data | The participants and roles | The time/place/format | Not the goal |
The selection criteria | The decision owner | The risks (a dominator, a conflict of interest).

**The scenario:** `Time | Block | The exercise (the verbatim wording) | The method | The output | The timekeeper`.

**The action-item canvas:** `What | Why | Owner | Deadline | KPI | Status`.

**The session quality metrics (the guidelines from the course notes, calibrate them):** the share of speech of the dominant participant <~35%; the share of ideas from the silent
generation ≥~40%; the actions per participant ≥0.5; the publication lag of the minutes ≤15 min; the share of the epics/tasks created from the actions — 100%; the share
closed within 30 days — a guideline of 70%.

**Before the closing:** the board is saved; the tasks are created; the minutes are published; the parking lot is transferred; a bot/a reminder about the check is set.

## Typical mistakes
| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No brief and no measurable goal | A discussion without a result | The brief before the invitations |
| The facilitator promotes their own solution | A loss of neutrality and trust | Return the content to the group |
| No timeboxes and no reserve for the transitions | The session sprawls | A timer on every block |
| Divergence and criticism at the same time | It blocks the ideas | Criticism only at the convergence |
| Dot voting with 2–3 options | Scattered votes | A "thumbs up/down" or "the royal decision" |
| A decision "handed down from above", a workshop for show | Distrust | Say honestly what is being discussed and what is not |
| No minutes and no owners | A graveyard file | The minutes ≤ 1 page, the tasks in the tracker |
| Too many participants (>8 active) | The pace drops | Split into groups, reduce the roster |
| Online without a prepared board and engagement | Passivity | An activation every 10–15 minutes |

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which exact decision must appear, and who is its owner? If it is "just to discuss" — the format is a different one (interviews with the experts).
- Is there input data (the research, the metrics)? Without it the ideas will remain opinions.
- Is open criticism acceptable in the team (psychological safety)? Otherwise — written formats and anonymous voting.
- What is the deadline, how many people are there and can they be occupied for a day/week?
- What will happen to the output: will it get into the backlog, the roadmap, the budget?
- The agent cannot chair a live group neutrally, but it can prepare the brief, the scenario, the templates and the minutes.
