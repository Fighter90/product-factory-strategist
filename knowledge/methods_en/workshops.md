# Workshops and Facilitation (EN summary)

**When:** a decision can't be made by one person or a shared picture is needed: aligning a team around a goal, idea generation, root-cause search, prioritization, retrospective, a CJM walkthrough, a strategy session. Facilitation fits when typical meeting pains appear: "we agreed on nothing", one person does the talking, 100 stickies and zero ideas, the team doesn't believe the decision, "handed down from above". If the decision is already made and not negotiable, a workshop only imitates participation — don't run it.

**Core:** facilitation = simplifying and improving group discussion. The facilitator owns the **process**, the team owns the **content**; they are neither boss nor expert — their main asset is neutrality.

Four functions: guardian of the rules (the goal and meeting rules agreed by the group), keeper of time (timeboxes, buffer for transitions, stopping the unconstructive), neutral chair (takes no side, redirects questions to the group), manager of group dynamics (asks participants to DO something every 10–15 minutes; I → We → All). The facilitator doesn't voice opinions on substance, doesn't promote opinion leaders' solutions, doesn't break agreed rules unilaterally (a blocking rule goes back to the group), doesn't suppress conflict but helps positions be voiced and common ground found. **Divergence → convergence** (the "diamond" principle): first expand into ideas without criticism, then converge on a decision by criteria; the 1-1-1 time rule: a third expanding, a third narrowing, a third deciding and next steps.

**How:** 1) **Brief (before invitations)**: context | measurable goal | image of the result ("what will be on the board/in the protocol") | participants | time and place | what is NOT the goal (constraints). If the goal is "to discuss", the brief isn't ready. For decision workshops add: which decision, who decides (the decision owner), by which criteria, what data comes in; decision roles per DACI. 2) **Participants**: by role (user knowledge, implementation, business/strategy) and by knowledge (deep expertise, fresh eyes, adjacent); ≤8 active; better not to invite: the eternal critic, the direct boss of most attendees (the "boss's opinion" effect), the dominator, the silent passenger, anyone with personal negativity toward the topic; send data ahead (read-ahead: the brief, a research digest). 3) **Scenario (minute-by-minute plan)**, 60-minute skeleton: warm-up 5 → goal and result image 3 → individual silent divergence 10 → clustering 7 → voting 10 → discussing the top-2 (e.g. by ICE) 10 → decision and to-dos 5 → buffer 5 → closing "what I take with me" 5; reserve ~20% for transitions. Formats: 60 min — one problem → one solution; 90 — classic brainstorm + selection; 120 — add Ishikawa or Six Hats; online — board and templates pre-built, access checked, timer visible to all. 4) **Tools**: warm-up/"trailer" (a provocative question; let people speak afterwards or it feels manipulative); HMW `How might we <desired state> for <segment>, avoiding <constraint>?` (stays on the board); silent brainstorm / brainwriting / 1-2-4-All (ideas written silently → pairs → fours → all); Ishikawa diagram (the problem in the "fish head"; 3 min individual causes; clusters along the bones — processes, people, tools; "one more why" up to 5 times per branch; an owner per bone); Six Hats (de Bono): white — facts, red — feelings, black — risks, yellow — benefits, green — ideas, blue — process; a hard timer per hat; dot voting: `V = N/3` dots per person (N = number of ideas); with 2–3 options don't use it — thumbs up/down; "royal choice" — the goal owner decides with the golden right; Venn diagram (criteria as circles; the idea closer to the intersection wins); ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease (scales agreed in advance). 5) **Difficult participants**: dominator — "thanks, I'll note that; let's first hear out…", round-robin, limited speaking time, parking lot; the silent one — a gentle addressed invitation, ideas collected in writing beforehand, a 5-second pause after open questions; the skeptic — turn criticism into a group task ("how do we minimize this risk?"); off-topic — parking ("noted, we'll come back if time allows"); expert conflict — "I hear two positions; let's write down the facts each stands on"; phrase through "I hear/I see" and reference to the rules, never judging the person.

6) **Output and control**: protocol ≤1 page (date, goal, participants, board link, top ideas, actions with who/what/when/metric, decisions, parking); minimum viable output: top ideas, to-dos, owners and deadlines — otherwise the session was just talk; publication: a page in a shared place, link + short digest to the team, tasks in the tracker linked to the protocol; confirmation: the owner confirms the task, a reminder if silent, an execution check in 2–4 weeks; results into the UX backlog; score ideas on impact and effort while the team is "hot". 7) **Ready formats**: Design Sprint (5 days: map and focus → sketches → decision and storyboard → prototype → test on ~5 users; needs a concept and takes the team out of normal work); Lightning Decision Jam (60–90 min: pains → voting → HMW → silent ideas → voting → Impact×Effort → action canvas; many pains, little time, a quick win needed); Value Proposition Canvas session (~3 h: client jobs → pains/gains → fit → prioritization; the team argues about value). Choice rule: <1 day → LDJ; broad scope, a concept and validation needed → Design Sprint; doubt about value → VP canvas. Session-quality metrics (calibrate): dominant speaker < ~35% of talk time; ideas from silent generation ≥ ~40%; actions per participant ≥ 0.5; protocol lag ≤ 15 min; 100% of actions turned into tracker tasks; ~70% closed within 30 days.

Templates: workshop brief (context | decision/goal | result image | input data | participants and roles | time/place/format | not-goal | choice criteria | decision owner | risks: dominator, conflict of interest); scenario `Time | Block | Task (verbatim wording) | Method | Output | Timekeeper`; action-item canvas `What | Why | Owner | Deadline | KPI | Status`; before closing: board saved, tasks created, protocol published, parking transferred, check-in reminder set.

**Pitfalls:** no brief and no measurable goal → discussion without a result; the facilitator promoting their own solution → neutrality and trust lost; no timeboxes or transition buffer → the session sprawls; divergence and criticism at once → ideas blocked; dot voting with 2–3 options → scattered votes (use thumbs or the royal choice); a decision handed down with a show workshop → distrust (state honestly what's negotiable); no protocol or owners → a file cemetery; more than 8 active participants → tempo drops (split into groups); online without a prepared board and engagement → passivity (activate every 10–15 minutes).
