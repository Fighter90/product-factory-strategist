# Workshops and facilitation

## When to apply
When a decision cannot be made by one person, or a shared picture is needed: aligning the team around a goal, idea generation, the search for root
causes, prioritization, a retrospective, a CJM walkthrough, a strategy session. Facilitation is needed when typical meeting pains appear: "we agreed
on nothing", one person does all the talking, 100 stickies and zero ideas, the team does not believe the decision, it was "handed down from above". If the decision is already made and
is not up for discussion, a workshop only imitates participation — do not run it.

## Core idea
**Facilitation** — simplifying and improving group discussion. The facilitator is responsible **for the process**, the team — **for the content**.
They are not a "boss" and not an expert; their main asset is neutrality. Four functions: guardian of the rules (the goal, the meeting rules agreed on
by the group), keeper of time (timeboxes, a buffer for transitions, stopping the unconstructive), a neutral chair (takes no side,
redirects questions back to the group), manager of group dynamics (every 10–15 minutes asks the participants to do something; I → We → All).

The facilitator does not voice their own opinion on the substance, does not push the solutions of opinion leaders, does not break agreed rules unilaterally
(if a rule gets in the way — it is brought back to the group), does not suppress conflict by force, but helps the positions be voiced and common ground found.

**Divergence → convergence** (the "diamond" principle): first we spread out into ideas without criticism, then we converge on a decision using criteria.
The "1-1-1" time budget: a third on expanding, a third on narrowing, a third on the decision and the next steps.

## Procedure

### 1. The brief (a compass) — before invitations
Context | Goal (measurable) | Image of the result ("what will be on the board/in the protocol") | Participants | Time and place | What is NOT the goal (constraints).
If the goal is "to discuss", the brief is not ready. For decision workshops, add: **which decision we are making, who makes it (the decision owner), by which
criteria, what data comes in as input**. Decision roles can be framed per [[M-decision-daci]].

### 2. Participants
By roles (knowledge of the user, implementation, business/strategy) and by knowledge (deep expertise, a fresh look, adjacent specialists). Up to ~8 active participants.
Better not to invite: the eternal critic, the direct manager of most attendees (the "boss's opinion" effect), a dominator, a silent "passenger", anyone with
personal negativity toward the topic. Hand over the data for discussion in advance (read-ahead: the brief, a digest of the research).

### 3. The scenario (a minute-by-minute plan)
A typical 60-minute skeleton: warm-up 5 → the goal and the image of the result 3 → individual divergence (silent) 10 → clustering 7 →
voting 10 → discussion of the top-2 (for example, by ICE) 10 → decision and To-Do 5 → buffer 5 → closing "what I take with me" 5.
Reserve a margin for transitions between tasks (about 20%). Formats: 60 min — one problem → one solution; 90 min — a classic
brainstorm + a choice; 120 min — add Ishikawa or the Six Hats. For online — the board and templates are prepared in advance, access rights are checked, the timer is visible to everyone.

### 4. Tools
| Tool | What for | How |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up / "trailer" | Switch the brain on; a provocative question on the topic | After the exercise, let people speak up, otherwise it feels like manipulation |
| HMW ("How might we…") | Turn a problem into a challenge | `How might we <desired state> for <segment>, avoiding <constraint>?`; the wording stays on the board |
| Silent brainstorm / brainwriting / 1-2-4-All | Divergence without domination | Ideas are written silently → pairs → fours → all |
| Ishikawa diagram | Root causes | The problem in the "head", 3 minutes on causes individually, clusters along the "bones" (processes, people, tools…), for each branch "one more why" up to 5 times, an owner for each bone |
| Six Hats (de Bono) | Change the angle of view | White — facts, red — feelings, black — risks, yellow — benefits, green — ideas, blue — process; a hard timer per hat |
| Dot voting | Convergence | `V = N/3` dots per person (N — the number of ideas); with 3 ideas do not use it — "thumb up/down" |
| "The royal choice" | A fast selection | The owner of the goal decides/votes with the golden right |
| Venn diagram | A multi-criteria choice | Define the criteria (the circles); an idea closer to the intersection wins |
| ICE | Comparing the top ideas | Impact × Confidence × Ease (scores set in advance) |

### 5. Difficult participants
The dominator: "Thanks, I'll note that down. Let's first hear the rest…"; a round-robin; limited time; a parking lot for topics. The silent one: an addressed
but gentle invitation; a message before the discussion; a 5-second pause after an open question. The skeptic: turn the criticism into a task for the group ("how
do we minimize this risk?"). Off-topic: the parking lot ("I'll write it down and we'll come back to it if we have time"). A conflict of experts: "I hear two positions; let's write out the facts
each of them stands on". Wording — through "I hear/I see" and a reference to the rules, never an evaluation of the person.

### 6. Output and control (protocol — publication — confirmation)
1. **The protocol** ≤1 page: the date, the goal, the participants, a link to the board, the top ideas, the actions (who / what / when / metric), the decisions made,
   the parking lot. The minimum viable output: the top ideas, To-Dos, owners and deadlines; otherwise the session was just chatter.
2. **Publication**: a page in a shared place, a link + a short digest to the team, tasks in the tracker with a link to the protocol.
3. **Confirmation**: the owner confirms the task; a reminder if there is no reaction; a check of execution in 2–4 weeks.
4. The results go into the UX backlog if there is one ([[M-ux-backlog]]); score the ideas by impact and effort while the team is "hot".

### 7. Ready-made formats
- **Design Sprint** (5 days): the map and focus → sketches → the decision and the storyboard → a prototype → a test on ~5 users. A concept and validation are needed; the team drops out of its normal work.
- **Lightning Decision Jam** (60–90 min): pains → voting → HMW → silent ideas → voting → Impact×Effort → an action canvas. Many pains, little time, a quick "quick win" is needed.
- **A Value Proposition Canvas session** (about 3 h): the client's jobs → pains/gains → fit → prioritization. The team argues about value ([[M-jtbd]]).
- The choice by rule: time <1 day → LDJ; a broad scope, a concept and validation needed → Design Sprint; doubt about value → the VP canvas.

## Template / checklist

**Workshop brief:** Context | Decision/goal (measurable) | Image of the result | Input data | Participants and roles | Time/place/format | Not the goal |
Choice criteria | Decision owner | Risks (a dominator, a conflict of interests).

**Scenario:** `Time | Block | Task (the verbatim wording) | Method | Output | Timekeeper`.

**Action-item canvas:** `What | Why | Owner | Deadline | KPI | Status`.

**Session quality metrics (guidelines from the course notes, calibrate them):** the share of speech of the dominant participant <~35%; the share of ideas from silent
generation ≥~40%; actions per participant ≥0.5; the lag of protocol publication ≤15 min; the share of epics/tasks created from the actions — 100%; the share
closed within 30 days — a guideline of 70%.

**Before closing:** the board is saved; the tasks are created; the protocol is published; the parking lot is transferred; a bot/reminder for the check-in is set.

## Typical mistakes
| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No brief and no measurable goal | A discussion without a result | A brief before invitations |
| The facilitator promotes their own solution | Neutrality and trust are lost | Return the content to the group |
| No timeboxes or buffer for transitions | The session sprawls | A timer on every block |
| Divergence and criticism at the same time | Ideas get blocked | Criticism only at convergence |
| Dot voting with 2–3 options | Scattered votes | "Thumb up/down" or the royal choice |
| The decision was "handed down", the workshop is for show | Distrust | State honestly what is up for discussion and what is not |
| No protocol and no owners | A file graveyard | A protocol ≤ 1 page, tasks in the tracker |
| Too many participants (>8 active) | The tempo drops | Split into groups, reduce the roster |
| Online without a prepared board or engagement | Passivity | Activation every 10–15 minutes |

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which exact decision should appear, and who is its owner? If it is "just discuss" — the format is a different one (interviews with experts).
- Is there data on the input (research, metrics)? Without it the ideas will remain opinions.
- Is open criticism acceptable in the team (psychological safety)? Otherwise — written formats and anonymous voting.
- What is the deadline, how many people, and can they be occupied for a day/week?
- What happens to the outcome: will it reach the backlog, the roadmap, the budget?
- An agent cannot neutrally chair a live group, but it can prepare the brief, the scenario, the templates and the protocol.
