# Delivery (EN summary)

**When:** when strategy or roadmap commits to dates and effects: to assess realism, understand which roles and stages stand between "hypothesis validated" and "value metric moved in production", and not promise more than the process can bear. Short card — no Scrum/DevOps detail.

**Core:** Delivery is the process of building and shipping the product: it starts when Discovery has produced a feature scope and ends when the value metric "moves" in production. Discovery answers "why and what", Delivery answers "how, when, with what risk" and verifies the "why" actually happened; the whole team participates, including the PM; business analysis is the Discovery/Delivery seam. Stages and rough timelines: prioritization and scope (MoSCoW, RICE) 2 days–1 week; documentation and design (user stories + acceptance criteria) 2 days–1.5 months; development and testing from 1 week; release ~1 day. Prioritization may cut 30–70% of Discovery scope. **MoSCoW**: Must ("would the business collapse without it in the next ~6 weeks?"), Should, Could, Won't; rule of thumb — Must ≤ ~40% of what the team physically builds in 1–2 sprints. **RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort**; Effort is estimated only by dev and QA, not the PM; the cut-line: after it nothing is added without recalculating value; a new "urgent" idea goes through RICE and Cost of Delay.

Documentation: user story ("as [who], I want [action], so that [result]") + acceptance criteria (Given-When-Then), each checkable by a yes/no answer; exceptions and corner cases described; **the analytics event is part of the specification** — otherwise the feature is "blind". Ready for Dev: stories and criteria final, mockups, API specs, events and dashboard, PM sign-off. Development: sprints (Scrum, 1–2 weeks) or flow (Kanban, releases every 3–5 days); dependency chain: no business requirements → no test cases → no development → no release; complex products coordinate releases with adjacent teams and release windows. Release rules: no big release on Friday; everything on prod instrumented with events and covered by alerts; separate releases of large features so effects don't mix; risky features only behind A/B (and non-risky ones too, to measure effect); verify "not all bugs are critical" yourself. **Feature flags, three types**: release flag (hides unfinished work, TTL 1–2 sprints or it becomes tech debt), experiment flag (traffic split for A/B, lives to test end), operational flag ("red button" — instantly disable part of the system); the flag key lives in a config table and flips without a code revert. **Canary rollout**: 5% → 20% → 100% at ~30-minute steps; auto-rollback on latency/5xx alerts; 100% during daytime, not Friday evening; 24 hours without red alerts → flag on for everyone; with a flag, rollback is a button, not an overnight git rollback. When A/B vs ship now (cost of a minute vs cost of an error): a minor UX tweak deserves an A/B; a critical bugfix ships immediately; pricing changes are verified longer (LTV and client reaction) — the PM decides.

Alerts speak in actions, not red lamps: every alert has an owner field (auto-escalation on vacation); an ownerless alert closes after 30 days; grouped by type (latency, financial, UX); false positives muted. First 24 hours in four segments: 30 minutes — smoke test of main scenarios; 1 hour — fast funnel cut, PM watches GMV per minute, anomaly → flag OFF; up to 6 hours — automatic alerts work, on-call answers only "rollback or fix?"; by 24 hours — a 5-minute micro-retro. Watch three signal groups: technical (latency, CPU, 5xx), product (conversion, clicks), business (GMV, NPS). "Release isn't done until numbers sing": value proven → scale; doubtful → iterate; negative → rollback and a new hypothesis. Post-release review with action items; root cause via "five whys" to the systemic error, not the culprit. **Delivery health metrics (DORA)**: Lead Time for Changes, Deployment Frequency, Change Failure Rate, MTTR; business layer: On-Time Delivery %, Escaped Defect %, Cost of Delay Saved; collected automatically; fake improvements — splitting releases for frequency, rollbacks without tags, bugs hidden under flags. A metric in the yellow zone for two sprints running → 10% of next sprint's capacity goes to fixing the process; quarterly delivery planning with ~10% of velocity permanently reserved for process tasks; stop optimizing if CFR grows faster than Lead Time falls; sell infrastructure to the CFO via ROI. **DHI** aggregate: on-time releases, escaped defects, alert response speed, clean regressions; red at MBR → cluster "red card" and a freeze on non-market experiments. Multiple teams: Release Train (predictable for regulators/CFO; "missed" = a week's wait) vs pure continuous (instant value; one conflict wrecks the portfolio) vs platform hybrid (core rides the train, frontends and ML services stay continuous); rituals: shared release calendar, dependency matrix as a "metro map", Reg-Gate before the train, release-readiness meeting (R²M) two days ahead — the PM says Go/No-Go. Security "shifts left": security requirements in the spec next to design checklists (data masking in AC, negative test "unauthenticated request → 401"), CI automation (secret scanning, dependency analysis, static analysis), permanent security backlog at 10–15% capacity.

**How:** 1) take the prioritized hypothesis and MVP/MLP scope; separate Must; strike whatever doesn't affect the measurable metric; 2) get the estimate from dev and the analyst (Effort, dates, dependencies, blockers) — never the PM's own; no estimate → write a range marked "forecast, not confirmed by the team"; 3) map against capacity (sprints, holidays, release windows, feature freeze, A/B sampling time) and cross-team dependencies (legal, security, adjacent APIs) — A/B needs sampling time beyond development; 4) confirm the scope includes analytics events and dashboard, A/B design, approvals (legal, security, support), rollback criteria, flag launch; 5) set post-release decision criteria: success threshold, counter-metrics, observation window (e.g. first 24h for tech, weeks for A/B), what counts as rollback; 6) record in the roadmap: stage, owner, dependency, date, milestone decision, estimates marked as forecasts; 7) after release, reconcile fact vs plan (dates, effect, counter-metrics) and feed actions into the process.

**Pitfalls:** shipping a "blind" feature without analytics events; Friday evening launch without alerts; stuffing "nice-to-haves" into the sprint; Must = everything; floating acceptance criteria; the PM self-estimating effort; a roadmap without estimates and release windows; mixing releases of big features so effects can't be separated; treating Delivery as not the PM's job and stopping at deploy instead of "numbers singing"; no time reserved for A/B, approvals, support and rollback; release frequency as a goal instead of value (On-Time, Escaped Defects); an alert without an owner; a release flag living forever; a metric in the yellow zone two sprints running with no process task in the backlog; each team shipping green releases while the summed effect hits latency and GMV — no shared calendar or dependency map.
