# Lean Canvas (EN summary)

**When:** a new product/direction or a model rebuild: capture the business's hypotheses on one screen — problem, customer, value, channels, monetization, costs, metrics — and know which cell is the riskiest. In the agent it is filled at intake/discovery/strategy from the claims registry.

**Core:** nine blocks; the fill order matters. 1 **Problem** — 1–3 real pains, each with existing alternatives. 2 **Customer segments** — early adopters, not "everyone". 3 **UVP** — one clear value message that distinguishes from alternatives. 4 **Solution** — the 3 most important capabilities (after the problem!). 5 **Channels** — how to reach: scalable and not. 6 **Revenue streams** — monetization model, price, value metric. 7 **Cost structure** — fixed/variable, what dominates. 8 **Key metrics** — the activity funnel (AARRR slice): what proves progress. 9 **Unfair advantage** — data, network, contracts, brand. Link to unit economics: cells 6–8 are the inputs of the unit-economics calculation; a Lean Canvas without the calculation is a declaration, not a hypothesis.

**How:** (1) fill cells 1–2 from the situation/discovery documents — every pain and segment backed by C###/S### claims; (2) UVP and solution (3–4) from the chosen option; run the promise ↔ proof check; (3) channels/revenue/costs (5–7) with numbers from the calculations (unit economics workbook); (4) metrics (8) aligned with the validation plan — one headline metric + a guardrail; (5) mark the **riskiest cell** and add its test to the plan — that test is the next experiment.

**Pitfalls:** "everyone" as the segment; a UVP made of adjectives ("convenient, reliable"); solution before problem; channels as "social media" without a cost per contact; vanity metrics (downloads instead of repeat purchases); "we do X better" as the advantage — that's not a moat; look for structure (data, network, contracts).
