# Formulating a USP and value proposition (Job Story + Value Proposition Canvas)

## When to apply
You need to answer: "how does our product differ so that people choose us specifically", "which promise to put into communication and into the MVP", "which feature counts as a competitive advantage and which one is hygiene". This card comes after knowledge about customers and competitors and before communication and development.

## Essence
A **USP (unique selling proposition)** — a characteristic or feature of the product that distinguishes it from others and that is *hard or impossible to copy*. If a competitor repeats it within a week, it is not a USP.

**What is copied easily and what is hard (the grocery delivery example from the course).** Easy: a beautiful website, a convenient app (the development is expensive, but with resources it is repeated), the tone of communication, promotions and discounts, gamification, a set of well-known brands. Hard, expensive, slow to repeat: delivery in 15 minutes (logistics), delivery from pharmacies, a special click-and-collect delivery model, a private label. The criterion: "hard, expensive, time-consuming" for competitors.

**Starting questions for developing a USP:** for whom am I making the product? for what (which customer job am I solving)? Look not at people and demographics but at their *needs* — the jobs they want to get done.

**Job Story:** "When [context/situation], I want/can/do [action], so that [motivation/result]". Hypotheses are formulated from the consumer, not from a product function (based on the Jobs-to-be-Done idea, see [[M-jtbd]]).

**Value Proposition Canvas (VPC, Osterwalder).** Two halves:
| Customer Profile | Value Map |
|---|---|
| **Jobs-to-be-done** — what the customer wants to accomplish (why they decided to use the product, which job they perform, which personal and emotional needs exist) | **Products & Services** — which services and functions close the job |
| **Gains** — what would make them happy; results exceeding expectations; social, emotional, financial benefits | **Gain Creators** — how to help achieve success, exceed expectations, simplify life |
| **Pains** — what irritates and worries; what prevents the job; what they will encounter; what would make them stop using the product | **Pain Relievers** — how to ease the pain, remove worry, eliminate barriers (and which barriers cannot be removed — how to make them less irritating) |
**The USP lives at the intersection** of the unique, hard-to-copy part of the Value Map and the customer's critical pain/gain. An important condition: honestly ask whether we remove the pain or merely reduce it, so as not to promise the unrealistic.

**Sources for a USP:** desk analysis and benchmarking (white space, the market standard), trends, in-depth interviews (pains, barriers, language), segmentation, brand tracking (an image gap versus a competitor). Desk data yields a candidate; verifying the promise with the target audience is mandatory.

**Strength of evidence.** A USP candidate derived from a benchmark and interviews is a hypothesis about *value and difference*. A headline test (clicks on an A/B listing) measures interest in the promise, not demand, not willingness to pay and not retention. Confirmation of value — behavior and money (pre-order, payment, retention) and the segment's repeatability of choice.

## Procedure
1. **Define the segment and the scenario.** Take the key segment ([[M-audience-research]]) and a concrete situation (e.g., buying pet supplies). Write from the buyer's perspective, not the seller's.
2. **Collect Job Stories** (3–7) based on interviews, analytics and support: context — action — motivation.
3. **Fill in the left half of the VPC:** for each job — Pains and Gains with an indication of how important they are (frequency × severity). Do not invent: mark "fact" (from interviews/data) and "hypothesis".
4. **Fill in the right half of the VPC:** products/services, pain relievers, gain creators; for each item — which specific pain/gain it closes.
5. **Check against competitors:** who already offers such a pain reliever (the competitor matrix, [[M-competitors]]); what is the market standard (hygiene) and what is white space.
6. **Filter candidates by four questions** (from the course notes): *Valuable?* — the customer would change a habit for it; *Rare?* — competitors do not promise the same; *Hard to copy?* — it won't be repeated within a week; *Clear?* — understandable on first reading. Any "no" — refine the advantage or the wording.
7. **Prioritize (Value × Effort):** estimate the value (how much pain it removes × the number of people with the pain) and the costs (complexity, time, money, partners). Quadrants: high value and low cost — quick wins (into the MVP); high value and high cost — the main USP "bet" (requires investment and a plan B in the form of quick wins); low value and low cost — by leftover capacity; low value and high cost — postpone. One USP candidate per focus.
8. **Formulate the USP statement** (a short phrase, ≈15 words): "For [a specific segment] we are the only ones who [solve a specific pain] [in a unique way]". One segment, one pain, a marker of uniqueness, no vague words ("care", "convenient").
9. **Verify the promise:** the desk analysis and benchmark are already done; next — concept interviews, a headline test (an A/B listing: identical creative and targeting, only the headline changes; the metric — CTR or a click-through; n, the effect threshold and the significance level are set in advance, [[M-quant-methods]]), then an MVP/pilot ([[M-mvp-pilots]]) with behavioral metrics.
10. **Embed into communication and metrics:** the landing page, sales, a feature-scope criterion ([[M-product-communication]]); tie it to a product metric and OKRs.

## Template / checklist

**Job Story:** When ______, I want ______, so that ______.

**VPC (one job):**
| Block | Content | Fact / hypothesis | Importance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | | | |
| Pains | | | |
| Gains | | | |
| Products & Services | | | |
| Pain relievers | | | |
| Gain creators | | | |

**USP candidate filter:**
| Candidate | Valuable | Rare | Hard to copy | Clear | Value (1–5) | Effort (1–5) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**A course example (pet supplies, a classified-ads service):** the job — "when the pet food ran out, quickly buy a suitable one"; pains — catalog chaos, I forget to buy/vaccinate; gains — "I open it and immediately see products for my pet". Candidates: a pet profile with an auto-catalog (hard to copy: an attribute schema and ML matching are needed), reminders (technologically simple, often present at competitors), an SOS button with a search for a veterinary clinic (hard: a partner network and SLAs). Status — a hypothesis until tested on users.

**Examples of "before → after" wordings:** "lots of food and toys, plus delivery too" → "We'll pick the right food for your breed and remind you to buy it". Bad: "a convenient app", "we care about pets" (not measurable and easily copied).

**Checklist:** one segment; one pain; a wording of ≤≈15 words; a marker of uniqueness present; the four questions passed; a Value/Effort estimate present; one "bet" and the quick wins chosen; a verification method and a success metric defined; risks recorded (dependence on partners, regulation).

## Typical mistakes
- Presenting hygiene as a USP ("a convenient interface", "quality service", discounts).
- Formulating from the product ("we have smart matching") rather than from the customer's job and pain.
- One USP "for everyone": a blurred segment; two pains in one phrase.
- Writing a USP without competitor analysis: the "uniqueness" may be a market standard.
- Taking interest in a headline (a click) as confirmation of demand; running a test without pre-set thresholds and controls.
- Changing not only the text but also the creative/audience in the A/B listing.
- Not backing the promise with a real ability to deliver (logistics, partners): communication runs ahead of the product.
- Treating the USP as eternal: the market and competitors move; return to verification.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which pain of the segment is genuinely frequent and severe (per data), and which one only per our assumptions?
- What will competitors be able to repeat, and in what time? Do we have an inimitable resource (data, partners, logistics, brand)?
- What happens if they copy it: do we have a second line of defense?
- Can we fulfill the promise at scale and on time?
- How to measure the success of the promise (an acquisition metric, conversion, retention, loyalty scores — [[M-loyalty-metrics]])?
- Does the USP contradict the value core of the chosen segment (compare with the segment matrix)?
