# Formulating a USP and value proposition (Job Story + Value Proposition Canvas)

## When to apply
You need to answer: "how does our product differ so that customers choose us specifically", "which promise goes
into communication and into the MVP", "which feature counts as a competitive advantage and which one is
hygiene". This card comes after knowledge about customers and competitors and before communication and
development.

## Core idea
**USP (unique selling proposition)** — a characteristic or feature of the product that differentiates it from
others and that is *hard or impossible to copy*. If a competitor repeats it in a week, it is not a USP.

**What copies easily and what copies with difficulty (the grocery delivery example from the course).** Easy: a
beautiful website, a convenient app (even though development is expensive, it is repeatable with resources), the
tone of communication, promotions and discounts, gamification, a set of well-known brands. Hard, expensive and
slow to repeat: 15-minute delivery (logistics), pharmacy delivery, a special "one-click" delivery model, a
private label. Criterion: "difficult, expensive, time-consuming" for competitors.

**Starting questions for developing a USP:** for whom am I building the product? for what (which client job am
I solving)? We look not at people and demographics but at their *needs* — the jobs they want to get done.

**Job Story:** "When [context/situation], I want/can/do [action], so that [motivation/outcome]". Hypotheses are
formulated from the consumer, not from a product feature (at its core is the Jobs-to-be-Done idea, see
[[M-jtbd]]).

**Value Proposition Canvas (VPC, Osterwalder).** Two halves:
| Customer Profile | Value Map |
|---|---|
| **Jobs-to-be-done** — what the client wants to accomplish (why they decided to use the product, which job they perform, which personal and emotional needs they have) | **Products & Services** — which services and features close the job |
| **Gains** — what would make them happy; results that exceed expectations; social, emotional, financial benefits | **Gain Creators** — how to help them succeed, exceed expectations, simplify their life |
| **Pains** — what annoys and worries them; what prevents the job from being done; what they will run into; what would make them stop using the product | **Pain Relievers** — how to ease the pain, remove the anxiety, eliminate barriers (and which barriers cannot be removed — how to make them less irritating) |
**The USP lives at the junction** of the unique, hard-to-copy part of the Value Map and the client's critical
pain/benefit. An important condition: honestly ask whether we remove the pain or merely reduce it, so as not to
promise the unrealistic.

**Sources for a USP:** desk analysis and benchmark (white space, market standard), trends, in-depth interviews
(pains, barriers, language), segmentation, brand tracking (an image gap versus a competitor). Desk data gives
you a candidate; validating the promise with the target audience is mandatory.

**Evidence strength.** A USP candidate derived from a benchmark and interviews is a hypothesis about *value and
differentiation*. A headline test (clicks on an A/B listing) measures interest in the promise, not demand, not
willingness to pay and not retention. Confirmation of value is behavior and money (pre-order, payment,
retention) and the repeatability of the choice across the segment.

## Procedure
1. **Define the segment and the scenario.** Take the key segment ([[M-audience-research]]) and a concrete
   situation (for example, buying pet supplies). Write from the buyer's side, not the seller's.
2. **Collect Job Stories** (3–7) based on interviews, analytics and support: context — action — motivation.
3. **Fill in the left side of the VPC:** for each job — Pains and Gains with an estimate of how important each
   is (frequency × severity). Do not invent: mark each item "fact" (from interviews/data) or "hypothesis".
4. **Fill in the right side of the VPC:** products/services, pain relievers, gain creators; for each item —
   which specific pain/gain it closes.
5. **Check against competitors:** who already offers such a pain reliever (competitor matrix,
   [[M-competitors]]); what is the market standard (hygiene), what is white space.
6. **Filter the candidates by four questions** (from the course notes): *Valuable?* — the client would change
   a habit for it; *Rare?* — competitors do not promise the same; *Hard to copy?* — it will not be repeated in
   a week; *Clear?* — it is understood at first reading. Any "no" means reworking the advantage or the wording.
7. **Prioritize (Value × Effort):** estimate the value (how much pain it removes × the number of people with
   that pain) and the costs (complexity, time, money, partners). Quadrants: high value and low costs — quick
   wins (into the MVP); high value and high costs — the main USP "bet" (requires investment and a plan B in the
   form of quick wins); low value and low costs — do them with residual effort; low value and high costs —
   postpone. One USP candidate in focus.
8. **Write the USP statement** (a short phrase, ≈15 words): "For [a concrete segment] we are the only ones who
   [solve a concrete pain] [in a unique way]". One segment, one pain, a marker of uniqueness, no vague words
   ("care", "convenient").
9. **Validate the promise:** desk research and benchmark are already done; next — concept interviews, a
   headline test (A/B listing: the same creative and targeting, only the headline changes; the metric is CTR
   or click-through; n, effect threshold and significance level are set in advance, [[M-quant-methods]]), then
   an MVP/pilot ([[M-mvp-pilots]]) with behavioral metrics.
10. **Embed it into communication and metrics:** the landing page, sales, a criterion for feature scope
    ([[M-product-communication]]); tie it to the product metric and OKRs.

## Template / checklist

**Job Story:** When ______, I want ______, so that ______.

**VPC (one job):**
| Block | Content | Fact / hypothesis | Importance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | | | |
| Pains | | | |
| Gains | | | |
| Products & Services | | | |
| Pain relievers | | | |
| Gain creators | | | |

**USP candidate filter:**
| Candidate | Valuable | Rare | Hard to copy | Clear | Value (1–5) | Effort (1–5) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**Example from the course (pet supplies, a classifieds board):** the job — "when the pet food has run out, buy
a suitable one quickly"; pains — a chaotic catalog, forgetting to buy food / get vaccinations; gains — "open it
and immediately see the products for my pet". Candidates: a pet profile with an auto-generated catalog (hard to
copy: you need an attribute schema and ML matching), reminders (technologically simple, competitors often have
them), an SOS button with a search for a veterinary clinic (hard: a partner network and SLAs). Status —
hypothesis until validated with users.

**Examples of "before → after" wording:** "lots of food and toys, and delivery too" → "We will pick the food for
your breed and remind you to buy it". Bad: "a convenient app", "we care about pets" (not measurable and easy to
copy).

**Checklist:** one segment; one pain; the wording is ≤≈15 words; there is a marker of uniqueness; the four
questions have been passed; there is a Value/Effort estimate; one "bet" and the quick wins have been chosen; a
validation method and a success metric are defined; risks are written down (partner dependencies, regulation).

## Typical mistakes
- Presenting hygiene as a USP ("a convenient interface", "quality service", discounts).
- Formulating from the product ("we have smart matching") instead of from the client's job and pain.
- One USP "for everyone": a blurred segment; two pains in one phrase.
- Writing a USP without competitor analysis: the "uniqueness" may be the market standard.
- Taking interest in a headline (a click) as confirmation of demand; running a test without thresholds and
  controls set in advance.
- Changing not only the headline but also the creative/audience in the A/B listing.
- Not backing the promise with a real ability to deliver (logistics, partners): communication runs ahead of the
  product.
- Treating a USP as eternal: the market and competitors move; return to validation.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which pain of the segment is genuinely frequent and severe (according to data), and which one only according
  to our assumptions?
- What can competitors repeat, and in how long? Do we have an inimitable resource (data, partners, logistics,
  brand)?
- What happens if they copy it: do we have a second line of defense?
- Can we fulfill the promise at scale and on time?
- How do we measure the success of the promise (acquisition metric, conversion, retention, loyalty scores —
  [[M-loyalty-metrics]])?
- Does the USP contradict the core values of the chosen segment (compare with the segment matrix)?
