# Lifecycle: each stage has its own game and its own metrics

## When to apply
Strategy, channels, and metrics are chosen by the product stage; a stage error = playing someone else's game (scaling before PMF, optimizing costs during growth, and the like). Stage diagnostics is a mandatory step of strategy.

## Essence
- **Introduction (0→1)**: the goal — test the value and find the first repeat users. Focus: PMF signals, qualitative research, minimal marketing. Danger — premature scaling.
- **Growth**: the goal — capture the pace while preserving retention. Focus: channels and funnels, conversions, unit economics turning positive per cohort. Danger — growth with breaking unit economics.
- **Maturity**: the goal — defense and profit. Focus: retention/ARPU, segmentation, upsells, cost efficiency, sideline innovation (v2, adjacencies). Danger — complacency and market disaggregation (the start of decline).
- **Decline**: the goal — managed value harvesting. Focus: cuts, "milking" the cash cows, user migration, or product renewal. Danger — a late pivot.
- **Stage diagnostics by metrics**, not by feel: the pace of new users vs the retention of old ones, market share, channel elasticity.

## How to do it
1. Determine the stage from data (the dynamics of new/old users, retention, market share).
2. Choose the stage's target game and its stage metrics (see the tables in the `M-pmf-scaling` and `M-product-metrics` cards).
3. Check the initiative portfolio: does every initiative match the stage? Move the off-stage ones over.
4. Set stage-transition indicators → a scheduled strategy review (in 06/monitoring).

## Pitfalls
- "We are in growth" without retention data — scaling breaks the product.
- Treating maturity with discounts instead of innovation.
- Not admitting the decline (no value-harvesting plan).
