# The PMF survey: measure product/market fit with one question

## When to apply
The product has been live for several months and has active users — you need to know whether PMF exists and on which segment, before investing in growth.

## Essence
- The key question for active users: **"How would you feel if the product were no longer available?"** — "very disappointed" / "somewhat" / "not very" / "no longer applies".
- Sean Ellis's benchmark: **≥40% "very disappointed"** — a precursor of PMF. Below 40% — the product is not yet obligated to scale: product/segment first.
- **The point is not the average but segmentation**: find WHO these 40% are (by role, scenario, frequency, acquisition channel) and strengthen the product/marketing specifically for them (the Superhuman method: build the "high scorer" profile, narrow the positioning, work only with that segment until the next measurement).
- Supplement with open questions: "Which type of people benefits the most?", "What is the main benefit you get?" — positioning wording is taken from the answers, not invented.
- The survey does not replace retention: a PMF score without repeat usage is a false signal.

## How to do it
1. Select active users (e.g., ≥N sessions per month); run the survey inside the product, not on a cold list.
2. Collect ≥40–80 responses — a segment-forming volume; compute the share of "very disappointed" and the breakdowns.
3. Extract the segment with the highest score → interview them → phrase the value in their words.
4. Decision: score ≥40% + retention → growth; below — discovery (the opportunity tree) and/or a segment change.

## Pitfalls
- Surveying everyone indiscriminately (including "tried it once") — the score drops without a diagnosis.
- Believing the average percentage without breakdowns — the main signal (the segment) is lost.
- Measuring once and forever: PMF is a movement; repeat the measurement after significant changes.
