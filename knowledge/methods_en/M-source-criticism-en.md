# Source Criticism and Analytical Reports (EN summary)

**When:** any external figure, forecast or claim feeds a conclusion, model or recommendation: a consulting/vendor/government report, a benchmark, a market size, a trend, an LLM answer, a user's words about their own metrics. Rule: **a report is a hypothesis, not a fact**, until verified and triangulated.

**Core:** evidence grades — **A**: primary source or project data with disclosed methodology; confirmed by ≥2 independent sources (triangulation); within age tolerance; comparable by segment, period, definition → High confidence, a decision backbone. **B**: authoritative secondary source with methodology (Tier-1 consulting, a regulator, a report with disclosed method); partially confirmed or with comparability caveats → Medium, use with a range and a caveat. **C**: single source, retelling, no methodology, sponsor interest, age at the tolerance edge, unchecked expert estimate → Low, hypothesis/orientation only; sensitivity analysis and a verification plan mandatory. **D**: unverifiable or red flags present (no primary source or definitions, "internal analysis", too-precise round numbers without an interval), or an agent assumption without a source → Very low, not a decision basis; only an explicitly labeled assumption. **Weak-link rule:** a conclusion's grade is never higher than its weakest critical input; if the decision is sensitive to a C/D-grade input — verify or collect data first. Source types and their first checks: government/international statistics (data lag 6–12 months, political optics); IT-company reports (no methodology; product changes create fake "trends"; interest in favorable reading); consulting (interpretation layer, hidden sponsor; niche firms weaker than Tier-1); solo analysts (cherry-picking, conflicts of interest); bank research and regulators (financial optics); public filings (audited finances but may hide the inconvenient, own metric definitions); VC reports (fast market signals; narrow venture focus, fund interest). The five-level check: (1) purpose — which decision this serves, horizon, geography; (2) type and author — set the table-driven checks; (3) source integrity — author competence, single or serial report, sponsor and beneficiary, conflicts of interest; (4) method integrity — definitions of terms ("user", "active", "AI market"); data type — survey (sampling bias, social desirability), statistical model (assumptions, extrapolation), transactions/telemetry (incompleteness), desk research (secondary), mixed (best); sample geography/industries/roles/weights/collection period; (5) claim integrity — pick 3–7 key claims, confirm each with a second independent source; sanity checks: shares ≤100% and parts summing to the whole; currency and year match; order of magnitude; implied growth `CAGR = (V_end / V_start)^(1/n) − 1` realistic; "2026 report" ≠ "2026 data" — check the collection date. Then assign the grade with age rules: fast-moving values (CAC, CPI, retention) ≤24 months; stable ones (margin, long-term LTV) 3–5 years; market-sizing data ≤2–3 years. **Triangulation:** 2–3 independent sources of different types (state statistics + report + primary data); for benchmarks — 3, spread >30% demands caution; dependent retellings of one report count as one source. Red flags: no market/category definition; numbers without a primary source; too-precise forecasts ("+14.7%") without an interval; a vendor report posing as independent; no geography or respondent profile; "internal analysis" as the sole source; updates without a methodology-change note. Green flags: methodology/appendix, glossary, limitations section, update history with described edits, weighting and data cleaning described, scenario-based forecasts with ranges and drivers, disclosed sponsorship. Analyst's cognitive traps: confirmation bias, overconfidence, survivorship bias, authority bias, anchoring and curse of knowledge when reviewing one's own work.

**How:** run the five-level check per report/source → assign grade A–D with confidence → keep the **Evidence Log** (one row per claim): ID (E-001…); claim (value, unit, period, segment/geography, definition); source (publisher, title, year, page/section, URL; primary or secondary); method and sponsor; dates (publication · data collection · when read); triangulation (IDs of confirming records or "none"); grade A–D + confidence + why; usage (where in the conclusion/model; result sensitivity); next check (what and who before the decision). Special cases: an **agent's own calculation** — record the formula, input IDs, and grade by the weak-link rule; **a user's words about their metrics** — "user data", graded by verifiability (export, dashboard); **an LLM answer without a source** — grade D; never invent a link, state that a search is needed. In the output text, the citation sits directly at the number (hyperlink or log ID) with the year and grade — not as an end-of-document list. Keep a key-claims table: claim · status (confirmed/not) · confirmation · grade · note. Checklist: source verified; method understood; key figures triangulated; no obvious conflict of interest; limitations disclosed; definitions match ours.

**Pitfalls:** taking the headline figure without reading the methodology; comparing numbers from reports with different market definitions; counting three retellings of one report as three sources; confusing the collection date with the publication date; treating a vendor report as neutral and precise forecasts without intervals; writing an assumption into a model without labeling it as one; leaving an A-grade conclusion standing on C-grade inputs.
