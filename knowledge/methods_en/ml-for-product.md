# ML for Product Managers (EN summary)

**When:** the team says "we need ML here" — price hints, antifraud, recommendations, demand forecast; deciding whether ML is justified, turning the idea into a testable ML hypothesis, agreeing model-quality and business metrics, validating the effect in the product — including ML-enhanced A/B testing.

**Core — where ML is justified (and where not):** when the pattern is complex/multidimensional and not expressible as rules: search and ranking (marketplaces and content services don't scale without ML); recommendations (personal blocks, feeds; grow engagement and conversion); risk classification (antifraud, content moderation, scoring — Recall is critical); regression/forecast (price, time-to-sale, demand, load); clustering (segments, hypothesis generation); pricing and autobidding. ML is NOT needed when: no data exists; the dependency is simple and captured by rules/SQL; a human decides on qualitative data; model accuracy doesn't pay for complexity and risks. Rule: a simple model/rule first as baseline; ML when it demonstrably beats the baseline on the business metric. Task types through a PM's eyes: regression — predict a number (price, time-to-sale, demand; MAE, MAPE, RMSE); classification — predict a probability/class (fraud, violations, "will buy / won't"; Precision, Recall, ROC-AUC); clustering — find segments (silhouette + interpretability); ranking — order objects (search, recommendations; NDCG, MRR, Recall@k, Precision@k). Precision = share of correct among those the model flagged; Recall = share found among all real cases (fraud/moderation bar typically ≥90%); ROC-AUC = class-separation quality; "precision for a regression" signals a wrong problem statement.

**7-step ML hypothesis template:** (1) problem/business context with numbers ("in category X sellers underprice by 20–30%, hurting GMV and margin"); (2) narrow ML task carved out of the problem (classification "good/bad price", regression "optimal price", ranking "which price to show"); (3) data — historical examples, labels, behavioral data, and availability at inference time; no data → dead hypothesis; (4) **decision point** — where the model's output enters the product (at listing creation, in the feed, at payment); ML without an application point is meaningless; (5) business metric — the hypothesis KPI (GMV, CR, retention, time-to-sale, fraud share); (6) model-quality metrics per task type; (7) success criteria on all three axes (business e.g. +4% GMV in category; model e.g. MAE < 12%; UX e.g. ≤10% of users reject the hint). Example business↔model pairs: moderation — risk down/trust up ↔ Recall > 90%; time-to-sale forecast — sellability CR up ↔ MAPE < 20%; recommendations — GMV up ↔ NDCG > 0.25; plan feasibility checks (is AUC > 0.75 / MAPE < 20% attainable on this data?).

Model metrics are a development tool; the business metric is the success criterion — AUC 0.95 with zero CR impact is a useless model. Classification threshold follows the product's error cost (antifraud sacrifices Precision for Recall; price hints the reverse). Offline metrics ≠ online effect: ship only after a live A/B; offline/online divergence is a normal research trigger, not a reason to "tune the threshold". Ranking needs NDCG/MRR/Recall@k — plain CTR is insufficient. **ML in A/B tests**: **CUPED / ML-CUPED** variance reduction — covariate X = the metric's pre-test value (classic) or an ML prediction of "what the metric would have been without the feature" (uses more data and dynamics); adjusted metric = Y − θ·(X − E[X]), θ estimated on the pre-period; effect up to 2–3× faster tests (21 days → 7–10). Propensity score / stratification — reweighting heterogeneous groups when the split came out unequal on "strong" users. **Uplift modeling** — when the average effect ≈ 0 but segments differ: predict individual effect (T/S/X-learners, causal forests, uplift trees); roll out to the strongest segment. Complex metrics (search, recommendations) tested on NDCG/MRR/Recall@k with ML variance reduction. Risks: untested covariate bias, a covariate using the post-test period (= leakage), applying without business understanding.

**How:** 1) check ML necessity: is there data, is the task solvable by rules, does the effect pay for the complexity; 2) fill the 7-step template; 3) fix a simple baseline to compare against; 4) offline training and validation: correct per-task metrics, leakage check (nothing "from the future"), Precision/Recall balance by error cost; 5) design the online check: A/B with CUPED/ML-CUPED under high variance, uplift analysis when the average effect ≈ 0, guardrail metrics (conversion, complaints, latency) and thresholds; 6) decide on the business metric (not the model metric); fix drift monitoring and the retraining plan; 7) write learnings to the knowledge base: what worked, which data/features were decisive.

**Pitfalls:** "improve search/recommendations" instead of a narrow task with a metric — not a hypothesis; no business metric (model accuracy as the goal, AUC for AUC's sake); no data — or data that won't exist at inference time — yet the hypothesis goes to development; leakage (features containing target information or "future") inflating offline metrics; blind trust in the model — no baseline, no sanity check, no business logic in validation; wrong error balance (optimizing Precision for fraud loses violators; or blocking normal users); a CUPED covariate taken from the post-test period; unmonitored data drift — the model quietly degrades after launch; black-box decisions touching users directly (moderation, prices, scoring) without explanations and sampled audits — trust and regulatory risk; ML for ML's sake where a rule or SQL is cheaper, clearer and more reliable.
