# Auctions and Marketplaces (EN summary)

**When:** allocating a scarce resource (ad slots, search positions, leads) among many buyers; deciding whether an auction is needed at all or a fixed price suffices; choosing the auction type; judging whether the auction is healthy and revenue is "toxic"; understanding marketplace economics — liquidity, take rate, balance of the two sides.

**Core:** an auction is a mechanism for competitively allocating a scarce resource among three parties: the platform (owns the resource, wants money without losing quality), buyers of the resource (advertisers/sellers bringing content and bids), and a matching algorithm. **Score** balances money and quality: `Score = f(Bid, buyer quality, seller quality)` — otherwise the platform "buys" the SERP, trust and CTR fall, advertisers leave ("lose the buyers and sellers follow"). Advertising ≠ promotion: advertising is absolute positions with a separate auction and purpose-made content that must report; promotion is relative re-weighting of real listings in organic results and can be a hidden auction.

Classification: open formats (English — bids rise; Japanese — leader raises, others exit by limit; Dutch — price falls, first taker wins) don't scale in digital (millions of parallel trades per millisecond); sealed-bid is the digital basis — one lot: First Price (pay your bid) | Second Price/Vickrey (pay the second bid); many lots: GFP | GSP | VCG (pay the marginal harm to others). FPA/GFP: easy to explain ("bid 100, pay 100") but breeds **bid inflation** in recurring auctions (real slot value 80, bids creep 100→120→130) plus manipulation by large players; SPA/GSP: truthful bidding is optimal (overbidding is pointless — you pay the second price) but harder to explain (support load; collusion practically impossible at scale); VCG: theoretically optimal with truthful bidding as the dominant strategy, but nobody can explain the payment — professional closed communities only. Hidden auction (participant doesn't know they're bidding, e.g. classified search) allows complex mechanics unexplained. Allocation example (2 slots of 100/60 clicks; bids per click A 5, B 4, C 3 ₽): optimum A→slot 1, B→slot 2, total value 740; GFP pays 500/240, GSP 400/180, VCG ≈340/180 (marginal harm to others).

Selection rules: three conditions for introducing an auction — scarce resource; demand > supply; differing willingness to pay; otherwise fixed price (and a startup should take SaaS, not build its own). **"The more we must explain, the simpler the auction must be"**: mass market → GFP; professional market or hidden → GSP; trader communities → VCG; test — "would an average user understand why they paid exactly this, without a 'because'?" Money metrics: `Revenue = price × outcome (impressions/clicks/leads)`; **ASP** = revenue / units sold (low ASP = junk traffic or no competition); average bid vs payment (normal gap in GSP, alarming in GFP); RPA = revenue per auction. Market metrics: active participants; **participants per specific auction** (a narrow local market "suffocates" invisibly inside the platform average); **fill rate** = filled slots / all slots — paradoxically critical: an empty slot breaks habitual UX (banner blindness) and advertisers come complaining about falling impressions; selling a slot slightly cheaper while keeping fill rate is often better. Sustainability: share of participants winning anything, advertiser retention/churn, complaints (early churn signal). Advertiser economics sets the bid ceiling: `profit per click = margin per sale × CR click→sale` (phone: 20,000 ₽ × 2% = 400 ₽ max click bid). **Toxic (bad) revenue** — income growing at the market's expense: revenue rises while active participants fall, dependence on 1–2 large buyers grows, fill rate or result quality drops, participants "game the system"; defense: market-health metrics alongside Revenue, participant diversity, moderate floors. Antifraud: bid manipulation and bot traffic kill ASP → cascade "ASP falls → advertisers leave → Revenue falls"; monitor ASP as a leading indicator.

Marketplace: classified connects sides (transaction off-platform — no commission, lead generation) vs marketplace runs the transaction through the platform (higher operational complexity — schedules, documents, logistics — but quality control and commission); classified→marketplace transition (delivery, booking, escrow) = switching on transactional monetization. Flywheel: more sellers → more content → more buyers → more demand → sellers again; **liquidity = both sides present in each specific market (category × geo), not on average**. Take rate = platform share of deal money; in digital auctions compute implicit TR (ad spend / client revenue); category benchmarks: auto lead-gen ~1% of price, housing rent 8–20%, food delivery 15–35%. Balance the sides: a private seller needs ~10 contacts per sale, a professional with 2,000 items needs tens of thousands — allocate proportionally to budget and need, or the small side churns.

**How:** 1) check the three conditions (scarce resource, demand > supply, differing WTP) — otherwise fixed price; 2) define the parties and quality factors entering the score (listing CTR/conversion, seller response speed, relevance); 3) choose mechanics by explainability, decide open vs hidden, fix the right not to explain ranking; 4) calibrate floors/reserves, quality score, anti-domination limits; 5) set money metrics (Revenue, ASP, bid vs payment, RPA); 6) set market metrics (participants, per-auction participants, fill rate); 7) set sustainability metrics (share winning anything, advertiser retention/churn, complaints); 8) compute the client's bid ceiling (profit per click/lead); 9) monitor toxic-revenue signals with reaction thresholds; 10) run antifraud with ASP as the leading indicator.

**Pitfalls:** launching an auction without the three conditions (too few participants — "just let them buy"); GFP for a professional recurring market (bid inflation eats value); SPA for a mass market (endless "why didn't I pay my own bid" tickets); sacrificing fill rate and UX stability for short-term margin on one slot; optimizing only Revenue (revenue up while participants and ASP fall = toxic revenue); comparing average bid with ASP without adjusting for auction type; watching only averages while local auctions (category × geo) suffocate; ignoring client profit-per-click — bids above the client's ceiling wash advertisers out.
