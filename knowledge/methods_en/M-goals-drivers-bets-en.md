# Goals, Growth Drivers, Streams, Focuses, Bets and Portfolio (EN summary)

**When:** turning "we want to grow" into a concrete hierarchy: what exactly changes (goal), through which levers (drivers), in which big direction (stream), what to concentrate on this period (focus), which large hypothesis to back (bet), and which actions (initiatives) verify it. The card guards against the classic jump straight to features.

**Core:** decision hierarchy: goal → streams → drivers → focuses → bets → initiatives → experiments (strategic: goal/streams/drivers; tactical: focuses/bets; operational: initiatives/experiments). Goal ≠ metric ≠ initiative: a goal is what changes in the system (D7 retention +5 pp), "launch a referral program" is an initiative. Six goal types: Growth (registrations +25%), Activation & Retention (activation 40%→55%, D30 12%→18%), Monetization (ARPU +15%), Cost & Efficiency (cost-to-serve −12%), Supply/Quality/Trust (active sellers +20%), Strategic Expansion (new SMB segment, 10% of revenue); a technical "goal" is a task. Goal choice formula: **Strategy × System constraint × Controllability = priority goal** — lifecycle context (PMF → activation/retention; growth → growth/supply; maturity → monetization/efficiency; decline → harvest/pivot), data diagnostics (funnel bottleneck, limiting metric, potential vs benchmarks), controllability (can we move it, is the effect visible within the period); scaling at low retention is "pouring water into a leaky bucket". SMART example: "raise D7 retention from 22% to 30% in 3 months". A **growth driver** is the source of a key-metric change, not a feature: "cut payment-step drop-off by 20 pp" is a driver; "add Apple Pay" is an initiative; "redesign" is a tool. Wording tests: if you can ask "why?" it's an initiative; if there's exactly one implementation path, it's an initiative (a real driver has at least two). A metric decomposes into a product of drivers: `orders = active users × order conversion × order frequency per user`; find the multiplier with the biggest gap vs benchmark → locate the segment and failure point → formulate 2–3 drivers; drivers live at output/input level of the metrics pyramid (outcome and NSM can't be moved "with a button"). Three confused levels: driver (a metric change), bet (an "if… then…" hypothesis about the mechanism), initiative (a concrete action with a deadline). Five-step driver search: goal → decompose the funnel and segment (new/returning, paying/not, devices) → quantitative data in the problem segment → qualitative research → 2–3 drivers grounded in facts. A **stream** is a systemic direction bundling several drivers around one big goal; gives teams focus for at least a quarter; number of streams ≤ number of teams with resources allocated per stream up front (otherwise a wish-list). A **focus** is the concentration direction inside a stream for the period; pick 3–5 (1–2 per team): rely on goals, study funnels and segments, find the bottleneck (the biggest drop, not the most interesting), check resources, estimate metric impact; justify to leadership from data ("drop-off 40% at step 3; cutting to 20% = +X conversions"); review quarterly — the stream may stay, the focus inside it rotates. A **bet** is a large hypothesis materially moving a key metric: "if we do X, metric Y changes by Z within T"; six checks: inside a focus? specific pain? pain confirmed by data? causal mechanism clear? measurable target effect? constraints and resources clear? Pain verification: JTBD (what the person tries to do, what blocks, "product not hired"), CustDev (frequency, emotion, high cost of inaction, workarounds — Excel/YouTube instead of the app), behavioral data (long steps, high drop-off, retry loops), monetary confirmation (the strongest). Pain pyramid: blocking > significant > annoyance > wish list — work the top two; rule: ≥3 of 5 indicators (frequency, scale, emotion, workaround, willingness to pay) = real pain. False signals: "if you add X I'd definitely use it", one loud complaint, a single big client's request, correlation instead of causality. **Portfolio:** 1–2 big bets, 3–5 mid, 5–10 small; Impact/Effort matrix — do-now (high/low, rare), big bet (high/high), quick checks (low/low), decline (low/high); Impact estimated from data, Effort by engineers (t-shirt, planning poker); add Confidence (ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease) — low confidence means research first. Matrix failure signs: everything scored "high impact, low effort"; no data for impact (discovery phase needed); team can't estimate effort (tech spike needed); all initiatives of one type. Quarterly revision: scale what confirmed, kill or reframe what failed, update estimates on new data, reassemble if strategy changed.

**How:** (1) determine lifecycle stage and strategic priorities; (2) formulate 1–2 SMART goals with baseline and benchmarks, test them against the formula, discard tasks disguised as goals; (3) find drivers via the 5 steps; phrase each as a metric change; run the "why?" and "only way?" tests; label type (product/business); (4) bundle drivers into streams; set an objective and measurable key results per stream; (5) pick 3–5 focuses with bottleneck data, expected effect, resource needs, and the alternative ("if we did Y instead — effect Z"); (6) formulate 1–2 bets per focus, check against the six questions and pain indicators; mark what's data-proven vs assumed; (7) per bet describe alternatives: another mechanism, a cheaper test, "do nothing" (what the metric gives at inertia), and a closing condition; record the strongest objection and answer it before deciding; (8) assemble the portfolio, check balance and skew, score Impact (data) and Effort (engineers); (9) pass bets to the risks card and the outcome roadmap (phases, gate criteria, stop signals).

**Pitfalls:** goal = initiative ("launch a referral program"), no baseline or deadline, unrealistic goals, goals mismatched to stage (monetization at D30=5%), several goals at once; level confusion (retention treated as input; NSM = revenue; a broken pyramid — loading speed doesn't move payment conversion if users drop off at another step; unsegmented "hospital average"); driver = initiative ("add Apple Pay"); interviews without quantitative data; only business drivers while value is weak; a fashionable "beautiful focus" without a bottleneck; a bet without confirmed pain ("it feels like it would help"); the user "always right" (asks for solutions instead of describing the problem); socially desirable answers instead of behavior; a static portfolio without revision; Impact estimated without data; everything scored "high impact, low effort" (poor estimation quality).
