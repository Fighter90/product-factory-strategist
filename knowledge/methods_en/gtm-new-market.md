# Entering a new market: GTM by country

## When to apply
When the product enters a new country/region: choose the entry market, estimate its potential and the price of
entry, plan localization, payments and regulation. The map is a **checklist of questions, not legal conclusions**:
for every regulatory item there is a question for a local expert, not an answer (rule 5 of AGENTS: there must be
no closed data and no unverifiable legal claims).

## Core idea
Market entry is a sequence of checks: demand → economics → the mechanics of delivering the product → compliance
risks. The order matters: a beautiful TAM does not save a market where the payment mechanics or the regulation
make the unit economics negative.

Checklist blocks (for each one: questions, data, and the evidence class per [[M-source-criticism]]):

1. **Demand and size:** PAM→TAM→SAM→SOM for this geo separately (not "a share of the global TAM"); behavioral
   substitutes (how the job is solved today); purchasing power and willingness to pay (WTP) in local prices.
2. **Economics:** the CAC of this geo's channels; ARPU in local prices; `LTV/CAC` and payback on the geo's
   cohorts; FX risk (revenue currency vs cost currency).
3. **Product and localization:** the language of the UI/support/content; local UX expectations (date, address and
   receipt formats); local integrations (maps, delivery, content providers); brand and positioning — check whether
   "home" promises are being projected that sound different in this market.
4. **Payments:** the market's payment methods (cards, local wallets, bank transfers, cash on delivery); the share
   of failed payments; **VAT/GST**: rates, registration thresholds, who pays — a question for a tax advisor;
   **MoR (merchant of record) vs your own legal-entity scheme**: speed of entry vs commission and liability.
5. **Data regulation:** where to store personal data (data localization), consent and cross-border transfer
   requirements, age restrictions; questions for a lawyer on the specific jurisdiction — marked as
   "assumption: to be verified by a local expert".
6. **Competitors and channels:** who is already on the market and what their mechanics are ([[M-competitors]]);
   the availability of acquisition channels (ad platforms, partnerships, retail) and their price; cannibalization
   of your own channels in neighboring geos.
7. **Operations:** support (time zones, language, SLA), refunds and chargebacks, logistics (if a physical product).

The entry decision is made only when blocks 1–2 add up to positive unit economics with explicit assumptions, and
blocks 4–5 have a list of open questions for experts with close-by dates.

## Procedure
1. Shortlist 2–3 geo candidates by criteria: SAM size, entry cost, product proximity, regulatory risk.
2. For each, fill in the checklist of blocks 1–7; anything that cannot be computed is recorded as a question with
   an evidence class.
3. Compute the unit economics of entry for each geo (CAC of local channels, ARPU in local prices, payback).
4. Assemble the list of regulatory and payment questions for local experts (taxes, data, payments) with deadlines.
5. Choose the beachhead: one geo, one segment, one channel — and plan the "tour of duty" (the first cohort as an
   experiment).
6. Define the continue/change/stop criteria for the first cohort: payback, retention, failed payments.
7. Plan the expansion: the conditions under which geo #2 opens (the criteria met for geo #1).

## Typical mistakes
- "Global TAM × 5%" instead of the local SOM: the potential is inflated severalfold.
- Transplanting the home market's CAC and conversions onto a new geo without validating the channels.
- Payments "later": an unavailable local payment method kills conversion at checkout.
- Legal conclusions "by analogy" with a neighboring country without a local expert.
- Entering 5 countries at once: support and marketing are spread thin, and no cohort reaches a conclusion.
- Ignoring FX: revenue in a weakening currency while costs are in dollars.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which single regulatory/payment question can make the market unviable — and who will answer it, and when?
- What share of the SOM is actually reachable through our channels in the first year?
- Won't the new geo cannibalize the revenue of neighboring markets ([[M-cannibalization]])?
- What would have to happen with the first cohort for us to stop the expansion?
