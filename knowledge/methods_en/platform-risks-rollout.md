# Platform Risks, Review, Pilot, Scaling, Investor Case (EN summary)

**When:** "what could revise the platform decision and how do we prepare?", "how to package and defend the final document?", "how to choose a pilot project and decide on its results?", "how to scale the platform and raise client adoption?", "how to assemble the investment case for the committee?" — the closing loop of the platform track, after the concept and economics cards.

**Core:** a positive ROI shows the platform's potential, not the probability of realizing it — the case is tested through risks, a pilot and regular reviews. Distinguish: risk (event hasn't happened but may change the outcome), problem (happened, requires reaction), constraint (known condition we work within), assumption (accepted in the model, unconfirmed). Platform risk categories: product & value/adoption; technology & data; economics; organization; legal & compliance; PR — adoption itself is a risk (four clients will connect, migration takes a quarter, savings in a year… may become delayed migration, an expensive transition and a shifted or vanished break-even). Prioritization: probability × impact → P0 blocking (can stop the launch or make the platform unviable), P1 critical (changes scope, economics, architecture, roadmap), P2 significant (requires action, doesn't change the decision), P3 local (monitored in the team); risk phrased as **cause → event → consequences**. **Concept review document:** case passport + traffic lights (traction-model validation, investment control) on page one; executive summary and decision ask; FAQ blocks by section (product idea, segments, strategy fit; tasks, GTM); traction and financial summary; concerns and risks; success criteria and next-review date; appendices and sources. Every key claim follows the chain **thesis → source → calculation → decision**; cross-check the team on: USP, problem-hypothesis checks, MLP description, input-metric linkage to the product's essence, traction model and IRR, criteria for the next review and stop criteria. **Pilot:** candidate criteria (rate poorly/medium/well + why): learning value (which platform hypotheses it tests), client readiness (owner and resource present), influential sponsor, impact/clear value, bounded reversible risk, effort (development and integration cost). Measured as: Easy Start before launch — qualitative interviews with platform users (what was easy/hard, what docs lacked, what constraints blocked, what to change so the next team launches unaided) plus quantitative — support requests to the platform team, integration-start-to-launch time, effort of both sides in person-sprints, changes made specifically for the pilot; functionality during (triggers/rules work, audience respected, data correct, failure behavior defined, analytics computed); stability (critical incidents, downtime ≤0.5%, ≥99% of events processed successfully, monitoring helps diagnosis). Decision: **change / scale / stop** — change: fix before scaling (e.g. the team misjudged integration); scale: grow client count while monitoring every launch; stop: effect significantly negative or a blocker found; effect untested due to a narrow launch audience → another pilot; a top manager doubts the direction itself → work the opinion, don't "scale". **Scaling and adoption:** usage ≠ adoption — usage means the action happened; adoption means the behavior stuck (returned, regular use, part of the routine); granted access ≠ adoption (link/demo/account don't count; count changed working behavior: assigned an owner, launched, came back). Three adoption measures: by clients (teams launched within N days / all eligible), by places/components (slots via the platform / all eligible — replacement depth), by volume (impressions/transactions via the platform / all eligible). Client-team adoption funnel: eligible → aware → requirements finalized → ready → launched → repeats/expands, each transition with barriers and cures: top of funnel (task wrongly judged ineligible → simple eligibility criteria + short consult; platform doesn't know its clients → scenario registry, study team plans, regular request collection); aware (no link to their task → offer in the team's language: "launch X with ready analytics", not "connect to the platform"; platform GTM); requirements (client uninterested → value, change agent, staged migration of the existing solution; can't phrase requirements → questionnaire template; rejects common rules → show consequences for other teams, a guaranteed test-traffic experiment with completion criteria — never disabling the rules, a dangerous precedent); ready (not in priorities, no resources → show the client's workload early, readiness checklist, shared OKR, sponsor; cross-team dependencies → dependency map); launched (new requirements mid-flight → fix MVP scope, new asks go to next iterations; underestimation → cut scope/re-date; slow development → docs, integration templates, regular blocker meetings); repeats (failed first experience → self-service growth points, improvement news; no next scenario → show other clients' cases). **Platform GTM:** landing page (value, scenarios, launch path, success cases, "discuss your task" button), internal PR (talks, demos, digests), work with teams (request collection, quarterly planning, change agents per vertical). Scaling forks resolve by "an exception is a dangerous precedent": seek a platform solution first; migrate in stages from the simplest segment; fix tech debt with a deadline. **Investor case (committee defense):** case passport (class, level, effect type) + traction-validation and investment-control traffic lights + vertical support; executive summaries of previous and current reviews (platform boundary, commits, facts); FAQ 1 idea and strategy; FAQ 2 tasks, success criteria, roadmap, progress ("done / criterion" tables); FAQ 3 traction — input/output metrics with old/new version columns and explanations; FAQ 5 financial summary — ΔFCF of the "without platform" vs "with platform" scenarios minus the platform team, IRR, upper-escalation signs (negative cash flow above a threshold, costs above a threshold within 12 months, M&A); FAQ 6 traffic lights (analytics yellow while the effect is measured on one client — until a second/third measurement; investment control green — costs bounded to one team, conservative model, on funding stop the platform transfers to the first client as a service); FAQ 7 success criteria and commits to the next review; appendices (P&L vs the previous committee, cost detail, incremental P&L, scenarios, measurement methodology, core composition component by component, integration rules, onboarding queue). Case strength lives in the sources: future product count from the company's approved plan; "without platform" costs from actual spend of own and neighboring teams; acceleration from the pilot measurement; integration cost in client person-days.

**How:** (1) build a risk register (5–7 risks; P0–P3 by the matrix; cause → event → consequences; mitigation plan — reduce probability or damage, what before onset, early signals, actions after; owner and involved parties; detailed plans for the top three); (2) translate concept constraints into risks (what breaks at the client → how compensated → if not, disclose honestly); (3) assemble the defense document per the skeleton; verify the thesis → source → calculation → decision chain in every section; cross-check sections and metrics for contradictions; mark gaps rather than hide them; (4) pick the pilot: score candidates on the 6 criteria in a summary table with each one's expected effect; (5) run the pilot and measure Easy Start (interviews + 4 metrics), functionality, stability; handle incidents by protocol (check scale → disable the faulty part → fix → resume on a small audience → extend the pilot if data is insufficient; support through a process, not personal chats); (6) decide change / scale / stop on actual metrics; record growth points and the improvement plan; (7) design scaling: onboarding queue in waves; the adoption funnel and its narrowest transition; a barrier-removal plan per transition; the GTM plan; conflicts resolved with platform solutions, not exceptions; (8) assemble the investor case: concrete ask (team and deadline), commits to the next review, explanations of changed numbers, traffic-light status with rationale.

**Pitfalls:** confusing risk with problem/constraint/assumption; risks phrased without cause and consequences ("risks always exist"); hiding yellow/red traffic lights or setting them without a methodology (traffic lights belong before the executive summary, with explanations); generalizing an effect measured on one client without a second/third measurement; counting access, demos and account creation as adoption; an adoption funnel built but barriers never named or removed; exceptions for strong clients (disabling common rules for one client destroys the platform); a pilot without a defined failure criterion (any result gets declared success); "we need leadership support" instead of specifics; investor-case numbers from guesses instead of plans and facts.
