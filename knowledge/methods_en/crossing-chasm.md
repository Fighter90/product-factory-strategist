# Crossing the Chasm: enthusiasts love it, pragmatists don't buy

## When to use
The product appeals to the early audience (enthusiasts/evangelists), but growth has stalled: new segments that are "almost like them" do not convert. The classic picture of the chasm between early and late groups of the diffusion curve.

## Core idea
- **Diffusion**: innovators → early enthusiasts → **early pragmatists** → late pragmatists → skeptics. The chasm sits between the enthusiasts and the first pragmatists: enthusiasts have patience for "rough" products, pragmatists do not; they buy complete solutions built for their industry, with references from "their own kind."
- **Beachhead**: choose ONE nodal niche (segment + scenario + problem) where the product becomes a "complete solution" without caveats — and dominate it, rather than spreading thin.
- **Whole product**: everything a pragmatist needs to succeed: support, integrations, documentation, training, references — not only the core.
- **Bowling alley**: from the dominant niche into adjacent niches sharing the same pain/decision criterion; each pin is a new "whole product."
- **Moore's positioning**: the claim ("for [whom] — [what] — unlike [the alternative] — our product…") — never "for everyone."

## How to do it
1. Discovery by segments: where the pain is strongest and the reference group is densest.
2. Choose the beachhead: criteria — pain, ability to pay, access to the segment, absence of a dominant "whole-product competitor."
3. Map the whole product: what is missing before "I will buy without hesitation" (from pragmatist interviews).
4. Positioning using Moore's formula; first-wave references become case studies.
5. The next niche only after dominance (metrics: niche share, repeat purchases, referrals).

## Pitfalls
- "Our product is for everyone" — a chasm signal.
- Growing into large "non-native" segments before beachhead dominance.
- A whole product assembled from a feature list instead of pragmatist interviews.
