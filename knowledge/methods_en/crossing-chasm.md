# Crossing the Chasm: the enthusiasts love it, the pragmatists don't buy

## When to apply
The product is liked by the early audience (enthusiasts/early adopters), but growth has stalled: the new segments that are "almost like them" do not convert. The classic picture of the chasm between the early and the late groups of the diffusion curve.

## Core idea
- **Diffusion**: innovators → early enthusiasts → **early pragmatists** → late pragmatists → skeptics. The chasm sits between the enthusiasts and the first pragmatists: the enthusiasts have patience for "raw", the pragmatists do not; they buy whole solutions for their industry, and with references from "their own kind".
- **Beachhead**: pick ONE anchor niche (a segment + a scenario + a problem) where the product becomes a "complete solution" without caveats — and dominate it, instead of scattering.
- **The whole product**: everything a pragmatist needs in order to succeed: support, integrations, documentation, training, references — not only the core.
- **The bowling alley**: from the dominant niche into the adjacent ones that share the same pain/the same criterion of the solution; every pin is a new "whole product".
- **Positioning by Moore**: the claim ("for — whom — what — unlike the alternative — our product…") — with no "for everyone".

## How to do it
1. Discovery by segments: where the pain is the greatest and the reference group is the densest.
2. Pick the beachhead: the criteria — the pain, the willingness to pay, the access to the segment, the absence of a dominant "whole competitor".
3. Assemble the whole-product map: what is missing until "I would buy without hesitation" (from the interviews with pragmatists).
4. Positioning by Moore's formula; the references of the first wave go into the cases.
5. The next niche — only after dominance (the metrics: the share of the niche, the repeat purchases, the referrals).

## Pitfalls
- "Our product is for everyone" — a signal of the chasm.
- Growing through large "non-native" segments before the beachhead dominance.
- A whole product assembled from a list of features instead of the interviews with pragmatists.
