# Adjacent Landscapes: Marketing, Technology, Business Model (EN summary)

**When:** when a growth strategy must survive the check "does it work together with the rest of the business?": can the product actually be built and delivered (technology, marketing, sales), does it make money (business model), does the client need it. Helps complement product bets with the internal environment, choose or revise the business model, and prepare an investment-committee case.

**Core:** The product is a value-creation mechanism; the business is a system of functions. Value must be delivered, monetized and repeated (retention/expansion). Functions: Product/Tech — feasibility, speed, quality, security, scaling; architecture and tech debt limit or accelerate; give data, experiments, stable value delivery. Marketing — create demand and right expectations, bring the "right client" at an acceptable price; packaging: message, pricing, segmentation; give market signals, JTBD, channel quality. Sales — turn demand into contracts and predictable revenue; walk the client through a complex decision (why change, why you, why now); give barrier signals and decision-maker requirements. Support/success — deliver the promised value, retain; give feedback. Finance/operations — budget, unit economics, decision frames. Three lenses of innovation — an initiative succeeds only if simultaneously: desirable (who is the main user, which segments get max value, which need, what makes them return), feasible (expertise and key people, capacity, external system dependencies), viable (who pays, for what, when; fixed and variable costs and whether the economics converge; most profitable clients; why now). Marketing spans four areas: performance (measurable acquisition: CPA, CPC, ROAS; days–weeks), brand (awareness and trust; months–years), product marketing (positioning, launch), growth (experiments: referrals, SEO, A/B). Funnel: awareness → interest → consideration → conversion → retention → referral. Acquisition metrics: CPA = spend / target actions; CPC = spend / clicks; CPM = spend / (impressions/1000); ROAS = ad revenue / ad spend; CAC = (marketing + sales) / new clients per period; K-factor = invites per user × invite conversion. Marketing is not advertising but a system of demand and meaning. Sales: three questions — why change (the risk of doing nothing), why us (value proposition and a client ROI model), why now (decision-maker, budget cycle, approvals); types: self-service (minutes, low CAC), inside sales (days/weeks), field sales (months, high CAC), channel sales via partners; translation chain function → benefit → outcome (dashboard → metric visibility → faster decisions). Tech: roles (development, QA, analytics, design, tech lead); collaboration rules: discuss "why" not "how", respect estimates, involve engineers in discovery early, budget tech debt, align roadmap and priorities. Business model = the mechanism of earning: who pays, for what, how price forms — not a business type; a large business is always a set of models; changing the model can be the main growth driver. Model formulas: retail margin = sale price − purchase price (₽/unit); subscription revenue = subscribers × ARPU (MRR/ARR; condition LTV > CAC); freemium revenue = free users × free-to-paid conversion × paying ARPU (benchmarks: SaaS ~2–5%, games ~1–5%, high-engagement products ~10–15%; the free tier must itself create value — a collection, a network — and the paid tier removes a constraint; risk: a generous free tier "feeds" users who never pay but create costs); pay-per-use = unit price × consumption volume; advertising revenue = impressions × CPM / 1000 × fill rate; commission = GMV × take rate; revenue share = total revenue × platform share. Subscription metrics: MRR = New + Expansion − Churned − Contraction (shows the source of growth — acquisition or expansion); plus ARR, churn, LTV, CAC, ARPU, payback; types: fixed, tiered, usage-based, all-you-can-eat. Main risk is churn: 5% monthly churn loses more than half the base in a year — subscription is justified only with a long-term need and repeating value. Commission economics in one chain (illustrative figures): 10,000 active sellers × 5 transactions/seller/mo × 3,000₽ average check = 50,000 transactions/mo; GMV = transactions × check = 150M₽/mo; revenue at 8% take rate = 12M₽/mo; minus per-transaction COGS (payments + delivery) = gross profit; growth levers: raise take rate (risk: sellers leave, disintermediation), cut COGS (logistics, payments), raise check (recommendations, cross-sell). Marketplace liquidity = completed deals / posted listings — the basic "does the market converge" metric. Two structural risks: disintermediation (sides deal directly, bypassing the fee) and chicken-and-egg (buyers need sellers and vice versa; launch concentrated on one side). Commission vs revenue share: commission is % of a deal (one side pays; the platform mediates; ~5–30% of GMV); revenue share is % of total revenue (both sides partners; ~30–70%; App Store 70/30, video platforms 55/45). Take rate rises with value added to the deal: classifieds ~3–10%, e-commerce marketplaces ~10–25% (logistics, fulfillment), services ~10–20% (verification, guarantees), delivery/taxi ~20–35% (logistics, payments, traffic), hotels ~15–25% (global traffic), app distribution ~15–30% (channel monopoly). Signals to revise the model: falling LTV/CAC; churn growth with a stable product; a competitor growing faster on another model; an uncovered segment; the chance to control the transaction (add commission); "free" users creating costs. Investment committee = a formalized internal pitch (resources always limited, more ideas than hands): problem (for whom, how big), solution, metrics and timeframe, resources, risks, alternatives (including "what if we don't" and a cheaper variant). Prioritization: ICE, RICE (Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort), MoSCoW, Value/Effort matrix.

**How:** (1) run the chosen bet through the three lenses; type every answer as fact / assumption / gap; (2) describe the current and target business model: who pays, for what, revenue formula, unit and period, unit economics; if the bet changes the model (e.g. classifieds → transaction), assess the impact on each market side and list risks; (3) check model compatibility (subscription and advertising can conflict; freemium and commission need care) — do revenue sources cannibalize each other?; (4) describe the marketing landscape: channels, CAC per channel, brand's role, paid-traffic dependency, season windows; (5) the tech landscape: platforms and data to lean on, dependencies on other teams, tech debt, limits on hypothesis-testing speed; (6) the business landscape: legal and financial frames, partners, roles of sales and support; (7) conclude which functions become the bet's bottleneck and what must happen before start; (8) prepare the committee pitch (six blocks: problem, solution, metrics and timeframe, resources, risks, alternatives — numbers and sources in each) and prioritize the portfolio (ICE/RICE or Value/Effort), stating what is postponed. Landscape map of the bet per area (marketing, tech, sales/support, business model & finance, legal/compliance): what exists | bottleneck | dependency/owner | what to do before start.

**Pitfalls:** "the product is everything" — ignoring that the business rests on cost structure, resources, partners, distribution and monetization; marketing treated as advertising, sales as "a call-center department"; one model for everything when it doesn't match client behavior; a skipped lens — desirable but infeasible or unprofitable, feasible but unwanted; no answer to "why now"; planning on development estimates without buffer and dependencies; a pitch without alternatives and risks.
