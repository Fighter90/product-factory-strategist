# Continuous Discovery: discovery every week, not as a project

## When to use
The team already works in sprints and cannot afford a "month of discovery." What is needed is a continuous stream of contact with users and a transparent map of "risk → opportunity → solution."

## Core idea
- **Opportunity Solution Tree**: the product goal at the top → branches of opportunities (user needs/pains, not features!) → under each one, solutions → under each solution, validation experiments. The same phenomenon may appear in the tree multiple times — that is normal.
- **Touchpoint cadence**: weekly contact with users (interview snippets of 20–30 minutes, not six-week "projects"). Torres's rule: "someone on the team talks to a user every week."
- **Three parallel risks** are tested simultaneously: value (is it needed), usability (is it understandable), feasibility/business viability (can it be built and is it worthwhile).
- **Interview snippets** — short structured records of facts from a conversation (quote + context), accumulated into a knowledge base instead of dying in a notes folder.
- **Mini decision canvas** before starting the work: the main risk, the most dangerous assumption, the minimal test.

## How to do it
1. Frame the product goal for the quarter (metric + boundaries).
2. Build/update the opportunity tree: interviews → opportunity statements in the user's voice ("I need a way to…").
3. Maintain the touchpoint cadence; each time — one research nuance (pains, price, context of use).
4. For the priority opportunity — 3+ alternative solutions and an assumption story table.
5. Pick the most dangerous assumption → minimal test → feed the result back into the tree.

## Pitfalls
- A tree of features instead of opportunities — the meaning is lost.
- Touchpoints "when there is time" — discovery dies; the cadence must be fixed in the calendar.
- Testing for testing's sake: a test must hit the most dangerous assumption.
