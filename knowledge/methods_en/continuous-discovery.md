# Continuous Discovery: discovery every week, not as a one-off project

## When to apply
The team already works in sprints and cannot afford a "month of discovery". What is needed is a continuous stream of contact with users and a transparent map of "risk → opportunity → solution".

## Core idea
- **The opportunity solution tree**: the product goal sits at the top → its branches are opportunities (user needs and pains, not features!) → under each opportunity sit solutions → under each solution sit experiments that verify it. The same phenomenon may appear in the tree many times — that is normal and expected.
- **Touchpoint cadence**: weekly contact with users (interview snippets of 20–30 minutes, not "projects" of 6 weeks). Torres' rule: "someone on the team talks to a user every week".
- **Three parallel risks** are checked simultaneously: value (is it needed at all), usability (is it understandable), feasibility/business (can it be done, and is it profitable).
- **Interview snippets** — short, structured records of facts from a conversation (a quote plus context); they accumulate into a shared base instead of dying in a folder of notes.
- **A mini-canvas of the solution** is drawn before the work begins: the main risk, the most dangerous assumption, the minimal test.

## How to do it
1. Formulate the product goal for the quarter (a metric plus its boundaries).
2. Build or update the opportunity tree: interviews → opportunity statements phrased in the user's own words ("I need a way to…").
3. Keep the touchpoint cadence going; each touchpoint covers one research nuance (pains, price, context of use).
4. For the priority opportunity — 3+ alternative solutions and a storytelling-style table of assumptions.
5. Pick the most dangerous assumption → run a minimal test → feed the result back into the tree.

## Pitfalls
- A tree made of features instead of opportunities — the whole point is lost.
- Touchpoints "when there is time" — discovery dies; fix the cadence in the calendar.
- A test for the sake of testing: every test must hit the most dangerous assumption.
