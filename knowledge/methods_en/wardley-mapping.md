# Wardley Mapping: strategy from a map, not from intuition

## When to apply
You need to understand where the components of a product/market "do not yet exist" yet and where they have "become electricity"; to decide what to build yourself, what to buy, what to hand over to a provider; to find the moments for an attack.

## Essence
- **The map**: the X axis — the evolution of a component (Genesis → Custom-built → Product (+rental) → Commodity/utility), the Y axis — the visibility for the user (the chain of needs from the top down). The components are nouns; the links are "is needed for".
- **Evolution = movement along X over time regardless of the will of the player** (standardization, competition). The position determines the game: Genesis — explore, Custom — build for yourself/a differentiator, Product — buy/rent, Commodity — consume like electricity.
- **Climatic signals**: competition lowers prices, standardization is inevitable, "yesterday's success", etc. — the map shows what is about to evolve (a window of opportunity/risk).
- **Games**: attack the evolving components of competitors (their differentiator has become a commodity), build higher up the chain, hand the non-differentiators over to a provider.
- Practice: the map is drawn with the team in 1–2 hours on a board; it is checked against the market, it is not "the truth".

## How to do it
1. Define the user and their need (the top of the chain).
2. Write out the components downward along "is needed for"; place them on the evolution axis (what does the market say: how many suppliers are there, are there standards?).
3. Mark the movement (what is evolving right now) and the hostile/friendly positions.
4. Strategy: build for the Custom differentiators, buy/rent for Product, watch for the Genesis windows.

## Pitfalls
- A map made once and forever — it is a process, keep updating it.
- Estimating the evolution "by wish", not by the market (the number of suppliers, standardization).
- Features on the map instead of noun components.
