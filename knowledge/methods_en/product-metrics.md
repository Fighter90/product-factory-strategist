# Product Metrics (EN summary)

**When:** choosing or validating a target metric; building a funnel or metric tree; estimating an initiative's effect; finding the bottleneck; formulating success criteria and counter-metrics; especially — verifying that formulas, units and periods agree before drawing conclusions.

**Core — metric validity:** a metric is a measurable indicator you define yourself to see and change product reality; it's fit for use only if ALL hold: computable; data exists and is logged correctly; everyone understands and computes it the same way; it reflects a specific goal; it can be influenced by concrete actions; its dynamics are trackable (chart, alert). Metric passport: what it measures · formula (numerator/denominator, units) · period and window · event source · owner · lever of influence · link to the goal. Classifications: product (DAU/MAU, retention D1/D7/D30, Task Success, NPS, CSAT) vs business (revenue/GMV, LTV, CAC, ARPU/ARPPU, ROI, net profit); input (leading, controllable) vs output (lagging); frameworks AARRR, HEART, One Metric That Matters, NSM, AIDA — adapted, not copied.

**Funnel:** CR = users passing step A→B / users at A per period; the funnel is built on the CJM; rules: events in the user's language; one path to the goal = one funnel (otherwise steps get "skipped" and percentages distort); the funnel window equals the real action cycle (e-commerce minutes–days, SaaS week–month). Reading: (1) where the sharpest collapse; (2) why there — compare with benchmarks and unit economics, hypothesize causes (high prices, slow delivery, technical errors, out of stock, irrelevant search results), cut by segments (device, source, hour, age); (3) money lost = lost users × average check × period (10,000 × 250 ₽ = 2.5M ₽/day). Unit checks: step conversion ≠ through conversion (search 100% → card 70% → cart 15% → payment 5%: step CRs are 70%, 21.4%, 33.3%; through-to-payment = 5%); percentage points ≠ percent (62% → 15% = −47pp and −76% relative). A micro-funnel inside the biggest-loss step; "metric passport" for events prevents renamed-event resets.

**Retention and cohorts:** Retention Dn = share of the day-0 cohort returning and doing the target action on day n; day zero must be defined unambiguously (registration, first activity, email confirmation) — shifting it inflates retention. Models: classic cohort (D0/D1/D7/D30; e-commerce, fintech), N-day rolling (7/28-day; socials, content), product return rate ("returned and did the target action"; B2B SaaS). Cohorts by device, traffic source, first feature/scenario, acquisition period; the average without cohorts hides problems; the plateau height = loyalty. Stickiness = DAU/MAU (0.2–0.3 typical for utilities, 0.5+ for top socials — depends on usage frequency); falling stickiness at stable MAU = "they come but don't live". For rare transactions use proxies (share of active listings). Satisfaction: CSAT (1–5 per interaction, ask immediately), CES (effort, 1–7, after complex/rare scenarios), NPS = % promoters (9–10) − % critics (0–6), quarterly, by segments.

**Money and growth (formulas with units):** ARPU = period revenue / active users; ARPPU = period revenue / paying users; GMV = sum of order prices; AOV = GMV / orders; CAC = period marketing spend (full composition: bonuses, store commissions, referral payouts, part of payroll) / new clients of the same period; ROAS = campaign revenue / campaign spend (24h and 7-day windows); ROMI = (income − marketing spend) / spend; Payback = CAC / marginal income per user per period; LTV ≈ ARPU × expected active periods ≈ ARPU / (1 − r) at constant retention r. Checks: don't divide daily ARPU by weekly retention; compare LTV and CAC in one currency and comparable margin (marginal LTV, not revenue); CAC and revenue for the same period and cohort; promo revenue separated from organic; multi-currency GMV converted at deal-date rates; "CM = ARPU − CAC" mixes periodic income with one-off acquisition cost — compare accumulated marginal LTV with CAC. Guidelines (verify, not norms): CAC ≤ ~30% of LTV; 7-day ROAS ≥ 1.2; payback ≤ 3 months for a mature business (up to 6 for a startup); paid-channel retention no more than ~5pp below organic.

**NSM and metric tree:** the NSM is the main metric reflecting user value, linked to revenue/LTV; selection: what is the core value, which unit of result is measurable, does it scale and drive growth; criteria: clear value link, correlation with revenue/LTV verified on history, changes weekly/monthly, several functions influence it, explainable in plain words; hierarchy NSM → drivers (2–4) → operational metrics → process metrics (Lead Time, Change Failure Rate); an NSM lives ~a year, changes with strategy. Tree: GMV = orders × average check; orders = users × purchase CR; average check = items per order × average price; rules: decompose mathematically everything possible; keep level connectivity; descend to the level the team can actually influence; every arrow has an owner and a proven link (correlation/experiment, not assumption). **Guardrails**: metrics that must not degrade while the target grows — NPS/CSAT/CES, SLA and response time, contact rate, returns, complaints, retention, CAC, neighboring-product metrics (cannibalization), latency — each with a threshold set before start (example: "fast delivery" raised GMV +6% but CES showed rising effort and NPS fell 4pp a month later). Metric trio for any scale: target (success criterion) + proxies 1–3 (move earlier, proven correlation with the target) + counters 2–3 (thresholds before start); after the experiment: **Scale / Iterate / Drop**. Good metric = sensitivity × stability × simplicity (CTR reacts in days, retention doesn't; test on history before OKR; reject noisy metrics, opaque composite indexes, manipulatively ambiguous ones like "time on page").

**A metric drop can be normal — triage algorithm:** 1) context and scale: magnitude vs usual spread, when exactly (release/campaign/date), for whom (all or one segment); 2) volatility vs trend: outline a 3–6-month "normal corridor" on history — inside it, no analysis needed; sharp short drop → bug/release/campaign/external event; gradual weeks-long decline → systemic cause (growth is checked the same way); 3) data verification — the most common cause: DWH recalcs, collection failures, changed calculation code, duplicated rows (a day's duplication gives a "spike and rollback" — almost always data, not behavior); 4) seasonality: YoY, WoW, deviation from forecast; 5) decomposition via the metric tree to localize the fallen branch; 6) segmentation (characteristics — category, city, source, platform) and cohorts (arrival time — new cohorts behaving worse; a retention heatmap with a diagonal reveals seasonal cohorts); 7) presentation: conclusion and recommendation on top, numbers and cuts below.

**How:** 1) write the goal and stage; pick one target metric (output), 1–3 drivers (input), 2–3 counter-metrics; 2) build a passport per metric and run the 6-point validity check; 3) build the funnel on the CJM, compute step and through conversions, find the max loss, price it, cut by segments and cohorts; 4) for retention: fix an unambiguous day zero and target action, build cohorts at least by source and period; 5) build the metric tree from the money/NSM goal to controllable drivers; remove nodes without owner or link; 6) per initiative record: expected change (metric, magnitude, units), baseline, comparison (control/before-after/cohort), observation period, success and failure criteria, counters with thresholds, expected side effects; statuses fact / hypothesis / forecast; 7) before concluding, run the units-and-periods check: pp vs %, step vs through, active vs paying vs new, day vs week vs month, revenue vs margin, currency, seasonality, cohort age, matching windows in numerator and denominator; 8) fix the formula, owner and review date.

**Pitfalls:** metrics for pretty charts not tied to a business goal; dozens of metrics without hierarchy; no passport — different teams compute differently, renamed events "reset" the funnel; averages instead of cohorts/segments; overall CAC without channel cuts; comparing ARPU and retention of different periods; dividing revenue by users of another period; success counted by numerator growth while denominator quality falls (GMV up, retention down); changing day zero or window mid-test; no counter-metric or threshold defined upfront; correlation in a tree arrow taken as causality without verification.
