# Lean Startup: a lesson per cycle, not a plan per quarter

## When to apply
High uncertainty: a new product, a new segment, a new channel. You need a cycle that turns doubt into verified facts as cheaply as possible.

## Essence
- **Build–Measure–Learn**: first formulate WHAT you need to learn (a hypothesis), then design the minimal experiment (Build), measure against a criterion set in advance (Measure) and draw the conclusion (Learn) — pivot or persevere.
- **An MVP is an experiment, not a miniature product**: its job is to test the single most dangerous assumption for minimal money/time. Types: fake door, concierge, Wizard of Oz, single-feature MVP, pre-sale.
- **Innovation accounting**: baseline → metric movement toward the target → decision. Metrics must be actionable (moving as a result of actions), not vanity (downloads, signups without activation).
- **Pivots are the norm**: changing the segment, channel, value proposition or monetization upon negative learning. A pivot without validated learning is panic.
- **Classes of hypotheses**: value, growth, monetization — they are tested with different experiments and metrics.

## How to do it
1. Formulate the hypothesis: "We believe that [segment] experiences [problem] and will [take action], if [solution]".
2. Define the minimal experiment and the success metric IN ADVANCE (before any results).
3. Run it, compare against the criterion, record the validated learning in the claims registry.
4. Decision: persevere / pivot / the next most dangerous assumption.

## Pitfalls
- An MVP without a success criterion set in advance — any result gets interpreted as "encouraging".
- Measuring vanity metrics instead of movement toward the goal.
- Cycles measured in quarters instead of weeks.
