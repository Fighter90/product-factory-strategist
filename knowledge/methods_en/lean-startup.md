# Lean Startup: a lesson per loop, not a plan per quarter

## When to apply
High uncertainty: a new product, a new segment, a new channel. You need the loop that turns doubt into verified facts at the lowest cost.

## Core idea
- **Build–Measure–Learn**: first formulate WHAT to learn (a hypothesis), then design the minimal experiment (Build), measure it against a criterion fixed in advance (Measure) and draw the conclusion (Learn) — a pivot or doubling down.
- **An MVP is an experiment, not a mini-version of the product**: its task is to test the single riskiest assumption for minimal money/time. Types: fake door, concierge, wizard of oz, single-feature MVP, pre-sale.
- **Innovation accounting**: baseline → movement of the metric toward the goal → decision. Metrics must be actionable (they move as a result of your actions), not vanity (downloads, sign-ups without activation).
- **Pivots are the norm**: changing the segment, the channel, the value proposition or the monetization after negative learning. A pivot without validated learning is panic.
- **Hypothesis classes**: value, growth, monetization — they are tested with different experiments and different metrics.

## How to do it
1. Formulate the hypothesis: "We believe [segment] experiences [problem] and will [action] if [solution]".
2. Define the minimal experiment and the success metric IN ADVANCE (before the results).
3. Run it, compare with the criterion, record the validated learning in the claims registry.
4. Decision: persevere / pivot / the next riskiest assumption.

## Pitfalls
- An MVP without a success criterion fixed in advance — any result is read as "encouraging".
- Measuring vanity metrics instead of movement toward the goal.
- Cycles measured in quarters instead of weeks.
