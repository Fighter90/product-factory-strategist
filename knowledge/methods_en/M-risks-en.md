# Strategy Risks and Mitigation (EN summary)

**When:** for any strategy and any big bet, especially when resources are being requested. The risk section is a mandatory item of the defense — no investment decision without it. The card gives the procedure from a threat list to an action plan with triggers and owners, plus update rituals.

**Core:** a product risk is anything that can go wrong while building, launching and running the product and prevent it from reaching goals, succeeding in the market and solving user tasks. Why risks matter: for the business — controllability of the result, budget for the worst case, decision quality, vision synchronization, investor trust; for the PM — managing expectations (a maturity signal), requesting resources with risks priced in upfront (asking for "another 20%" after the fact is far harder), readiness to maneuver instead of firefighting. Everything always goes off plan — the question is not whether a risk materializes but whether the PM is ready; if it does, they will be asked — so they must speak up in time.

**Eight risk types**: product (users don't understand the value, metrics fall); technical (incidents, bugs, integrations); business (the model doesn't close, monetization doesn't cover costs); market (overestimated demand, competition, seasonality); legal (laws, licenses, fines, liability); fraud (user and partner fraud, leaks); operational (support load, SLA); marketing (channels don't work, value isn't communicated). **Assessment**: Risk = Probability × Impact; probability 0–100% (or 1–5); impact on key metrics (revenue, users, deadlines, reputation). To compare heterogeneous risks, convert damage to one metric — usually revenue: conversion → via traffic × average check × take rate; seller churn → lost bookings; reputation → churn × LTV; operational → staff and compensation cost. Matrix zones: **red** (high probability × high damage) — immediate attention, usually avoid or mitigate with a mandatory plan B; **orange** — mitigate or transfer; **yellow/green** — accept or minimal mitigation. **Four reactions (AATM)**: Accept (mitigation costs more than the risk; conscious acceptance, not neglect); Avoid (red zone — too expensive and too likely; better not to do it); Transfer (a third party can bear it: an insurance partner pays damages; a bank bears installment default risk); Mitigate (reduce probability or damage: tests, pilot, plan B).

**Mitigation methods**: minimal hypothesis check (MVP, A/B, fake door — a button for a non-existent feature → "Coming soon, leave your email"; CTR measured; confirmation threshold ~3–5%; costs days; shows interest, not willingness to pay; unlike an MVP, nothing works behind the button); pilot launch (region/segment; regional tests on at least two regions — one gives too small a sample); resource reservation (the **"+20%" rule** applies to timelines, budget, headcount — "tech lead said 2 sprints, count 2 + 20%" — and conservatively to funnel conversions in the model); agreements with teams (priority, resources, fixed dependencies); **plan B** — a trigger metric with a justified threshold, action scenarios, owners. The threshold comes from economics (the traction model shows allowable churn at minimal project revenue), not off the ceiling: "if seller churn > 40% — don't win back old ones, acquire new via partners; more than 3 violations → block". **Validation method by stage** (the earlier, the cheaper the method): focus group and fake door — primary at Discovery; MVP — primary at MVP stage; A/B — primary at Growth and Maturity; pilot — appropriate at MVP/Growth; +20% reserve, dependency fixation and plan B — always. **Seasonality is part of risk assessment** (travel example: peak Q3, rise Q2, decline Q4, low Q1): mass launches and metric reads only in the peak season (off-season effects drown in seasonal noise and can't be proven); off-season — preparation, integrations, seller retention; a counter-seasonal launch needs separate justification; always ask "which season are we measuring on?". **Legal risk decision tree** instead of blanket mitigation: probability > 50% and damage > threshold → avoid (don't do it / change the model); probability < 50% but very large damage → mitigate (lawyer + plan B); otherwise transfer (insurance, contract) or accept + legislation monitoring; damage thresholds set in money per year and fixed. **Antifraud pyramid** by rising cost: (1) basic content moderation; (2) behavioral detection (platform bypass, contact exchange in chat); (3) penalty system (strikes, warnings); (4) channel restriction (communication closed until payment); (5) proactive ML anomaly detection — phased as transaction share grows; building everything at once is expensive and hurts conversion. Risk types are conventional — one risk can be fraud and business at once; the type of measure matters more than the label. **Stakeholders and DACI**: Driver (the PM, accountable for the result), Approver, Contributor (marketing, legal, BD, teams), Informed; don't forget peers and your own managers; agreements fixed in a table: stakeholder, agreement, status, single contact, deadline. **Rituals**: threat modeling, post-mortems, a unified risk map, "three amigos" review (PM, developer/tester, designer looking at mockups early), quarterly dive-in with stakeholders; a new risk → update the strategy's risk section (not the goals); a materialized risk → socialize and act on the new data. Maturity levels: 0 chaos; 1 reactive (post-mortems); 2 a list exists but isn't updated; 3 proactive (matrix, AATM, plan B, triggers, reviews); 4 systemic (map in rituals, threat modeling, alerts) — target level ≥ 3.

**How:** 1) list risks across all eight types (minimum one per type; sources: research, interviews, experience of similar launches, technical reviews, lawyers, support); per risk: what happens, how we'll notice (indicator), which strategy assumption it depends on; 2) mark cascades (seller churn → supply falls → conversion falls); 3) score probability and impact; convert impact to revenue; state the source and type (fact / assumption); 4) place in the matrix, determine the zone; 5) choose the AATM reaction (justify accept by mitigation cost; for transfer name the counterparty and terms); 6) describe mitigation for red and orange: method, cost, deadline, owner, residual risk; 7) plan B for red: trigger metric, threshold and its justification, action, decision owner, reaction time; 8) agree via DACI + the agreements table; put dependencies into the roadmap; 9) schedule rituals and a review date (at least quarterly).

**Pitfalls:** "no risks" or "all average" — no focus and no trust; risks without scores and plan B; risks written but never updated; treating mitigation as mandatory — some risks are cheaper to accept; a trigger threshold off the ceiling (30%/40%) without economics; a regional pilot on a single region; damage assessed in different units (reputation, conversion, money) without conversion to one metric; requesting resources without a reserve; stakeholders learning of the risk at the moment it materializes; a list of only internal risks (legal, market and fraud forgotten).
