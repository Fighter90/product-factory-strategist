# Customer Journey Map: As-Is, To-Be, Barriers and Opportunities (EN summary)

**When:** seeing at which journey stage the client loses the result and why — before choosing what to fix or build; attaching metrics (CSAT, drop-rate) to concrete steps; building a common team language. Without a map the team sees the product in fragments and ideas smear over the surface instead of hitting bottlenecks.

**Core:** CJM = a visual map of the client's path from the emergence of a need to repeat use. Don't confuse: User Flow — steps inside an interface (clicks, fields, screens), no emotions; BPMN — a process with roles, systems and wait times, the user is one "player" among many; CJM — human experience: goals, expectations, thoughts, emotions, barriers; without the emotional line it's just a chronology. As-is = the experience as it is now, built strictly on facts; to-be = the target experience after changes — every claim is a hypothesis until validated on people. Layers (course template): lifecycle stage (awareness → search → comparison → choice → purchase → usage → loyalty), client actions (no atomic clicks), touchpoints (site, app, push, support), goals and expectations, thoughts and questions, emotions (a word or smiley — the feeling is the point), barriers/drivers, step importance, actual experience (fast/long/inconvenient), stage metric (CSAT, drop-rate, Time-to-X), recommendations (next to the pain), illustrations. Course rule: one fact — one source link; an emotion without a quote or metric is "fake pain". Barrier = a step where the path breaks: conversion drop, back-navigation, pause >3s, a spoken question ("is this really where?"), an unexplained error, a workaround, a support call. Anxiety = an emotional dip with normal actions (the client did everything right but isn't sure it worked — typically no confirmation or status). Opportunity = a goal-critical step with low experience (high importance, low experience); a mass workaround (people invented their own replacement); an underserved job from an importance×satisfaction survey; a step where a competitor is clearly better. Pattern, not accident: one observation is an incident; repetition by two or more people is a pattern candidate — confirm with a metric. Barrier record: Stage | Description (what is seen/heard) | Source and n | Metric | Impact | Cause hypothesis | What to check.

**How (as-is):** frame — one segment, one scenario, one client job, path start and end; one role per map; data — at least two source types: "what they say" (interviews, surveys, support) + "what they do" (analytics, sessions, logs, diaries, observation, dogfooding); 6–7 macro stages max (at 10+ the map becomes a list; atomic actions only when needed); fill the layers for each stage with a source tag in every cell ([quote n=…], [metric], [support], [observation]; no source → [hypothesis], visually distinct); a metric under each barrier (stage CSAT, drop-rate, time) — without numbers the map stays a poster; mark pains where at least two signals coincide (metric dip, negative quotes, observed error/stop, support calls); prioritize by Pain × Importance (× segment reach) or dot voting in a workshop (3 dots per person); write hypotheses for the top-3 barriers: Job Story + metric (baseline → target shift) + owner + rough effort estimate; fix version, date, owner, quarterly revision (or after a major release). 40-minute express workshop (roles: facilitator, PM, analyst, designer, developer; input: 2–3 fresh interviews, dashboard on key stages, top-10 support tickets): 0–5 goal, key metric, rules → 5–15 stages and 1–2 actions each → 15–25 pains and emotions (red/blue stickers) → 25–30 dot vote → 30–35 hypotheses per pain (Job Story, metric, shift) → 35–40 owners, rough estimates, check date. To-be: take as-is top barriers, 1–3 change options each; redraw affected stages with expected emotion and expected metric, every cell marked "hypothesis" until checked; verify key changes on a prototype and/or experiment; update the map with facts and close hypotheses confirmed/refuted. Metrics link: stage CSAT (short post-action survey), process metrics (Time-to-X, drop-rate), alerts on deviations; the key value stage ties to the product's main metric; compare the effect before/after in one window and one segment.

**Pitfalls:** a map drawn inside the team from assumptions (it confirms own hypotheses — rely on user stories, quotes, data); mixed roles (client + courier + warehouse = you built a BPMN; one person — one map); no emotions (a checklist of actions, not experience — add the emotional line with quotes); steps too granular (100 cells, no insight — macro stages, details into User Flow); missed "annoying but minor" moments (exactly where people leave — record them too); no metrics (a pretty picture — a stage metric under every top barrier); a map detached from the backlog (dies after the release race — link pain → ticket); to-be presented as fact (false confidence — all to-be cells are hypotheses until checked); a stale map (>6 months — it reflects a past product; regular revision).
