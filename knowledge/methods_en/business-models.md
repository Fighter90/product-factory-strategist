# Business Model, PLG/MLG/SLG and Three Lenses of Innovation (EN summary)

**When:** "which business model and growth engine to choose (PLG or SLG, subscription or commission)?"; "is the innovation viable?"; "how do product and business strategy connect?"; "how to defend the project to leadership?".

**Core:** three growth engines. PLG (product led): acquisition via virality, organic, low time-to-value; freemium/trial → felt value → self-serve purchase; retention through in-product habit; fits low price, mass audience, value visible in use; PM does deep discovery and research-based roadmap. MLG (marketing led): triggers, discounts, campaigns, USP; product supplies conversion; retention via reminders; fits mass B2C/FMCG where value needs explaining. SLG (sales led): direct outreach to decision-makers; demo, trial, contract; retention via success manager and QBRs; fits B2B/enterprise, high deal size, customization. Strategy linkage: business inherits product strategy (PLG/tech businesses) or product inherits business strategy (MLG/SLG), or a unified cascade: Revenue → Conversion → DAU → Retention; good synergy example (Miro): PLG + bottom-up B2B + virality. Business models: Retailer (margin), Subscription (MRR/ARR, requires LTV > CAC), Freemium (free→paid conversion), Pay-Per-Use (price × volume), Commission (% of deal), Advertising (CPM/CPC/CPA), Revenue Share; plus white label, licensing, franchise, razor & blades, data monetization, retainer; big companies combine (classifieds: subscription + freemium + pay-per-use + commission + ads + revenue share). Lifecycle: idea/launch → growth → maturity (defend share, optimize) → decline or new cycle; logic of the stage defines both growth model and monetization. "Death valley": the product grows but doesn't yet self-finance — users exist, revenue doesn't, costs rise; survive by reaching breakeven early and confirming willingness to pay; growth without a working economic model is an illusion (each user at negative UE deepens the loss). Value/price balance: at start give far more value than you charge (≈100 vs 5), raising the captured share as you mature. Growth-framework skeletons: AARRR, HEART (Google), Flywheel (Zoom: built-in virality through participants), ROSE — same journey stages with metrics, choose per product type. Two-sided markets: classified (sides matched, transaction off-platform) vs marketplace (on-platform transaction: more operational complexity but quality control and commission); the value loop closes via network effect; cold start — begin with one side, subsidize it, focus geographically. Three Lenses of Innovation — an idea is viable only passing all three: Desirability (do users want it; which segments; what brings them back), Feasibility (can we build it; expertise, capacity, dependencies), Viability (sustainable business; who pays; does the economics converge; why now). Business functions: Tech, Marketing, Sales ("why change / why us / why now"), Support, Finance/Ops.

**How:** (1) growth model — answer: price and deal size? audience scale? self-serve possible? value visible in use? who buys (user or decision-maker)? match to the "when it fits" columns; a mixed answer = hybrid, describe each engine's role; (2) business model — what do people pay for and who pays; pick 1–2 models; write the revenue formula; (3) compatibility (4 fits): product↔market (PMF, retention), product↔channel (fast growers often get 70%+ of clients through one channel), channel↔model (viral freemium vs enterprise sales), model↔market (size, WTP, competitors); (4) three lenses — verdict per lens "passed / partial / failed" with 2–3 facts and an A–D evidence grade; Viability must be backed by market sizing and unit economics; (5) function map — who owns what under the chosen engine, dependencies on Tech/Sales/Support; (6) strategy synergy — which strategy is inherited, how goals cascade; (7) defend the project via the six-question template: customer problem (segments, value proposition, addressable market), benefit to the business, who approved (stakeholders, concerns, commitments), risks (blocking/non-blocking + mitigation), success criteria (hypotheses, method, deadlines), resources (team, budget, dependencies).

**Pitfalls:** PLG for a high-price product with a decision-maker (or SLG for mass self-serve); Viability described in words without unit economics and market size; a lens "passed" by team opinion rather than user data; product and business strategies unaligned — different focus metrics with no common cascade; scaling growth without matching standards (the IE example: growth without standards lost the market).
