# Business model, PLG / MLG / SLG, and the three lenses of innovation

## When to use
"Which business model and growth model to choose (PLG or SLG, subscription or commission)?", "is the innovation viable?", "how to connect product strategy and business strategy?", "how to defend the project before leadership?".

## Core idea
### Three growth models
| | PLG (Product Led) | MLG (Marketing Led) | SLG (Sales Led) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main engine | product | marketing | sales department |
| Acquisition | virality, organic, low time-to-value | triggers, discounts, campaigns, USP | reaching decision-makers directly |
| Monetization | freemium/trial → value realization → purchase; the customer decides themselves | positive image + lowering the barrier; the product provides CR | demo, trial period, contract; a human shapes the desire |
| Retention | habit through the product | reminder triggers | success manager, QBR, regular contact |
| When it fits | low price, mass audience, self-serve, value visible in use | mass B2C, FMCG, value not obvious without explanation | B2B/Enterprise, high deal size, customization |
| Product manager's role | deep discovery, research-driven roadmap | conversion to purchase, marketing tools | top-of-mind features, signals from Sales |

### Connecting business and product strategies
The business inherits the product's strategy (PLG, technology businesses) · the product inherits the business's strategy (Sales/Marketing Led) · a single strategy (a mature cascading culture). Goal cascade: Revenue → Conversion → DAU → Retention — different focal metrics, aligned top-level goals. Good synergy (Miro): PLG + bottom-up B2B + virality.

### Business models
| Model | How it earns | When applicable |
|---|---|---|
| Retailer | margin (price − procurement) | physical goods, logistics |
| Subscription | MRR/ARR, need LTV > CAC | long-term relationships, recurring value |
| Freemium | free → paid conversion | low unit cost, scale, PLG |
| Pay-Per-Use | price × volume | varying usage, B2B infrastructure |
| Commission | % of the transaction | intermediation |
| Advertising | CPM/CPC/CPA | large traffic, mass audience |
| Revenue Share | total revenue × share | a partner renders the service |
Additional: white label, licensing, franchise, razor & blades, data monetization, retainer. Large companies combine models (a classified ads service: subscription + freemium + pay-per-use + commission + advertising + revenue share).

### Lifecycle, "valley of death," and the growth model (the course's delta)
- Stages: idea/launch (innovators tolerate a raw product) → growth → maturity (defending share, optimization) → decline or a new wave (different monetization, new segment). The stage's logic determines both the growth model and monetization: a mistake is applying the logic of the wrong stage.
- **The "valley of death"** — the period when the product grows but does not yet finance itself: users exist, revenue doesn't, costs grow. Survival strategy: reach break-even as early as possible (confirm willingness to pay), do not count on an endless flow of investment. Growth without an economic model is an illusion: every new user with a negative UE increases the loss.
- The "value/price" balance: at the start the product gives away more value than it takes in money (100 vs 5); as it matures the share of extracted value grows — otherwise you stay stuck in an unprofitable state.
- **Product model frameworks (lesson 2):** AARRR (a simple funnel, e-com), HEART (Google: Happiness/Engagement/Adoption/Retention/Task Success — UX focus), Flywheel (Stranger→Onlooker→Beginner→Regular→Champion, closed through champion-referrers — B2B/high LTV, the Zoom case: built-in virality through participants), ROSE (segment, value proposition, North Star, wow magnets, acquisition/activation, Product DNA: retention + monetization + referral). The connection with CJM is direct: these are the same journey stages with metrics and goals. Choose by product type and goal — see [[M-growth-frameworks]].
- **Two-sided markets:** classified (we connect the sides, the transaction happens off-platform) vs marketplace (the transaction runs on the platform: more operational complexity — schedules, closing documents — but quality control and the possibility of a commission). The value cycle closes through a network effect (more job seekers → more responses → more employers → more vacancies); launching is "chicken and egg": start with one side, subsidize it, focus geographically.

### Three Lenses of Innovation
An idea is viable if it passes all three lenses simultaneously:
- **Desirability** — do users want it? in which segments is the value maximal? what makes them come back?
- **Feasibility** — can we build it? is there expertise and capacity? what do we depend on?
- **Viability** — sustainable value for the business? who pays? does the economics converge? why now?

### Business functions
Tech (implementability, scale, data infrastructure) · Marketing (demand, packaging, market signals) · Sales ("why change / why us / why now") · Support (delivering the promised value, feedback) · Finance/Ops (budget, unit economics).

## Procedure
1. **Growth model.** Answer: price and deal-size level? audience size? is self-serve possible? is value visible in the usage itself? who is the buyer (user or decision-maker)? Matches with the "when it fits" column give PLG / MLG / SLG; a mixed answer — a hybrid, explicitly describe what each engine does.
2. **Business model.** Determine what people pay for and who pays ([[M-monetization]]); pick 1–2 models from the table; write the revenue formula.
3. **Compatibility (the 4 fits from product-context-strategy):** product ↔ market (PMF, retention), product ↔ channel (fast-growing companies often get 70%+ of customers through one channel), channel ↔ model (viral freemium or enterprise sales), model ↔ market (size, WTP, competitors).
4. **Three lenses:** for each — a verdict "passed / partially / failed," 2–3 facts and an **evidence class A–D** ([[M-source-criticism]]). Viability must be backed by calculation: market size ([[M-market-sizing]]) and unit economics ([[M-unit-economics]]).
5. **Function map:** who is responsible for what under the chosen growth model; dependencies on Tech/Sales/Support.
6. **Strategy synergy:** which strategy is inherited, how goals cascade.
7. **Project defense** by the six-question template.

## Template / checklist
**Six defense questions:**
1. Customer problem — segments, value proposition, addressable market.
2. Benefit to the business — alignment with strategy, impact, costs.
3. Who approved — stakeholders, concerns, commitments.
4. Risks — blocking/non-blocking, mitigation plan ([[M-risks]]).
5. Success criteria — hypotheses, verification method, timeframes.
6. Resources — team, budget, dependencies.

Three lenses:

## Common mistakes
- Choosing PLG for a product with a high deal size and a decision-maker (or SLG for mass self-serve).
- Describing Viability in words without unit economics and market size.
- Counting a lens as passed by the team's opinion rather than user data.
- Not aligning product and business strategy (different focal metrics without a common cascade).
- An example of bad synergy from the skill (IE): large-scale growth without meeting standards led to losing the market.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Who makes the purchase decision: the user, a manager, or procurement?
- Can the user reach the value without a human?
- What is the sales cycle and average deal size — does it cover the cost of sales?
- Which lens is weakest now, and is it cheap to verify?
- "Why now": what changed in the external environment ([[M-external-environment]])?
