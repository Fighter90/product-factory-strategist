# Business model, PLG / MLG / SLG and the Three Lenses of Innovation

## When to apply
"Which business model and growth engine should we choose (PLG or SLG, subscription or commission)?", "is the innovation viable?", "how do the product strategy and the business strategy connect?", "how do I defend the project to leadership?".

## Core idea
### Three growth engines
| | PLG (Product Led) | MLG (Marketing Led) | SLG (Sales Led) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main engine | the product | marketing | the sales department |
| Acquisition | virality, organic, low time-to-value | triggers, discounts, campaigns, USP | reaching decision-makers directly |
| Monetization | freemium/trial → realizing the value → purchase; the client decides themselves | a positive image + lowering the barrier; the product provides the CR | demo, trial period, contract; a person shapes the desire |
| Retention | habit built through the product | reminder triggers | success manager, QBR, regular contacts |
| When it fits | low price, mass audience, self-serve, the value is visible in use | mass B2C, FMCG, the value is not obvious without an explanation | B2B/Enterprise, high deal size, customization |
| Role of the PM | deep discovery, a research-based roadmap | conversion to purchase, marketing tools | top-of-mind features, signals from Sales |

### How business and product strategies connect
The business inherits the product strategy (PLG, tech-driven businesses) · the product inherits the business strategy (Sales/Marketing Led) · a single strategy (a mature culture of cascading). The cascade of goals: Revenue → Conversion → DAU → Retention — different focus metrics aligned by top-level goals. Good synergy (Miro): PLG + bottom-up B2B + virality.

### Business models
| Model | How it earns money | When it applies |
|---|---|---|
| Retailer | margin (price − purchase cost) | physical goods, logistics |
| Subscription | MRR/ARR, requires LTV > CAC | long-term relationships, recurring value |
| Freemium | free → paid conversion | low unit cost, scale, PLG |
| Pay-Per-Use | price × volume | varying usage, B2B infrastructure |
| Commission | % of the transaction | intermediation |
| Advertising | CPM/CPC/CPA | large traffic, mass audience |
| Revenue Share | total revenue × share | a partner delivers the service |
Additional ones: white label, licensing, franchise, razor & blades, data monetization, retainer. Large companies combine models (a classifieds service: subscription + freemium + pay-per-use + commission + advertising + revenue share).

### Lifecycle, "death valley" and the growth model (course delta)
- Stages: idea/launch (innovators tolerate a raw product) → growth → maturity (defending share, optimization) → decline or a new wave (a different monetization, a new segment). The logic of the stage determines both the growth model and the monetization: the mistake is applying the logic of the wrong stage.
- **"Death valley"** — the period when the product grows but does not yet finance itself: there are users, there is no revenue, costs are rising. The survival strategy: reach breakeven as early as possible (confirm the willingness to pay) and don't count on an endless inflow of investment. Growth without an economic model is an illusion: every new user at negative unit economics increases the loss.
- The "value/price" balance: at the start the product gives away more value than it takes in money (100 versus 5); as it matures the share of captured value grows — otherwise you stay stuck in an unprofitable state.
- **Frameworks of the product model (session 2):** AARRR (a simple funnel, e-commerce), HEART (Google: Happiness/Engagement/Adoption/Retention/Task Success — a UX focus), Flywheel (Stranger→Onlooker→Beginner→Regular→Champion, closed through champion-referrers — B2B/high LTV; the Zoom case: built-in virality through participants), ROSE (segment, value proposition, North Star, wow magnets, acquisition/activation, Product DNA: retention + monetization + referral). The link with CJM is direct: these are the same journey stages with metrics and goals. Choose by product type and goal — see [[M-growth-frameworks]].
- **Two-sided markets:** classifieds (it brings the sides together, the transaction happens off the platform) vs marketplace (the transaction happens on the platform: more operational complexity — schedules, closing documents — but quality control and the possibility of a commission). The value loop is closed by a network effect (more job seekers → more responses → more employers → more vacancies); launch is a "chicken and egg" problem: start with one side, subsidize it, focus geographically.

### Three Lenses of Innovation
An idea is viable if it passes all three lenses simultaneously:
- **Desirability** — do users want it? in which segments is the value highest? what makes them come back?
- **Feasibility** — can we build it? is there the expertise and the capacity? what do we depend on?
- **Viability** — a sustainable value for the business? who pays? does the economics work out? why now?

### Functions of the business
Tech (implementability, scale, data infrastructure) · Marketing (demand, packaging, market signals) · Sales ("why change / why us / why now") · Support (delivering the promised value, feedback) · Finance/Ops (budget, unit economics).

## Procedure
1. **Growth model.** Answer: what is the price level and the deal size? how large is the audience? is self-serve possible? is the value visible in the use itself? who is the buyer (the user or the decision-maker)? Matches with the "when it fits" column give PLG / MLG / SLG; a mixed answer means a hybrid — describe explicitly what each engine does.
2. **Business model.** Determine what people pay for and who pays ([[M-monetization]]); choose 1–2 models from the table; write down the revenue formula.
3. **Compatibility (the 4 fits from product-context-strategy):** product ↔ market (PMF, retention), product ↔ channel (fast-growing companies often get 70%+ of clients through one channel), channel ↔ model (viral freemium or enterprise sales), model ↔ market (size, WTP, competitors).
4. **Three lenses:** for each one, a verdict "passed / partially / failed", 2–3 facts and an **evidence grade A–D** ([[M-source-criticism]]). Viability must be backed by a calculation: market size ([[M-market-sizing]]) and unit economics ([[M-unit-economics]]).
5. **Function map:** who is responsible for what under the chosen growth model; dependencies on Tech/Sales/Support.
6. **Synergy of strategies:** which strategy is inherited, how goals cascade.
7. **Defend the project** using the six-question template.

## Template / checklist
**Six questions for the defense:**
1. The customer's problem — segments, value proposition, addressable market.
2. The benefit to the business — alignment with the strategy, impact, costs.
3. Who approved it — stakeholders, concerns, commitments.
4. Risks — blocking/non-blocking, the mitigation plan ([[M-risks]]).
5. Success criteria — hypotheses, validation method, deadlines.
6. Resources — team, budget, dependencies.

## Typical mistakes
- Choosing PLG for a product with a high deal size and a decision-maker (or SLG for mass self-serve).
- Describing Viability in words without unit economics and market size.
- Treating a lens as passed based on the team's opinion rather than user data.
- Failing to align the product strategy with the business strategy (different focus metrics with no common cascade).
- An example of poor synergy from the skill (IE): large-scale growth without matching the standards led to losing the market.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Who makes the purchase decision: the user, a manager, or procurement?
- Can the user reach the value without a human in the loop?
- What is the sales cycle and the average deal size — does it cover the cost of sales?
- Which lens is the weakest right now, and is it cheap to test?
- "Why now": what has changed in the external environment ([[M-external-environment]])?
