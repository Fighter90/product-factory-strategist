# DACI and the Decision Journal (EN summary)

**When:** assigning who on and around the team does what and who decides at each product stage; recording a concrete decision so that a quarter later you can reconstruct why it was made, who is accountable, and under which conditions to revisit it.

**Core:** DACI — a role matrix for decisions and activities: **D — Driver** (drives the activity/decision to the result, gathers inputs, pushes for deadlines); **A — Approver** (approves, has the last word; ideally one person); **C — Contributor** (brings expertise and work, shapes the content); **I — Informed** (learns the result, no influence). The decision matrix is part of the product perimeter artifacts (next to the stakeholder map and dependency list); responsibility ≠ control — some decisions are made outside the perimeter and the Approver may sit outside the team. Role distribution per the Avito Design template: for problem-hypothesis search the PM drives research/competitor scans, stakeholder talks, user interviews, hypothesis formulation (researcher, designer, analyst contribute); analyzing current CJM and flows — designer drives (PM informed). Problem validation: cross-team sync — PM; Voice-of-Customer analysis — researcher; qualitative and quantitative studies — researcher; analytical validation — analyst; sizing, prioritization, and metric definition (primary, proxy, counter) — PM. Solution design: PM drives solution-hypothesis generation, feature potential, prioritization, user-value formulation, final scenario/edge-case description, legal sign-off, launch planning; designer drives draft mockups and final visuals/copy; researcher drives prototypes and UX tests; analyst drives logging design and the A/B experiment; for rollout: developer drives delivery and rollout, **analyst runs and interprets the experiment**, PM decides on rollout; researcher analyzes feedback; PM runs demos. Conclusion: the PM is the driver of prioritization, metrics, approvals and the rollout call — but not of A/B interpretation (that's the analyst) and not of coding; an empty Approver column in the template is a signal that an Approver must be assigned explicitly for every decision. The decision journal (decision record) is the product's unit of memory; the course basis: decision memo (goal/ask, problem, solution, PMF criteria, risks, resources, "which decision is needed"), risk log (probability/impact, owner, SLA, mitigator), post-mortem (hypothesis → metric/MDE → fact → causes → actions). Principle: a post-mortem without action items is a diary; a decision without an owner and a revision condition is an opinion.

**How:** (1) state the decision in one sentence (choosing what between what) and type it: reversible/irreversible, error cost, deadline; (2) assign roles: one Driver, one Approver, a Contributor list, Informed; verify there's no second driver, D and A don't overlap, and the Approver actually has budget/priority authority; (3) gather inputs and split them: facts (source, date, period), assumptions/hypotheses, forecasts (scenarios); list alternatives including "do nothing"; (4) fix decision criteria (metrics, thresholds, budget, deadline) and counter-metrics before choosing; (5) the Approver decides; record the chosen option, the rejected options, and the reasons; (6) write revision conditions: which metric values, events (missed TTM corridor, red risk, market or constraint change) or dates reopen the decision, and who initiates the revisit; (7) assign actions (owner, deadline, definition of done); update the roadmap and the risk log; (8) notify Informed; on the check-in date compare fact vs expectation (post-mortem) and update the record: confirmed / not confirmed / revised.

**Pitfalls:** no Approver (column empty) or Approver = "everyone"; two drivers on one activity; roles tied to job titles instead of decisions (different decisions need different Approvers); the decision recorded without alternatives and rejection reasons — logic unrecoverable a quarter later; no revision condition (the decision becomes dogma or gets revisited on mood); the PM interpreting A/B results instead of the analyst (or the analyst approving a product decision); Contributors added "just in case"; Informed substituting real approval; post-mortems as blame-seeking without actions or assumption updates; decisions made verbally in a meeting and never recorded.
