# Forecasting and Traction Analytics (EN summary)

**When:** deciding "is the idea worth investment", setting goals for a period, planning budget/load, or answering honestly "what happens to the metric if we do nothing" — that's the baseline, without which an initiative's effect cannot be computed. A forecast is not a promise: it is a scenario with assumptions, always checkable, always shown with an interval.

**Core:** Two approaches. Extrapolation (time series) extends history: trend + seasonality + noise; works with deep stable history (12–24 months) and unbroken market rules — the basis for baseline. Driver model (traction) assembles the metric from parts (Revenue = PU × TPU × AOV), each part a driver with a rationale and source; works with short history, changing rules, or when the effect of a specific initiative is needed. Best practice is a hybrid: baseline from the series, initiative drivers on top; pure "as-is" extrapolation across horizons with product/market changes is a classic error. Series components: trend (long-term direction, e.g. the category grows 15% YoY); seasonality (repeating pattern with a known period — daily, weekly, monthly salary peaks, annual December-up/January-down; recognizable seasonality is not a product problem); noise (random fluctuation — why a "+3" week at an average of 20 can be luck); anomalies (one-off spikes: campaigns, outages, A/B tests, external events — removed or replaced before modeling, otherwise the model learns them as law). Baseline = the forecast of metric behavior without the initiative; NOT a forecast with the feature, not the PM's opinion, not last year's analytics — a pure forecast on historical data. Without it you can't separate feature effect from season ("PU falls 20% every February; with the feature it fell 15% — success, not failure") or compute uplift. Build: collect 12–24 months history at day/week granularity with segment cuts → remove anomalies (campaigns, big A/Bs, one-off promos, spikes) → model per segment by increasing complexity: seasonal naive (value = last period × seasonal factor) → Prophet/ARIMA/ETS → gradient boosting with features when many external factors; where the model underfits seasonality, force it: baseline[t] = trend + last-year seasonality + adjustments for other products → verify the baseline "holds" (visual check, backtest on recent months, residuals without systematic bias, prediction intervals; if it "jumps", redo) → always segment (new/old users, categories, platforms, plan types): the same initiative can lift PU in one segment and crush ARPPU in another; an aggregate baseline hides this. Traction gate model: Gate 0 idea (days) → Gate 1 back-of-envelope traction (logic check: drivers, order of magnitude; precision not required) → Gate 2 detailed model (analyst + PM, days–weeks) → Gate 3 validated by A/B facts after the pilot. Detailed model contents: (1) target metric (Revenue/PU/ARPPU/GMV/retention per feature); (2) baseline; (3) mandatory segmentation; (4) drivers — factor decomposition of the target (Revenue = PU × TPU × AOV; ARPPU = TPU × AOV) with a per-driver forecast and rationale; (5) uplift per driver with a source (past launches, other teams' A/Bs, market benchmarks, UX research, expert estimate), each assumption carrying a confidence level (e.g. 50–100%) and status (proven/hypothesis); (6) rollout schedule by month (10% → 30% → 60% → 100%) — the effect stretches over time ("January profit ≠ June profit"); a timing slip shifts the whole forecast; (7) final forecast = baseline + uplift × rollout share, monthly, with cannibalization and cross-launch intersections; plus logical checks (bottom-up must not pierce the top-down market cap), sensitivity on 2–3 main assumptions, base/optimistic/pessimistic scenarios. Accuracy: backtest (train until T, predict after T, compare with fact); MAE (metric units), MAPE = mean(|fact − forecast|/fact) × 100% for positive metrics, RMSE (punishes big misses); business-forecast target MAPE < 20% (strong < 10%), the key bar being error below the economically meaningful effect the forecast must catch; residuals (fact − forecast) must have no trend/seasonality; always show an interval, never a point; after launch reconcile fact vs baseline vs forecast regularly. Baseline from history cannot "lie like an opinion" — it is a checkable statement ("what would have happened without us"), objective by construction. For a new product with no history, extrapolation is impossible: the "forecast" becomes scenarios with assumptions (top-down from the market, bottom-up from unit economics, three scenarios instead of baseline) — presenting them as a forecast is an error. A forecast is useless/harmful when: the horizon exceeds assumption stability (rules of the game change), one unverified premise carries everything, accuracy is unmeasured, intervals are hidden (long horizon = wide interval — show the narrowing confidence honestly). A forecast without an owner, assumptions and fact-vs-plan reconciliation dates is a picture, not a tool: assumptions visible, each with a responsible person, reconciliation scheduled by dates.

**How:** (1) define question and horizon; check history ≥12 months and rule stability — this picks the approach; (2) collect and clean history (anomalies, duplicates, outages), choose granularity and segments; (3) build baseline: seasonal naive → time-series model → force last-year seasonality; validate with backtest and residuals; compute MAPE/MAE; (4) decompose the target metric into drivers; for each driver state uplift, source and confidence level; (5) overlay uplift on baseline with the rollout curve; account for cannibalization and intersections; check against the market cap; (6) build base/optimistic/pessimistic scenarios and sensitivity on the 2–3 main assumptions; (7) present the forecast with intervals and assumptions; agree control: dates for fact vs baseline vs forecast reconciliation and the revision trigger.

**Pitfalls:** "extend the line" instead of a seasonality-aware baseline; December's peak booked as initiative success (or January's dip as "the feature killed the product"); anomalies and campaigns not cleaned out — the model learned them; a point forecast without interval; scenarios replaced by "the most likely" number; uplift from thin air — no source, no confidence level, no cannibalization; one aggregate baseline instead of segmented; rollout ignored — a 100%-audience effect attributed to month one; no backtest, no MAPE; nobody ever reconciles fact with forecast; extrapolation "by analogy" for a new product passed off as a forecast.
