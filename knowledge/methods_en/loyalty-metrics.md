# Loyalty and Customer Metrics (EN summary)

**When:** measuring how customers perceive the product: satisfaction with a specific step, how easy a task is, willingness to recommend, interface usability. The card picks the metric for the question and shows formulas, collection methods, benchmarks and interpretation traps.

**Core:** experience metrics are an early signal for money — investments in customer experience raise revenue over time; clients return and pay for good experience. They belong to the *attitudinal* layer and complement *behavioral* metrics (DAU/MAU, conversion, retention, load time): behavior says **what** happens, attitude says **why** and how much it hurts. Three questions before launching any metric: which business KPI it explains; what we'll do if it falls or rises; do we have resources to react at the required frequency — no answer → choose another metric or lower the frequency.

Formulas: **CSAT** = ratings 4–5 / all responses × 100 (or mean score; scale 1–5/7/10) — satisfaction with a specific interaction, stage or feature; survey right after a deal, support contact, payment; simple and automatable but mood-dependent, not long-term loyalty. **CES** = share of "easy" answers (on a 1–5 scale where 1 = "extremely easy", count ratings 1–2) × 100, or mean score (lower is better) — task ease; survey immediately after onboarding, payment, listing, long forms (KYC); finds friction points, correlates with retention, needs segmentation. **NPS** = % promoters (9–10) − % critics (0–6), range −100…+100, passives (7–8) excluded; willingness to recommend; for regular brand-loyalty tracking and benchmarks; vulnerable to inflation and cultural/regional differences, doesn't assess individual steps. **SUS**: 10 statements, 1–5; odd items score response − 1, even items 5 − response; sum × 2.5 → 0–100 points (not %); 68 = average usability; minimum sample ≈ 20 respondents. **UMUX**: SUS-like logic, 4 items, 1–7, normalized to 0–100; **UMUX-LITE**: 2 items, (sum − 2)/12 × 100 — when the respondent can't be loaded. **SUPR-Q**: 7 items (usability, trust/reliability, appearance, loyalty) + 1 NPS item; sum of 7 + (NPS item / 2), all / 8; site/app diagnostics with percentile benchmarks (raw score vs someone else's percentile can't be compared). Benchmarks (compare first with your own history and competitors): CSAT red < 70%, grey 70–80%, green > 80% for mass B2C (finance/insurance higher: 75 / 75–85 / 85+); CES "easy" share red < 50%, grey 50–65%, green > 65% (mean-score: red > 3.0, grey 2.5–3.0, green < 2.5); NPS red < 0, grey 0…+30, green > +30 (+40 for finance and premium). Evidence strength: an experience metric is a measurement of opinion on a specific sample and channel — it shows level and dynamics, not cause or guaranteed behavior; a drop coinciding with a product change stays a hypothesis until seasonality and sample/channel shifts are excluded.

Sampling and schedule ("rolling sample"): for CSAT/CES — a random 5–10% of completed operations daily (survey fatigue), full coverage only for rare events; for NPS — by schedule (e.g. quarterly on actives) plus a wave to dormant users (>90 days without actions) and a measurement after critical incidents; cooldown ≈ 14 days between repeated surveys of the same user; if refusal grows among veterans, stratify (novices surveyed more often). The channel changes both the sample and the number: the same question via push and by phone gives different results; use two channels for key metrics and record the channel in the data. Required precision: 95% CI for a share ≈ ±9.8pp at n=100, ±5.7pp at n=300, ±3.1pp at n=1000; NPS is wider (two shares combined): n=300 ≈ ±9–10 points, n=100 ≈ ±16; a 3–5-point NPS change on a small sample is not a signal.

**How:** 1) formulate the question and metric: stage/event → CSAT or CES; overall loyalty → NPS; interface usability → SUS/UMUX(-LITE); complex site diagnosis → SUPR-Q; one survey = one goal. 2) Define trigger and moment: CSAT/CES immediately after **successful** completion (within hours, not after errors); NPS by schedule and after significant incidents. 3) Choose the channel and sampling share (≈5–10% of traffic). 4) Write the question: one thought, scale direction explicitly labeled, event context named ("order", "listing"); after the rating add the open "What influenced your rating?". 5) Fix before launch: alarm threshold, reaction owner, reaction deadline, action on a drop, analysis segments (novices/experienced, device, region, channel). 6) Collect with an identifier (user-id/session) to link with behavior. 7) Report with confidence intervals, dynamics and segmentation; compare with the previous wave and competitors, not "abstract norms". 8) Link to the backlog: red zone → ticket with owner → re-check after release → close or re-study. 9) Quarterly metrics retro: update thresholds, frequency, channels; count respondent load; correlate ΔCSAT/NPS/CES with repeat orders, CAC, activation — correlation < 0.3 → rethink the metric or measurement point; move thresholds slowly: `new threshold = min(3-month mean − ½σ; current threshold + 3pp)`. Survey-health dashboard: email open rate > 25%; completion > 60% pop-up / > 40% email; survey fatigue < 2%; share of alerts driven to action ≥ 70%; also track the share of CSAT critics (ratings 1–2) — a rising negativity share is an earlier signal than a falling mean. SLA examples: CSAT below red (3-day rolling) → backlog ticket ≤ 8h; CES mean > 3.0 → analysis and quick-fix prototype ≤ 3 days; NPS −5pp QoQ → cause and plan at the quarterly review.

**Pitfalls:** a metric for its own sake — no KPI, threshold, owner or reaction plan; mixing metrics (NPS brand loyalty vs CSAT touchpoint satisfaction; CSAT for "the app overall" — that's SUS/UMUX; NPS per micro-moment); no open "why" question (a promo can temporarily mask the delivery pain); unlabeled or reversed scale direction — check platform labels before computing, fix the interpretation in the metric passport; surveying after errors or before scenario completion (CES must follow successful completion); biased sampling (only actives, only the happy, one channel); averages without segments; treating an ordinal-scale mean as an exact number and small-sample comparisons without intervals; survey fatigue and incentivized answers; a lone NPS value without a benchmark ("is +30 good?" depends on industry and segment); importing western norms (SUS 68, NPS 50) without adjustment.
