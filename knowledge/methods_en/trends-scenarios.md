# Trend analysis and scenario planning

## When to apply
"Where is the market heading?", "what will happen in 3–5 years?", "is it hype or a trend?", "which scenarios to embed in the strategy and the growth model?" The method distinguishes signals from hype and turns the future into a set of testable scenarios with signals and actions.

## Essence
| Concept | Definition |
|---|---|
| Signal | an early indicator, an anomaly, a "seed" of a trend |
| Hype | a spike of interest without a guarantee of durability (NFT, metaverse) |
| Trend | a stable sequence with a clear direction |
| Insight | the trend's impact on your product |
| Megatrend / driver | a fundamental force moving many trends (digitalization, demographics, climate) |
| Image of the future | an idea of the future given the trends' development |
Types by PESTLE: political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental ([[M-external-environment]]).

**The three horizons of strategy (McKinsey):** Run — 1–3 years (current profit, efficiency); Change — 2–5 years (future profit, adaptation); Disrupt — 3–10 years (breakthrough, new markets).

**When not to forecast:** the result does not influence the decision; the cost of a mistake is less than the cost of delay; the speed of change exceeds the speed of planning; the solution space is too complex (more than 3 variables).

### Six methods + a supplement
| Method | Essence | When to choose |
|---|---|---|
| Horizon scanning | hunting for signals: scientific papers, startups, patents, news | to see trends early, replenish the trend map |
| Delphi | anonymous rounds of expert surveys (usually 2–3), results shown between rounds → consensus | little data, an expert estimate of a parameter is needed |
| Scenario planning | 2–4 images of the future with their own logic ("what could be", not "what will be") | high uncertainty, a 3–10 year strategy |
| Diffusion models | adoption speed: Bass, Logistic, Gompertz; the S-curve "start → acceleration → plateau" | forecasting the adoption of a technology/product |
| Backcasting | from the desired future to the present ("if in 2030 we are the leader, what do we do now?") | a clear goal exists, a path is needed (the Amazon example — the future press release) |
| Prediction markets / crowd forecasting | trading outcomes, collective estimation (Metaculus, PredictIt) | cross-checking your own probabilities |
| System dynamics | delays and feedback loops (Forrester) | complex systems with lag |

## Procedure
### A. Scenario analysis, the simple method (10 steps)
1. Clarify the trend, the market, the horizon.
2. Express it in a measurable quantity (%, millions, an index).
3. Baseline: the current state and the stability trajectory.
4. The acceleration scenario (favorable conditions).
5. The rollback/stall scenario (unfavorable).
6. Drivers/triggers of each scenario.
7. Signals: what to observe to understand which scenario is unfolding.
8. Adaptation of the product and the strategy.
9. Split the actions: **no-regret moves** (under any outcome) and **option bets** (bets on an outcome).
10. Assess the product's current readiness.

### B. The 2×2 matrix
1. Choose two trends — the most influential and the least related to each other.
2. Axes — extreme values (High/Low) for each.
3. Four quadrants = four images of the future; give each a name and 3–5 signs.
4. Then steps 6–10 of the simple method for each quadrant (briefly).

### C. Telling a trend from hype (based on the skill's definitions)
Collect signals in a journal (source, date, credibility class per [[M-source-criticism]]) → cluster → check: are there several independent signals across different sources and periods, is direction and real adoption visible (money, behavior, regulation), rather than mere mentions. If not — leave it as a signal/hype under observation. If it is a trend → record the insight for the product.

### D. Delphi (a practical scheme)
Formulate a specific measurable question → recruit diverse experts → round 1: anonymous estimates and rationales → a summary (median, spread, arguments) → round 2 taking the summary into account → if needed round 3 → fix the final range and the dissenters. The result — a range for a model assumption, class no higher than B.

### E. Backcasting
Describe the desired state at a date, measurably → define the necessary conditions and capabilities at intermediate stages → compare with the present → a list of gaps and first steps → tie to the roadmap and the risks.

### F. Integration with the model and the strategy
Scenarios → three scenarios of the traction model (pessimistic/base/optimistic) and the external factors of effects ([[M-traction-model]]); signals → triggers for revisiting the roadmap; no-regret → into the nearest quarter; option bets → small bets with a stop criterion ([[M-risks]]). Diffusion (the standard logistic form, not from the skill): `N(t) = K / (1 + e^{−r(t−t0)})`, where K — the saturation ceiling, r — the speed, t0 — the inflection point; take the parameters from analogs with a credibility class.

### Scenario quality criteria
| Criterion | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Plausibility | plausible, based on facts |
| Internal consistency | no internal contradictions |
| Distinctiveness | the scenarios genuinely differ |
| Decision relevance | they influence decisions |
| Traceability | they trace back to drivers and signals |
| Testability | there are observable testable signals |

## Template / checklist
Passport: trend · horizon · market · measurable quantity · sources.
Trend map: signal | hype | trend | megatrend.
Scenarios:
| Scenario | Assumptions/drivers | Measurable quantity (value, year) | Indicator signals | No-regret actions | Option bets | Probability/credibility class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
Signal table: signal · source · date · what it means · reaction threshold.
Discipline: forecast regularly in small portions (monthly), prioritize significant trends, document hypotheses and check them after 3–6 months, run forecast retrospectives.

## Typical mistakes
- Taking hype for a trend based on the number of mentions.
- A single "forecast" instead of a set of scenarios; scenarios differing only in optimism.
- A horizon that is too long (beyond 10 years too much is unknown) without intermediate signals.
- Confirmation bias, overconfidence, survivorship bias, belief in authority; ignoring black swans.
- Scenarios not tied to decisions ("pictures of the future").
- No signals and no review dates.

## Questions that can change the decision
- Which decision changes if scenario A rather than B unfolds? If none — no forecast is needed.
- Which two factors determine the outcome most strongly, and how independent are they?
- What is cheaper for us: adapting to the scenario or influencing it?
- Which signals can we realistically track, and who is accountable?
- Which horizon is needed: Run, Change or Disrupt?
