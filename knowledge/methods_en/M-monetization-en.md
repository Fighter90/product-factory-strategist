# Monetization Models and Pricing (EN summary)

**When:** "how do we earn?"; "which model to choose and how to compute revenue?"; "what does the client pay for and how much?"; "does monetization fit the channel and the unit economics?"; "do we need an auction?" Monetization is a layer of the traction model, not an isolated choice.

**Core — maturity ladder** (mature models tie payment to value more precisely, give predictable revenue, lower the entry threshold — pay for results — and raise WTP): (1) **Pay-and-Pray** — Revenue = listings × listing price; (2) **CPV** — Σ views_i × CPV_i; (3) **TRX (commission)** — GMV × take rate, GMV = deals × AOV; (4) **CPA** — CPA = AOV × TR × CR action→payment; (5) **Subscription** — Subscribers × ARPU; MRR = payers × price; ARR = MRR × 12; (6) **Auction** (see auction section). Course case "Chairs" — the same 1,000 ₽/mo three ways: listing 1,000; 100 views × 10 ₽; 10 deals × 5,000 ₽ × 2% — equal totals, but with TRX the client pays nothing when there are no sales: lower entry barrier, higher loyalty. Compare models on the same client scenario. **Distributions, not averages** ("nothing happens on average — there are distributions"): Pay-and-Pray gives a wide distribution — a left tail (paid, got few deals → churns → retention falls) and a right tail (got many deals for a fixed fee → platform undercharged; because of it the left tail pays "dearly"); CPC narrows the distribution; transaction almost removes the tails (paid commission = got a deal). Check: build the client-outcome distribution, count the losing and "underpaid" clients, estimate left-tail loss = `N_losing × (1 − retention) × ARPU`. Four paid-value types (starting point): goods/service; access for a period (subscription); leads/deals (seller pays for clients); money management (fintech, insurance, lotteries). **Implicit take rate exists in every model**: implicit TR = platform payment / client revenue for the period (a 10,000 ₽/mo subscription at 200,000 ₽/mo revenue → TR 5%); auto lead-gen benchmark ~1% of the car's full price. Course formulas: `CPA = AOV × Take Rate × CR booking→payment`; `Revenue = 365 × DAU × OrderRate × CPA`; `LTV = Sessions_lifetime × OrderRate × CPA` with `Sessions_lifetime = Σ retention^n × stickiness` (accepted definition stickiness = DAU/MAU; the series 1 + r + r² + … gives expected lifetime months 1/(1−r) — check dimensionality). Sensitivity: CPA and LTV are linear in AOV and sessions; sessions (retention) is the main lever; the least reliable input is CR booking→payment, the most reliable AOV. Take-rate references (no source, grade D — use as hypotheses): marketplaces 5–20%, fintech 1–3%, taxi 20–30%.

**Value metric** — the unit of value the client pays for; drives WTP, perceived fairness and scalability: marketplace → deal/GMV (TRX); SaaS → user/month (subscription); ad platform → click/impression/action (CPC/CPM/CPA); cloud → resource consumption (pay-per-use); booking bot → appointment (CPA). Key metrics: ARPU = revenue / all users; ARPPU = revenue / payers; ARPU = ARPPU × CR free→paid. **Model-choice tree**: does the transaction pass through the platform (delivery, booking, escrow)? yes → transactional; no → is one listing one item? yes → Pay-and-Pray (used cars, secondary real estate); no (one listing = many units) → is there a clear pre-sale action? yes → CPA (new-builds: a call; jobs: an employee starting); no → CPC (clicks self-calibrate price to demand and geo). Freemium is risky for small products ("giving away free devalues"); works for platforms with network effects; check the funnel: 100,000 free → 3,000 trial → 900 paying — do 900 cover the cost of 100,000? **Pricing types**: suboptimal (startup/growth — headroom to raise under a bad scenario), optimal (maximum on the price×demand curve; mature #1), barrier (above optimum — soft squeeze during model migration, "we're not disabling it, it's just expensive"); raising price without creating value is a trap. Add-ons over the base model: bundling (hard/soft/status), adjacent products (apartment + insurance), promo mechanics (ML willingness-to-pay; antitrust caution; professionals are rational — promo targets private sellers), prepaid (money up front) vs postpaid (never miss sudden demand; needs scoring and limits). **Loyalty programs** — for retention, not acquisition: cashback with delayed crediting and expiry (return cycle, higher frequency), points with restricted spend categories, status tiers, paid ecosystem subscription bundle; constraint: cashback cannot exceed platform margin (cashback > 10% on a low-take-rate classified = loss); subsidized cashback as an acquisition bait breaks economics (arbitrage: buy with giant cashback → resell); measure loyalty effects in isolation — test group with points vs clean control.

**Auctions (short):** introduce when all three conditions hold — scarce resource, demand > supply, differing WTP; not suitable when: one buyer, user intolerance of ads, no real-time infrastructure, market too small. Formats: open (English), sealed-bid, Dutch (falling price). Multi-slot: sealed bids → slots by descending bid → payment rule: first-price (pay your bid), second-price/GSP (pay the next bid), VCG (pay the marginal value of the slot — the loss to others from your presence). VCG example: slots P1 100 clicks, P2 60; bids A 5, B 4, C 3 ₽/click → optimum A→P1 (500), B→P2 (240), C none; total 740; payment A = (B at P1 = 400 + C at P2 = 180) − 240 = **340 ₽**, payment B = (A = 500 + C = 180) − 500 = **180 ₽**, C pays 0; GSP comparison: A pays 4×100 = 400, B 3×60 = 180 — VCG makes truthful bidding optimal because the price depends on harm to others, not on your own bid. Metrics: monetary (revenue per auction, eCPM/eCPC/eCPA, fill rate, average bid, win rate), market (liquidity, participants per auction), behavioral (advertiser churn, bid frequency, elasticity, concentration index). Net income = auction revenue − refunds and fraud − taxes − bonuses − infrastructure − support and sales. Toxic revenue: revenue growing while liquidity, competition and trust degrade (active participants fall; dependence on 1–2 large buyers; resource quality for users falls; gaming the system); defense: market-health metrics alongside Revenue, floor/reserve prices without abuse, participant diversity. Rollout: demand validation → mechanism choice → MVP on limited inventory → calibration (floor, quality score, reserve) → scaling → toxicity monitoring.

**How:** 1) identify the value: what the client really pays for and who pays (user, master, advertiser); 2) choice questions: is value easily measurable (obvious → CPA/TRX; subjective → subscription); what risk will the client carry (low threshold → pay for result; high trust → fixed); competition for a resource → auction; product stage → simpler early; 3) pick metrics by lifecycle stage (value check: free/freemium, share of new in the base; demand check: share of payers among newcomers, average check, GMV; scale check: ROMI, UE, CAC by channel, LTV; regular business: margin optimization, churn, cost per deal); 4) choose the value metric: measurable, grows with delivered value, understandable to the client; 5) write the revenue formula with units and period, every input sourced and graded; 6) pricing: WTP range per segments; for a price change check the trade `(1 + ΔARPPU) × (1 − ΔPU) > 1`; 7) reconciliation points: Revenue vs Expenses (infrastructure, AI tokens, team) and client LTV vs CAC on the marketing-acquired cohort; 8) embed in traction-model layers 5–8: monetization (subscription, payer share, extra services, take rate, AOV), revenue forecast vs market, costs (fixed and driver-based), efficiency (UE, payback, NPV, IRR, EBITDA).

**Pitfalls:** choosing a model by fashion without checking that value is measurable; computing revenue ignoring the payer-base loss from higher prices; not reconciling LTV with CAC and Revenue with Expenses; mixing cohorts (marketing-acquired vs organic); confusing ARPU with ARPPU; taking a take rate "from the market" without a source; chasing auction revenue against market-health metrics.
