# Go-to-Market Launch Tiers (EN summary)

**When:** taking a feature or product to market; planning a quarter of launches; deciding how many resources a release deserves; moving launches from "a blog post after the fact" to a deliberate process.

**Core:** a tier is the size of the bet, not the genre of the announcement — it sets budget, timeline (T1 ≈ 8–12 weeks, T2 ≈ 4–6, T3 = changelog + one sentence to sales) and the DRI, decided before planning. Tiering criteria: revenue/goal impact (net new vs expansion vs retention), affected audience size, differentiation, depth of habit change; a scoring rubric (Intercom-style P1–P4) replaces volume with a table. Cadence: few T1s per year (1–4), dozens of T2s, a steady T3/changelog stream — launching everything at T2 fatigues the audience and dilutes real launches. Audience segmentation: existing users (in-app, segmented email), new market (PR, content, paid), sales & CS (enablement, talk tracks), internal audience (company, support). Channels follow the tier: T1 — press, landing page, campaign, enablement, event; T2 — blog, email to segment, in-app, social; T3 — changelog, docs, one sentence to sales. Launch metrics are fixed before launch: adoption (share of target audience using the feature at 30/90 days), conversion to the target action, pipeline/net-new contribution, retention of launched cohorts, plus counter-metrics (complaints, opt-outs, churn). Checklist = three phases: before (tier, goal, metrics, audiences, messages, assets, availability), during (go/no-go gate, launch-day runbook), after (30/90-day report, retro); a named owner runs the go/no-go gate 48 hours before launch.

**How:** score the release and assign the tier (PM + PMM; C-level sign-off for T1) → fix launch goal and 30/90-day metrics with a baseline before any work begins → map audiences × channels × activities per tier, with sales/CS enablement as a mandatory T1 stage → run the checklist (what/why-now messaging, top-3 objections, assets, availability model: all plans / beta / waitlist, support readiness) → launch with adoption and counter-metric monitoring, keep the go/no-go gate → 30/90-day report vs plan, retro feeding the roadmap and traction model.

**Pitfalls:** tiering "by feel" — every PM pulls toward T1 and volume wins; a T1 without prepared sales and support dies on the first customer call (enablement is not "after launch"); metrics assigned after launch make success indistinguishable from noise; everything at T2 — the audience turns off notifications and real T1s lose their weight.
