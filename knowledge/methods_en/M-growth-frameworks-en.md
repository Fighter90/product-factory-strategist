# Growth Frameworks (EN summary)

**When:** "how will the product grow?"; "which framework — funnel or loop?"; designing a growth loop / viral cycle; "which metrics to measure at each stage". The growth-model choice is a bespoke recipe; what matters is that the math closes.

**Core:** a linear funnel (AARRR, HEART) requires constantly refilling the top with external acquisition spend; users leak irreversibly at every stage. A loop (Flywheel, Growth Loop) is a closed cycle where the cycle's output (an acquired user, created content) becomes the cause of acquiring new users in the next cycle — growth "from inside". Recommended pairings by product type (guidance, not law): e-commerce → AARRR; SaaS → journey funnel + HEART; games → Growth Loop; social networks and media → Growth Loop + HEART. Choice factors: product stage, business goals, market, product type.

**AARRR (Pirate Metrics), six stages**: Awareness (SEO, SEM, content, affiliate, PR, socials, ASO; metrics: visits, reach, CTR, CPC, CPM, bounce, engagement) → Acquisition (blog, white papers, chat widgets, landing pages, webinars, retargeting, quizzes; subscriptions, downloads, Cost per Lead, trial signups) → Activation (first great experience, the Aha-moment; UX, tutorials, support, onboarding, drip; core feature usage, onboarding success, CAC) → Retention (email, pushes, extra features; churn, DAU, MAU, returning customers) → Revenue (UX, sales, checkout; CLV, conversion, upsell/cross-sell, average check, CLV/CAC) → Referral (referral mechanics, sharing, ratings, viral campaigns; invites, shares, viral coefficient, NPS). Weaknesses: constant top-of-funnel refill (expensive, limited scalability), irreversible leakage, no feedback loops. **HEART (Google)** — UX quality framework; per dimension: Goal → Signals → Metrics: Happiness (store ratings, in-app surveys, Customer Effort Score), Engagement (DAU, actions per user, saved items), Adoption (download rate, Time to Value, onboarding completion), Retention (30-/90-day retention, MAU, churn/uninstall), Task Success (task time, search success rate, error rate).

**Flywheel: Activate → Adopt → Adore → Advocate**: Activate (evaluating segment — remove barriers to the Aha; sign-up rate) → Adopt (novice — habit formation, feature education; retention rate) → Adore (regular — better experience, anticipating needs; NPS) → Advocate (champion — prompt recommendations; referrals invited — restarts the cycle). Example (jobs vertical of a classified): positive loops — more resumes → more responses → more employers → more vacancies → more job seekers; negative control loop — fewer job seekers → more fraudsters. **Growth Loop design**: universal cycle — product growth → more and better resources (people and capital) → bigger problems solved → product growth. Observe (real behavior scenario; motivations: to look good / feel good / help others / get / bond / convince); Design (what motivates and how to reflect it in the product; which action we expect and how to simplify it; what result the user gets and how to amplify it); Iterate (loop steps to track and a metric per step). Example: doctors share medical content → the product helps find supporting data → content with the author's photo and credentials is visible to others → they create their own; metrics: read/saved, shared, came via link, signups with UTM. **Growth Model Canvas**: target-audience persona · value proposition · North Star Metric · WOW moment · product DNA (customers need it and are "hooked") · the six AARRR stages with metrics.

**How:** 1) describe the product, stage, growth goal, market; define the NSM; 2) pick 1–2 candidate frameworks from the table and justify in writing why the others were rejected;

3) **check the math**: funnel — `Result = Input × CR1 × CR2 × …`, channel economics `LTV > CAC`, payback; loop — coefficient `K = i × c` (invitations/created content per user × conversion of invitees into new users); at `K < 1` with constant external inflow P, the steady state is `P / (1 − K)`; at `K ≥ 1` growth is self-sustaining, in practice capped by saturation; loop time and the quality of new users matter; 4) design the loop via Observe → Design → Iterate; write down trigger → action → result → next-cycle input; list leaks and negative loops; 5) assign a metric and measurement method (events, UTM) to every step; 6) fill the Growth Model Canvas and link it to the traction model: the loop is the virality driver, the funnel drives acquisition/activation/retention; 7) set control metrics per stage plus health counter-metrics.

**Pitfalls:** copying the "market leader's framework" without checking the math; treating HEART (UX quality) as an acquisition model or AARRR as a quality model; drawing a loop without measurable steps and conversions; ignoring negative loops (fraudsters, abusers, spam); optimizing Referral while Activation/Retention are weak; leaning on "virality" without computing K and loop time.
